# Frontend — layout, styling, MapLibre, fonts

Read before touching UI, layout, the design tokens, or any MapLibre pane.

## "Studio Papier" is the design system, expressed as Tailwind v4 tokens (2026-08-20)

**Decision.** `src/index.css` declares the palette and typography in an `@theme` block — warm paper surfaces (never pure white), ink text, **one** desaturated vermilion accent (`--color-accent: #d9442a`), a near-black `--color-frame` behind video, plus `Space Grotesk` (sans), `Instrument Serif` (serif) and `JetBrains Mono` (numerals), paper radii/shadows and an `--ease-paper` curve. Everything else is Tailwind utilities in the components. **Why**: one accent and one surface family is what makes the suite read as a single studio rather than nine tools. **How to apply**: use the tokens; do not introduce a second accent colour or a raw hex in a component. Numeric readouts use the mono face.

## The home-screen icon is a PNG set, and an SVG favicon is not one (2026-09-10)

**Reported**: the icon was right on the home screen for Winnow and wrong for Atelier, on Chrome for iOS. **Cause**: Atelier declared one thing, `<link rel="icon" type="image/svg+xml">`. iOS builds a home-screen icon from `apple-touch-icon` **only** — a PNG, square, opaque — and ignores `rel="icon"` whatever its type; with no such link it screenshots the page instead. Chrome for iOS is WebKit, so it reads exactly what Safari reads: the browser makes no difference, the missing tag does. Winnow shipped the full set from the start, which is the whole of the difference.

**The set, and it is the same in every repo of the portfolio** (`COMMON-PROJECT-SPEC.md` in `second-brain` carries it as the cross-repo contract): three SVG sources in `public/icons/` — `icon.svg` (rounded tile), `icon-apple.svg` (**full-bleed**, no corner radius of its own) and `icon-maskable.svg` (full-bleed, inverted, art inside the safe zone) — rasterised by `scripts/gen-icons.mjs` into seven **committed** PNGs: 192/512 any, 192/512 maskable, `apple-touch-icon.png` at 180, and 16/32 favicons. A static host cannot rasterise on request, so the output is tracked; the SVG alone is not what ships to a phone.

**Traps, each one measured here:**
- **Transparency is black on iOS.** A touch icon with rounded corners of its own comes back framed in a dark vignette under Apple's mask. Hence the full-bleed Apple source and the one `flatten()` in the generator — the other outputs keep their alpha, where it is wanted.
- **Vite rewrites `/…` hrefs in `index.html` with the base path** (verified in `dist/` at `BASE_PATH=/atelier/`, `rel="manifest"` included), so absolute paths there are correct and survive both the custom domain and a `/atelier/` preview.
- **It does not rewrite anything inside `public/manifest.webmanifest`** — that file is copied verbatim. Its `icons[].src`, `start_url` and `scope` are therefore **relative** (`icons/icon-192.png`, `./`), which resolves against the manifest's own URL and is right under either base. An absolute `/icons/…` there 404s on any base but `/`.
- The manifest is the **Android/Chrome half** and carries no weight on iOS. It is not what fixed this, so do not let a manifest question (its MIME type on Pages, say) hold up the touch icon.

**`display: "standalone"`, and the status bar is what bought it (2026-09-10).** It shipped as `browser` that morning, because Atelier's projects and trips are in this browser's IndexedDB and a home-screen launch can be given a storage container of its own — then the maintainer reported the status bar staying **system white** over the paper and asked for the colour, having weighed that cost and taken it. **The colour was never the missing part**: `theme-color` was declared in `index.html` and as the manifest's `theme_color`, survives the build (checked in `dist/`) and was deployed — a screenshot of his phone measures `#ffffff` in the bar and the paper gradient exactly right one pixel below, so the chrome was refusing a colour it had. A browser tab tints only if it feels like it (iOS has a per-device *Allow Website Tinting*); an installed app is told. So the lever is the launch, not the value: `display: standalone` plus **`apple-mobile-web-app-capable`** and **`apple-mobile-web-app-status-bar-style: default`**, which is the iOS half — iOS reads no manifest for this. `default` is the LIGHT style (dark glyphs over the theme colour); `black-translucent` would put white glyphs on cream and slide the page under the bar. `viewport-fit=cover` is deliberately NOT set: without it iOS keeps the web view inside the safe area and paints the inset itself, which is exactly the wanted result and leaves every existing layout alone.

**The cost is real and must be said out loud when it bites**: on iOS a home-screen app has its own storage, so a trip made in Safari is not in it. The ways across are an export/import (`.roadtrip.json`, `.atelier.json`) or keeping the document on a Winnow. Android shares one origin between the installed app and the browser, so nothing is lost there.

**How to apply**: change the mark in the three sources, then `npm i --no-save sharp` (it must never enter `package.json` — CI would pay for a native install on every job) and `node scripts/gen-icons.mjs`, and commit the PNGs in the same commit. The paper `theme-color` and `apple-mobile-web-app-title` sit beside the links in `index.html`; there is one theme (`color-scheme: light`), so one `theme-color`.

## A fixed edge takes no gutter (2026-09-10)

**Reported from a phone, once the document was locked and the masthead and the section bar became fixed by construction**: a stripe of paper between the masthead's border and the content, with the first card cut across. Measured at 390×844: header `0→48`, the tool's SCROLL CONTAINER `56→779`, the bar `787→844` — `<main>` was paying an 8px vertical gutter, so the scroller began 8px inside the chrome and clipped its content against a gap.

**The rule.** A gutter between a fixed edge and a scroll container is not spacing, it is paper the content gets clipped against: the content scrolls to the SCROLLER's edge, never to the screen's, so whatever sits between the two is permanently visible and permanently cut. Breathing room therefore belongs **inside** the scroller, where it scrolls away with the first row — `pt-3` on the two galleries' own sections, not on `<main>`. The sides keep their gutter (`px-2`): nothing is clipped horizontally, and a card touching the screen edge is a different fault.

**What is still owed at the bottom**: the bar pays `env(safe-area-inset-bottom)`, and since 2026-09-10 a compact tool screen always has one, so `<main>` owes nothing.

**The same rule read from the other end: a scroller owes air at its END (2026-09-11).** `<main>` paying a gutter is the stripe above; the scroller paying nothing is the complaint that followed — *"in the bottom of the pages we lack of padding"* — because the scroller's bottom edge IS the bar's top, so the last card in a column landed against it with nothing between. A `gap-4` between cards does not put anything after the last one. **`shared/ui/page-scroll.ts` is now the one class the three tool SCREENS scroll in** (the trip overview and the two galleries), `pb-4` included and unconditional: above a phone the frame's own `pb-3` sits OUTSIDE the box, so the last card was landing on the frame's edge there too. They had already written those five classes by hand and already drifted — two paid `pt-3` at compact and the third did not. The TOP deliberately stays out of it: `PageBar` clears the masthead itself, and a screen without one adds its own.

**Verified** at 390×844 with a list long enough to scroll: the scroller runs `48→787`, flush with both, and scrolled content reaches the masthead's border with no stripe.

## The shell wears THREE layouts, and names them once (2026-09-10)

**Decision, the maintainer's, taken off an interactive artifact showing all three widths side by side.** How much room the shell has is decided in ONE place — `shared/ui/layout-mode.ts`, DOM-free and tested — and published by the shell through `use-layout-mode.tsx` the way a tool publishes its media scope, inverted: `compact` (< 820px, a phone: the stage owns the screen, the tool's sections sit in a bottom bar, the panels are sheets over it), `medium` (820–1179: the library still docked at the left but collapsed to its rail, so a tool keeps the width its own layout needs), `expanded` (≥ 1180: the docked full library · stage · inspector, unchanged). **Why a published name rather than a utility**: the rule was spread by hand across dozens of literal `max-[820px]:` utilities in fourteen files, each re-deciding what small meant, and none of them able to say what the shell should DRAW at that width.

**820 keeps exactly its old meaning, so adoption is additive** — every utility already measured at 390, 744, 834 and 1280 stays correct, and only the upper boundary (1180) is new. `modeForWidth` is bottom-exclusive where `max-[820px]:` is inclusive, so the two disagree at exactly 820px and nowhere else; that pixel is the price of not re-tuning verified work, and is deliberate.

**This is NOT the query a tool's own layout splits on.** The container-query rule stands (`studio.md`, and «A stage that must fill the height» below): the Library rail eats 288px no viewport query can see, so a tool's internal columns stay on `@container`. A `LayoutMode` answers a different question — which chrome the SHELL wears, and therefore whether a tool draws its own tabs or hands its sections to the shell's bar. A tool reads the mode for that decision alone.

**What `compact` actually draws (2026-09-10).** The shell keeps its **fixed `h-dvh` at every width** — it used to give it up under 820px (`h-auto min-h-dvh`) so the page could scroll, and that is precisely what turned the suite into a stack (library card first, editor third, a 240px stage on an 844px screen) *and* what made every height above a stage indefinite. A definite height retires that bug class instead of patching it per tool, which is why three tools' `max-[820px]:overflow-visible` and the Studio stage's restated height all went with it: nothing hands the scroll back to the document any more, each tool scrolls its own list. The library is not a column on a phone at all — it rises as a `BottomSheet` summoned from the app bar, and `AssetSidebar` takes a `variant` (`docked | sheet`) that drops its frame, its title and its collapse control, because a sheet's collapsed state is being closed. Safe areas are paid at the two edges the shell owns (`pt-[env(safe-area-inset-top)]`, `pb-[max(…,env(safe-area-inset-bottom))]`). Home and `#/sources` are unchanged: they are reading pages and still scroll normally.

**The library stays DOCKED at the left everywhere but a phone; below 1180px it is the RAIL by default (2026-09-10, the maintainer's call).** A first pass put it out of the flow under 1180 as a slide-over drawer, and he asked for it back: *"on desktop and on tablet if we have enough room i would prefer to have the library sidebar at the left as usual"*. The constraint that forced the drawer is real and stays — the full column is 288px while a tool's own layout splits side-by-side at an **800px container**, so a full library leaves a 900px tablet 564px and the Studio stacks its inspector under a 240px stage — but the 48px rail leaves 804px, which clears it. So the answer is the narrow sidebar, not a hidden one: **visible at the left at every width above 820**, just collapsed where the full panel would cost the editor its shape. Widening it there is one click and stacking the editor is then a choice made in the moment.

**Measured across 820 · 900 · 1024 · 1100 · 1180 · 1280 · 1440** (the table is worth keeping): a full docked column only leaves ≥800px from **1180** up, a docked rail from **900** up. Hence both boundaries — 1180 is where the full panel returns as the default, and 820–899 (an 834px iPad in portrait included) stacks the editor whatever you do, which on a portrait screen is the right shape anyway. The `SideDrawer` component and `AssetSidebar`'s `drawer` variant were deleted rather than left unused.

**The collapse flag is remembered PER SIZE** (`atelier.library.collapsed`, and `…collapsed.medium` defaulting to collapsed): what you want beside a 1440px editor and beside a 900px one are two different preferences, and one flag made changing either silently change the other. The desktop key keeps its original name so an existing choice survives.

**A bar of VERBS is the other kind (2026-09-10).** `SectionBar.role` splits the two: `sections` are places in the document (an inspector's tabs, one of them current) and `actions` are things to START (a gallery's "new" and "import", none of them a state — `active: null`, and no cell is marked, because a tick on a verb claims a state the screen is not in). It decides only that mark; where the library is offered is settled below, and settled the same way for both. A tool publishing an actions bar also hides its own header buttons at `compact`, so no verb is offered twice on one screen.

**Both galleries publish the same `actions` bar, and the import question moved into a sheet (2026-09-10).** The Studio's gallery now matches Road Trip's: Library · New project · Import in the thumb zone, its own header buttons standing down at `compact` so no verb is offered twice. **`ImportDocumentModal` is engine-level** (`shared/ui/`) — the Studio became the second consumer, so the generic half moved out rather than being copied, the same move `StylePanel`, `GradePanel` and `SectionLegend` each made; `ImportTripModal` is gone and both callers pass their own noun, extension and sentence. What that fixes beyond the duplication: *where an imported document lands* was a `<select>` sitting permanently in the Studio's header for a gesture made twice a year, and on a phone that header has to share one line with the title. Asked in the sheet, at the moment it is decided — and **not asked at all with one document source**, where the file dialog opens straight away and no sheet is drawn.

**Two columns of cards on a phone, in BOTH galleries (2026-09-10, his ask, extended the same day).** An `auto-fill minmax(240–260px,1fr)` track gives exactly one column on a 374px content width, and a column of single cards wastes the half of the screen a cover does not need. At `compact` each grid is a plain `grid-cols-2`, and things have to step down with it or the card breaks — always the same three, plus whatever competes for the title's row:

- **Trips**: the cover zone (168 → 112px, in ALL three shapes it can take — a picture, the rhythm band, the "kept elsewhere" note — through one `coverHeight()` so they cannot drift), the body padding, and the date line's size. **A truncated date range says nothing**, the span being the whole point of the line, so it shrinks rather than losing its second half.
- **Projects**: the body padding, the title and the facts line. Its 16:9 preview is `aspect-video` and needs nothing. The **aspect chip moves off the title's row** into the facts, which wrap anyway: at 181px it was taking a third of that row and a project's NAME — the one thing you pick a card by — read as "Sydney har…".

**Neither gallery draws its own name any more.** The masthead already says `Atelier / Trips` and `Atelier / Studio`, and on a phone a serif heading plus a sentence of prose was the top fifth of the screen spent introducing the cards it then had no room to show. Both keep `<h1 className="sr-only">` for the outline and a screen reader — only the ink is given up — and both drop the whole header ROW at `compact`, since its two verbs are in the shell's bar and an empty row above the cards is worse than none.

**`eslint-plugin-react-hooks` is NOT installed, and a rules-of-hooks violation is therefore invisible to CI** (2026-09-10). Found by writing one: a `useIsCompact()` placed after `CoverArt`'s three conditional returns, which would have changed hook ORDER the moment a trip gained its first cover picture. **Measured: the codebase has zero violations**, so adopting the plugin is a two-line config change and no fixes — offered to the maintainer, not taken, because it adds a devDependency. Until then, read the hook placement yourself in any component with an early return.

**The library DOCKS on a phone; it does not cover (2026-09-10, the maintainer's design).** *"the library drawer when open would take half of the screen and the media zone preview would also take half of the screen so we can see both and see the result"*. `shared/ui/DockedPanel.tsx` is the suite's fourth cover, and what earns it is the VERB: a `BottomSheet` is for a panel you ADJUST (a form, an inspector — covering the stage while you fill it in costs nothing), a dock is for one you CHOOSE from, where the whole question is what the choice looks like on the thing behind it. A library over the stage makes picking a guess followed by a check; a library beside it makes picking a comparison. So: no scrim, no overlay, an ordinary flex child, and the stage genuinely shrinks. **Two rests** — `half` (0.46) to choose at, `strip` (0.13, one row of candidates) to compose at — one grip apart, same panel, so nothing is re-fetched or re-scrolled crossing between them; a drag below the strip closes it. The arithmetic is `sheet-snap.ts` unchanged: a dock and a sheet disagree about everything except the maths. **At the strip the panel draws no header** — 110px has room for one row of candidates OR a title bar, and the row is the reason it is open.

**A picker's row is a TILE, and it inverts which verb gets the big target.** In the dock the asset is a thumbnail with its name burnt in at the foot: no filename column (a name is not what tells two frames of one moment apart), no checkbox (the pool's tick decides nothing in a tool that reads the ACTIVE asset — the "6 selected" footer went with it), and tapping the picture SETS it while `⤢` in the corner looks at it large. The two-verbs rule holds; what changes is which verb owns the tile, on a surface whose whole purpose is choosing. At 58px in the strip there is no corner verb at all — it would cover a fifth of the tile. The drop zone folds to one line of links there too: dropping a folder is a desktop gesture and it was spending a third of the panel on something touch cannot do.

**What a docked panel demands of every stage above it (2026-09-10).** A stage that STATES its height ignores the dock and is simply clipped by the bar below — measured: Road Trip's badge stage kept its `h-[min(62vh,…)]` and lost its bottom third. On a compact shell the stage FLEXES instead, and `flex-1` is only worth anything if the **whole chain** flexes: the editor's stacked column must not be `overflow-auto` (a scroll container hands its child an indefinite height again — the trap this file already names), the stage column needs `flex-1 min-h-0`, and so does `BadgeStage`'s own wrapper. Miss one and the canvas measures 0. Verified at 390×844: the stage reads 430px with the dock at its strip and 151px with it at half, and picking a tile leaves the dock open. Everywhere else stacked — a tablet, where the inspector is still in that column — the column scrolls and the stage keeps stating its height, so the old rule survives exactly where it is still true.

**And what the dock takes, the CHROME gives up — never the picture (2026-09-10).** Flexing was necessary and not sufficient: the maintainer reported the preview *"very very small"* with the library at half, and the measurement said why. Of the 351px the piece editor's stage column has at 390×844, 105px went to the page bar, the piece's name and its date line, and 91px to the transport and the slide rail — so the canvas got 155px, and a 9:16 reel being height-bound, **86px of the 374px available across**. Half the screen each, and the picture was getting a fifth of its half.

So the shell publishes a third fact downward, `shared/ui/stage-room.tsx`: `StageRoom` is `full` or `shared`, the twin of `LayoutMode` and the mirror of `media-scope.tsx`. A tool reading `shared` sheds everything that is not the thing being looked at, and gets it all back when the dock closes. In the piece editor: the name field and the date line hide, and the transport hides. The slide rail was the third move — a row under the picture turning into a COLUMN beside it while docked — and since 2026-09-12 it is a column at every width and in both rests, so the dock no longer changes it (`roadtrip.md`). **Measured after**: canvas 290×162 at the half rest (3.5× the area) and 553×310 at the strip, where the picture is now as big as with the library shut. Nothing changes above 820px, and nothing changes with the dock closed.

Two traps it walked into. The rail turns itself at `@min-[860px]`, a CONTAINER query that is right while the question is "is there room for two columns" and wrong when the question is "which axis is scarce" — so `column` is a prop that wins outright rather than another query. And a hook read behind a `&&` (`compact && useStageRoom() === 'shared'`) is a hook that stops being called the moment the window is widened: read it unconditionally, then combine.

**A tool's SECTIONS go in the thumb zone, and so does the library — on every screen (2026-09-10, revised the same day).** The Studio's five tabs and Road Trip's four are published to the shell (`shared/ui/section-rail.tsx`, the twin of `media-scope.tsx`) and drawn as a bottom bar on a phone; at any wider width the same `TABS` array is the inspector's own strip, so one declaration serves two placements and they cannot drift. Picking a section is also what raises the panel, because the inspector is a `PanelHost` — one component, two placements, children never rebuilt by a rotation — and it opens CLOSED: the stage is what you came for.

**The library is the bar's FIRST cell everywhere**, and the app-bar burger is gone. It was first refused on a `sections` bar (the bar is the tool's, the library the shell's; a sixth cell squeezes five sections) and drawn only on a gallery's `actions` bar, which left the burger on every other screen. The maintainer reported it from the trip overview: *"on trips we have menu burger for library, I prefer we have the same bottom menu that we have on other pages, also on the editor"* — a phone has ONE bottom menu and the way to the pictures is in it, or the same tool is reached two different ways depending on which of its screens you are on. **So the shell draws the bar on every compact tool screen, even for a tool that publishes no sections at all**: an empty bar still carries the one thing every tool screen needs, and the reading tools stop having no way to their media. The squeeze argument was arithmetic and the arithmetic was checked rather than argued — six cells measured in the live CSS at 360px (Galaxy S portrait, narrower than the 390px iPhone) give 52px each against ~43px for "Overlay", **once** the library cell is content-sized (`shrink-0 px-2`) instead of stretching and the section cells drop to `px-0.5`. Widen any of that and the longest label truncates. Alone the library cell does stretch: a lone chip in the corner reads as something left over, a full-width row reads as the menu it is.

**A cell that is a toggle wears the toggle's mark, and a tap on the stage must REACH the sheet (2026-09-12, three reports from a phone).** Three faults of the same shape — a gesture whose result is off screen. (1) The library cell showed nothing while its dock was open, because the library is not a section and so is never the bar's `active`; it is a TOGGLE, so it takes the same accent wash under `aria-pressed`, and tapping it again closes the dock — the ✕ in the panel header is no longer the only way out. (2) Tapping an element on a stage switched the inspector's tab but left the sheet shut, so the tap read as doing nothing. The fix is not to open on selection: selection happens on pointer DOWN because a drag starts from it, and a sheet rising mid-drag covers the thing being moved. Both stages (`use-overlay-stage.ts`, `BadgeStage.tsx`) now also publish `onActivate` — fired on RELEASE when the press travelled ≤ 4px, the suite's usual tap threshold — and only that raises the sheet, only on `compact`. A Road Trip element that belongs to the closing card raises the trip sheet instead and must not raise a second one over it. (3) `DockedPanel` gained `mt-2`: sharing the height with the stage left the picture's bottom edge and the panel's own rule on the same line, which reads as one surface cut in half rather than two things on screen. It is paid out of the stage like everything else the dock takes, and it is not the gutter this file warns about — the panel is a flex sibling BELOW the scroller, not paper inside it.

**Verified in headless Chromium at 390×844, 900×700 and 1280×800**, driving the real dev server: the document scrolls neither axis on any of the nine tools, the shell measures exactly `innerHeight`, the sheet opens at 55%, drags to 92%, dismisses on a downward flick and cycles on a tap, and Escape closes it. The bar was checked end to end by adding a real JPEG through the file chooser and creating a project: five cells, each at least 44px tall, flush to the bottom, and tapping one opens the sheet titled with its name. Rebuild that probe rather than eyeballing this — `playwright-core` installs with `--no-save` against `/opt/pw-browsers/chromium`, and it must never enter `package.json`.

**The literals that REMAIN are meant to (2026-09-10).** The plan promised a sweep of "every hardcoded breakpoint onto the three modes"; on inspection most of them were already right and only sixteen were dead. Of the forty left, thirty-six are in **modals and overlays** (`WinnowBrowser`, `TripSettingsModal`, `TimelineImportPanel`, `MediaLightbox`) where a viewport query is the CORRECT tool and is documented as such below — a `position: fixed` sheet spans the viewport, so `max-[820px]:` measures exactly what it gets, and rewriting pure styling as a JS branch would make those files worse, not drier. The other four are `min-h` floors on the Overlay, LUT, Compare and Map stages; measured at 800×400, the shortest compact viewport worth caring about, none of them binds, so they clip nothing. **What was actually removed** was the collapsed rail's sixteen narrow-screen classes in `AssetSidebar`: the rail is only ever drawn when the library is docked, which now only happens above 1180px, so its "turn into a horizontal bar under 820" state became unreachable. Dead responsive classes are worse than none — they read as a supported layout.

**How to apply**: `useLayoutMode()`, `useAtLeast('medium')` or `useIsCompact()`, never a new literal breakpoint in a component — except in a `position: fixed` overlay, which is the one place a raw viewport query still belongs. Both `matchMedia` lists feed one `modeForMatches` on every change, because a drag across 1180 fires one list and a drag across 820 fires both — reading the pair each time is what makes either order land correctly. No provider means `expanded` (a harness assumes the least); no `matchMedia` at all means `compact` (the layout that fits anywhere, so a wrong first guess costs a re-render and never a broken screen).

## The Library's day is a stepper over a density strip, never a date field (2026-09-08, rev. 2026-09-09)

**Decision.** The Winnow tab picks its day with a **stepper** — `‹ Tue 8 Sep 2026 ›` in the mono face, a reading line under it (`● today · nothing here`, `● 24 days ago · 19 files`) — and the value opens the month as a **density strip**: one bar per day, height normalised to that month's own peak (`shared/sources/winnow/day-density.ts`, pure and tested), a month header whose arrows stop at the instance's `bounds`. **Why**: the verb in that rail is *browse the days around a shoot*, so stepping is the common move; and the question a picker has to answer at 288px is *which days have footage on them*, which a grid of empty squares cannot. `/api/assets/calendar` answers a span with a count per day **plus a library-wide `bounds`** — confirmed in Winnow's own route, which computes bounds ignoring the window — so the shape is read, never estimated. A month/year select was considered and refused: it lands you in a month you would then walk blind.

**Two readings, one click apart.** The strip and a mini **calendar** (fixed-height cells, never `aspect-square`; counts drawn in the cell) are both bodies of the same panel, toggled by a glyph at the end of the month header and remembered in `localStorage` (`atelier.library.month-view`). The maintainer wanted both and was right to: the strip says *where the shooting was*, the calendar says *which weekday it fell on*, and neither answers the other's question. Arrow keys walk both through one helper — 1 column for the strip, 7 for the calendar — and the step is counted over **every** day, disabled ones included: with the rest of the month out of reach, a seventh *enabled* button is not the same weekday a week later, so a week step over a filtered list silently lands on the wrong day.

**What that costs, and the rules it buys.** The invisible `<input type="date">` that used to lend the OS picker is **gone** — a native calendar cannot show counts. So this owns the popover contract instead: Escape and an outside `pointerdown` close it and focus returns to the value (the `ToolSwitcher` pattern, no library), the bars rove `tabIndex` so Tab does not visit thirty-one buttons while ←/→/Home/End walk them, and each bar is full strip height so a 6px-wide day is still a comfortable target — the arrows stay the fine adjustment. The 16px iOS rule no longer applies here: there is no field left to focus. A **picked day carries a full-height wash** as well as its bar, or "you are here" is invisible on a day holding nothing (a 3px stub). The next-day arrow disables on today; nothing was shot in the future.

**One month, one request**, cached by month key for as long as the popover lives and thrown away with it — counts move as the instance ingests, and a picker is open for seconds. Nothing is asked while it is closed.

**A state is stated once.** The reading line carries what `WinnowScopeGrid` used to say in a sentence ("asking…", "holds nothing shot on…"), so the grid takes an `announce` prop and goes quiet on those two cases while the picker is on screen; it still draws a real problem, in red. Any second surface for the same fact needs the same treatment — two sentences saying one thing read as a bug, not as emphasis.

**Verified** by mounting `DayPicker` against a stubbed `client.calendar` in a throwaway Vite entry (a harness page must sit at the project ROOT, not in `public/`: an HTML file Vite does not process gets no react-refresh preamble and the app dies on load). Rebuild that harness rather than trying to see this without a connected instance.

## Waiting never looks like an answer (2026-09-09)

**Reported, and it cost the maintainer real time**: walking the day picker's months, an unanswered month drew as thirty empty bars — indistinguishable from a month holding nothing — so he moved past months that turned out to be full, and learnt to sit and wait rather than read the control. The tile grid had the same shape: `rows === null` drew nothing at all.

**How to apply.** Any pane that waits on an instance draws a **busy state that cannot be mistaken for a result**: the day picker's month body keeps its days but at a uniform height, dimmed, pulsing and inert (`aria-busy`, `pointer-events-none`, `motion-reduce:animate-none`); `WinnowScopeGrid` draws eight skeleton tiles in its own grid; the reading line's dot pulses while its span is out. A *sentence* ("asking…") is not enough on its own — it is small, and the empty-looking content beside it is what the eye reads. Note the skeleton is drawn even where `announce` is false: the prop silences a duplicated **sentence**, never the feedback itself.

## A control row wraps; the shell clips — nothing scrolls the page sideways (2026-08-22)

**Trap, measured.** The studio's transport was one flex row of `flex-none` chips (play, clock, trim rail, duration, loop, shutter, preview speed, A/B). Each feature added one more, and on a 390 px phone the last one landed at x=437: the whole **document** gained a horizontal scrollbar and the interface drifted into the margin. Nothing was clipped, so nothing looked broken — it just scrolled sideways.

**The shape to keep.** The row is **two groups** inside a `flex-wrap` container: the *rail* (play, timecode, trim, duration) with `grow shrink basis-[15rem] min-w-0`, and the *tools*, itself `flex-wrap max-w-full`. Flex breaks lines before it shrinks, so the tools drop to a line of their own instead of pushing the last chip off the edge — and if even they cannot share one line, they stack. **How to apply**: a new transport control goes in the tools group and costs nothing; a new *row* of controls anywhere follows the same two-group shape.

**The net, and where it actually works.** The tool shell (`src/app/App.tsx`) clips on desktop but switched to `overflow-visible` under 820 px, which is what let the overflow reach the document. It is now `overflow-x-clip` + `overflow-y-visible` there: sideways it clips, vertically the page still scrolls normally (verified: a 2 000 px child no longer moves `window.scrollX`, a 2 000 px-tall one still scrolls). `clip`, not `hidden` — it establishes no scroll container, so sticky positioning inside the page keeps working, and `position: fixed` modals still cover the viewport (verified at 390 and 1400). **Do not** put this on `html`: Chromium computes it there but does not propagate it to the viewport, so the page scrolls anyway — measured, and the reason the rule sits on the shell.

## MapLibre's own CSS can collapse its container (2026-08-20)

**Trap.** MapLibre adds `.maplibregl-map` to its container, and that stylesheet — loaded *after* Tailwind — sets `position: relative`, overriding an `absolute inset-0` container and dropping it to `height: 0`. The symptom is a black map with the overlays still visible. **Remedy**: give the map container a real height as a flex child (`flex-1 min-h-0`), never rely on absolute positioning, and call `map.resize()` once the style loads as insurance against a 0-size measurement during the async (dynamic-import) mount.

## MapLibre is dynamically imported, JS *and* CSS (2026-08-20)

**Decision.** `use-flight-map.ts` and `use-composer-map.ts` import MapLibre lazily. **Why**: it is a heavy dependency and only two tools use it; static import would put it in the main bundle for everyone. **How to apply**: keep the import inside the hook, and keep the "map failed to load" state — the caller shows a fallback instead of a broken pane. The live-position marker uses inline styles so it never depends on the CSS scanner picking up dynamically-built class names.

## Canvas text needs fonts explicitly loaded (2026-08-20)

**Trap.** `fillText`/`measureText` only render and measure correctly with faces the document has actually loaded; the brand fonts arrive asynchronously, so an overlay drawn too early measures against a fallback and exports mis-sized text. **Remedy**: `overlay/fonts.ts` calls `document.fonts.load(...)` for each used face and awaits `document.fonts.ready` before the first measured draw and before export. System fonts (Arial/Georgia/Courier) need no loading. Any new canvas text path must go through the same helper.

## The file picker must listen to `cancel`, not guess (2026-08-20)

**Trap.** The old "did the user cancel?" heuristic (focus + timeout) raced the `change` event: when the page was busy — a video playing in LUT Studio — the timeout fired first and a real selection was silently discarded. **Remedy**: `shared/sources/file-sources.ts` uses the native `cancel` event (Chrome 113+, Firefox, Safari 16.4+), so a successful pick always resolves through `change` no matter how busy the page is. ## Keyboard shortcuts live in the shared hook, and yield to the focused element (2026-08-21)

**Decision.** Space plays/pauses, and the binding sits in `shared/media/use-video-transport.ts` (opt out with `spaceToggles: false`), so Studio, Grade, Compare, Composer and the legacy Overlay all get it from the one transport instead of five copies. It listens on `window`, not on the stage: in most tools the element that actually plays is a 1px off-screen `<video>` feeding a canvas, so there is nothing meaningful to focus. **Why the guard matters**: space is also the browser's activation key for buttons and a character in every field, so `transport-keys.ts` (pure, tested) decides whether the focused element has the stronger claim — fields, contenteditable, `BUTTON`/`SUMMARY`/`VIDEO`/`AUDIO`, and the ARIA widget roles — and when it does the handler returns *without* `preventDefault`, or the play button would stop working the moment it has focus. **How to apply**: a new global key goes through the same predicate, and any new one must also skip `e.repeat` (a held key would toggle forever) and modified presses. Tools whose `<video controls>` is the visible player (Telemetry, Flight Map) deliberately keep the browser's native space handling — Telemetry shows several cards at once, so a global key would have no obvious target.

**Trap.** The listener is mounted once (deps `[spaceToggles]`) and calls `togglePlay` through a ref refreshed on every render. Re-wiring it on `resetKey` like the media listeners looked equivalent and is not: Compare's `togglePlay` closes over `aIsVideo`/`bIsVideo` state, and a stale closure would toggle the wrong pair after a swap.

## Escape and Enter are one hook, and Enter yields to the focused element (2026-09-08)

**Decision.** Every modal binds its two keys through `shared/ui/use-dialog-keys.ts` (over the pure `dialog-keys.ts`, tested), never a hand-written `window` listener: Escape dismisses the sheet, **Enter runs its primary action** — Save, Create trip, Apply, Add to library, and in `TripSettingsModal`, whose fields autosave on every keystroke, closing it. **Why**: nine modals each carried the same Escape effect and none acted on Enter, so filling a form ended with a reach for the mouse — typing a date and pressing Enter did nothing. A per-field `onKeyDown` was the old answer and is now wrong: it fired only in the one input that had it, and left beside the hook it submits **twice**. Remove it when you adopt the hook.

**How to apply.** Pass `onConfirm: null` — never a no-op — whenever the CTA would be disabled (`canSubmit`, `picked.length`, a ticked diff) or a question is already on screen waiting for its own answer (`ProjectSettingsModal`'s "Replace the settings / Keep mine"); read the very condition the button carries, so the two can never disagree. `dialog-keys.ts` decides who owns the press, the twin of `transport-keys.ts` for space: a textarea, a button, a link, an `<input type=file|checkbox|…>`, a contenteditable and the ARIA widget roles keep Enter, while a text, date or `<select>` field yields it — which is exactly the browser's own form behaviour. A field that handles Enter itself calls `preventDefault` (`PlaceSearchField` looks a place up), and the hook stands down on `defaultPrevented`; a nested control that must keep Enter therefore has to say so on the event, not hope.

**Verified** in the dev server: Enter from the New-trip date field created the trip, Enter with an empty name (disabled CTA) did nothing, Enter in the From field opened the place-search consent instead of submitting, and Escape still closed the sheet.

## Library thumbnails always letterbox in a fixed box; the kind chip is a third text line (2026-08-22, rev. same day)

**Decision.** `AssetSidebar.tsx`'s `AssetRow` thumbnail is a **fixed 80×56 box for every row, unconditionally** — never resized per asset. The `<img>` is always `object-contain` (never `object-cover`), so a landscape/square clip fills the box width-first same as before, and a portrait clip pillarboxes with black bars left/right inside that same box, exactly mirroring how the tools' own video players letterbox a mismatched aspect ratio. **Why fixed, not variable-width** (rejected 2026-08-22 same day): an earlier version shrank the box width for portrait media so it would visually "stand up" — technically fine, but it made every row's thumbnail a different size, so titles no longer started at the same x and the list read as disordered. The kind chip (`video+srt`, `video`, `photo`, …) is back to its original full-word pill (`kindLabel()`/`chipClass()`), but as a **third line in the text column** (`flex flex-col`: title, facts, chip — `self-start` so the pill doesn't stretch) instead of beside the name or on the thumbnail. **Why not on the thumbnail** (also rejected 2026-08-22): the frame already carries up to two scrims (cadence top-left, fps bottom-left) and a third corner badge reads as cramped/overlapping, especially once the thumbnail was still variable-width. **Trap, measured in this environment's headless Chromium (Chromium 141, software rendering, `--headless=new`).** The thumbnail wrapper was `grid place-items-center`; changing the `<img>` to `object-fit: contain` with `width:100%; height:100%` silently stopped letterboxing — the image filled the box edge-to-edge regardless of its real aspect ratio, confirmed with a plain PNG (not just an SVG data URI, ruling out a data-URI/intrinsic-size issue) and reproduced with `viewBox` present. The identical percentage-sized `<img>`/`object-contain` combo inside a **flex** parent (`display:flex; align-items:center; justify-content:center`) or a plain block parent rendered correctly every time. **Remedy**: the wrapper is `flex items-center justify-center`, not `grid place-items-center` — swapped here and worth defaulting to for any future `object-fit` content on a centred replaced element, since flex reproduced correctly across every variant tried and grid did not, in this environment, regardless of whether `place-items` was set. Not confirmed whether this is a genuine cross-browser Blink bug or an artifact of this sandbox's software rasterizer — treat it as "flex is the proven-safe choice for object-fit centering here," not as a general indictment of CSS Grid.

**Verified** by screenshotting the compiled Tailwind output (`dist/assets/*.css`) against a static harness of landscape/portrait/square/photo rows with real (generated) PNGs in headless Chromium — this is what caught the grid/object-fit trap above; an SVG-only harness had masked it behind a different, coincidental failure mode.

## Tools own full height; only the inner list scrolls (2026-08-20)

## A modal is a full-screen sheet under 820px, and shows ONE pane at a time (2026-09-02)

**Trap, seen on an iPad.** `WinnowBrowser` is a fixed-height card (`h-[min(90vh,52rem)]`, `overflow-hidden`) holding a two-column grid that switched to `grid-cols-1` under 820px. Stacking put two panes in a height that never grew: the pictures pane landed *on top of* the calendar, clipped, and its "Choose a day on the left." sat across the grid. **The shape to keep**: below 820px the modal is the whole screen (`max-w-none h-dvh rounded-none border-0`, overlay `p-0`, `pb-[max(1rem,env(safe-area-inset-bottom))]` so the actions clear the home indicator) and only one pane is rendered — the picker until something is chosen, then the results with a back control (`hidden max-[820px]:inline-flex`). A drill-down, not a stack; the header, the filters and the action row stay pinned around it, and only the middle scrolls.

**A viewport query IS right for a modal** — the container-query rule (`studio.md`, `roadtrip.md`) exists because the Library sidebar eats 288px a tool cannot see; a `position: fixed` overlay spans the viewport, so `max-[820px]:` measures exactly what the sheet gets. Use `dvh`, never `vh`: on iOS Safari `vh` is the *large* viewport, so a `max-h-[90vh]` card can hang behind the toolbar with its Apply button under it.

**`hidden` loses to a base class carrying `inline-flex`.** Tailwind orders `display` utilities in its own layer, not by the order written in `className`, so `` `${pill} hidden max-[820px]:inline-flex` `` rendered the control at every width (measured at 834px). A conditionally-hidden button spells its own classes out rather than borrowing a shared pill.

**Touch sizing, applied at the same breakpoint.** Controls go up a size and any `<select>` or `<input>` reaches **16px** — below that, iOS zooms the page on focus and never zooms back. That is also why two native date fields no longer share a 390px row (`NewTripModal`), and why the filter selects wrap two-per-line on a phone (`basis-[10rem]`) instead of one being squeezed against the tabs. Calendar cells drop `aspect-square` when the calendar owns the full width (100px-tall squares on an iPad) for a `min-h-[3.5rem]` band.

**Long action rows follow the two-group shape** documented above for the transport: `[choice + hint]` with `grow shrink basis-[20rem]`, then `[cancel + go]` with `ml-auto`, so the actions never get stranded alone on a line while the hint keeps the one above. Any modal deep enough to scroll pins that row (`sticky bottom-0 -mx-6 px-6 pb-6 bg-surface`, container `px-6 pt-6`) — `ProjectSettingsModal` already did; `NewProjectModal` and `NewTripModal` now do too.

**Verified** in headless Chromium at 390×844, 744×1133, 834×1112 and 1280×900 against a throwaway harness that mounted the modal with `window.fetch` stubbed for the Winnow endpoints (no instance needed, thumbnails deliberately failing); `scrollWidth - clientWidth` was 0 at every width. Rebuild that harness rather than eyeballing this file — it is the only way to see the modal without a connected Winnow.

## A `1fr` grid track cannot be trusted to carry `aspect-ratio` (rev. 2026-09-07)

**Struck twice.** `WinnowBrowser`'s calendar cells already dropped `aspect-square` below 820px (`max-[820px]:aspect-auto max-[820px]:min-h-[3.5rem]`, entry above) because a `minmax(…,1fr)` track's width can turn indefinite once the grid's own container is inside a narrow or stacked context, and a browser resolves `aspect-ratio` against that indefinite size however it likes — observed as the tile collapsing to a thin band instead of a square. `DayFromWinnow`'s media tiles (added later, in the Road Trip piece editor's 22rem side panel — narrow on every width that matters) used the same `aspect-square` and hit the same collapse, because the lesson lived only in the file that had already paid for it. **How to apply**: a thumbnail tile in an `auto-fill`/`minmax` grid takes a **fixed pixel height** (`h-[74px]`, `h-[90px]` — whatever the design calls for), never `aspect-square`/`aspect-[…]`, matching `WinnowBrowser`'s own `Thumb` component (`h-[90px] max-[820px]:h-[120px] object-cover`). `aspect-ratio` on a grid-track-sized box is only safe once the grid's own tracks are a definite, non-`1fr` size — which a tile grid meant to fill available width never is.

## `max-h-full` measures nothing in a `flex-1 min-h-0` chain (2026-09-07)

**The bug**: `WinnowLightbox`'s picture came out cropped and its clip drew square. The media sat in a `grid place-items-center h-full overflow-hidden` box whose parent was a `flex-1 min-h-0` row, and carried `max-w-full max-h-full`. A percentage height needs a **definite** height on the containing block; a flex item sized by `flex-1` inside `min-h-0` has none until layout is done, so `h-full` fell back to content height and `max-h-100%` capped nothing — the picture drew at its natural size and the `overflow-hidden` cut it.

**How to apply**: to fill a flex line's cross size, stretch the box (`items-stretch` on the row + `self-stretch` on the item, arrows `self-center`), never `h-full`. Then let the box be the frame and CONTAIN the media in it — `absolute inset-0 w-full h-full object-contain` — rather than asking the media to measure itself against an ancestor. `object-contain` is also what gives a `<video>` its aspect ratio: an unfitted `<video>` takes its own default box, which is where the 1:1 clip came from. Twin of the `aspect-ratio` entry above: both are a box asking an indefinite ancestor for a size.

## A tool screen is an APP: the DOCUMENT is locked, not just the frame (2026-09-10)

**Reported on a phone**: the masthead slid off the top, the section bar lifted off the bottom and paper showed under the frame — *"the layout header and footer must be fixed, we must have a mobile app feel, no scroll outside of the ui"*. The frame was already `h-dvh` and clipping, and in a fixed-size browser that IS the end of it: measured at 390×844, `documentElement.scrollHeight === clientHeight` on every tool screen, before any change. **The overflow is not in the app's boxes, it is the document's**: on iOS the initial containing block is the LARGE viewport (toolbars retracted) while `dvh` is the current one, so `<html>` stays about a toolbar taller than the frame inside it, and that difference is what a finger drags. No amount of `overflow-hidden` on a child can reach it.

**Decision**: the shell tells the document which of its two lives it is in — `App.tsx` sets `data-shell="fixed" | "flow"` on `<html>` from whether a tool is mounted — and `index.css` locks the fixed one: `height` (see the entry below — `--app-h`, and it was `100dvh` until the unit proved unreliable), `overflow: hidden` and `overscroll-behavior: none` on **the root element and body both**. The root is the load-bearing one (overflow on `body` alone propagates back to the viewport); the explicit height is what trims iOS's larger ICB; `overscroll-behavior` is the rubber band and pull-to-refresh, because a page that cannot scroll must not bounce either or it still reads as a web page. Home and `#/sources` get `flow` and keep the ordinary page scroll they are written for. **How to apply**: a new full-height screen belongs to a tool route, or it must set its own lock — and never reach for `position: fixed` on the header and the bar, which is the fix this made unnecessary.

**`viewport-fit=cover` is set, and it is what makes `env(safe-area-inset-*)` mean anything** — without it every inset is 0 and the safe-area padding the shell already pays (`pt-[env(…)]` on the compact frame, `pb-[max(0.5rem,env(…))]` on the section rail) is dead code. Two edges nobody owned are paid now: `body` takes the left/right insets, since a landscape notch belongs to no component in particular, and Home's top padding is `max(its clamp, env(safe-area-inset-top))`. **No `maximum-scale`, no `user-scalable=no`** — pinch-zoom stays, and a form that must not zoom the page under it is still answered by 16px controls.

**Verified in headless Chromium** at 390×844 and 1280×900: the document is unscrollable on all three tool screens and scrollable on Home and `#/sources`, the flag follows an in-app hash navigation both ways, a wheel over the trip overview moves its own list by 310px with `documentElement.scrollTop` at 0 and the masthead still at y=0, and both bottom sheets still reach the bottom edge exactly. **What cannot be checked here is the thing that caused it** — a real iOS toolbar — so the iPhone is where this is confirmed.

## And `dvh` itself can be stale, so the app MEASURES its height (2026-09-12)

**Reported from Chrome on iOS, and only after a reload**: a band of paper under the bottom bar. Read off the screenshot at 3× on a 430pt phone, which is what identified it: the section bar is exactly its 57px, it ends **59.3 CSS px above the browser's toolbar**, and that band is uniform across the whole width — no chrome icons in it, so it is the page's own paper and the app's box is a retracted toolbar SHORTER than the screen it was given.

**The fault is the unit, not a box.** The browser keeps its toolbars retracted across a refresh, the page is laid out against the shorter viewport they were expanded for, and `100dvh` — "the current viewport", the right idea — is never recomputed, precisely because the entry above locks the document: a scroll is what would ask the question again, and there is none. The bug is therefore permanent for that page load and invisible to every other browser.

**So the height is read.** `shared/ui/app-height.ts` (pure, tested) reconciles the two viewports a browser exposes; `use-app-height.ts` publishes the answer as `--app-h` on `<html>`, and every box meaning *the whole screen* is sized in it instead of `100dvh` — the document lock, the tool frame, `DockedPanel`/`BottomSheet`'s fractions, and the four `max-[820px]:` full-screen sheets. `100dvh` stays as the variable's own fallback: the first paint, and anything mounted outside the shell.

The rule is **the larger of `window.innerHeight` and `visualViewport.height`, and neither while the page is pinched**, and each half earns its place. The visual viewport is the only reading that grows when the chrome retracts without the layout being told — the bug itself. The layout viewport is the only one the software keyboard does not move, and resizing the app to the strip above a keyboard would reflow the editor under the fingers typing in it. A zoom (in *or* out) freezes the measurement rather than shrinking the layout around the gesture. It is re-read on `resize`, `orientationchange`, `pageshow`, `visibilitychange`, the visual viewport's own `resize`, and `load` — the moments a phone hands back a different screen, several of which are not resizes.

**A drag reads the same number** (`readAppHeight()`): both sheets turned a finger's travel into a fraction of `window.innerHeight` while their CSS sized them in `dvh` — the same disagreement one level down, and it goes away by construction now.

**Verified in headless Chromium** at 390×844 driving the dev server: `--app-h` equals `innerHeight`, root, frame and the bar's bottom all land on it, the document overflows by 0, and Home still scrolls 3080px as `flow`. The failure is reproducible by hand and worth reusing — force `--app-h: 744px` and the bar sits 100px above the bottom, exactly the screenshot; one resize event puts it back flush. **What no headless browser can produce is a real iOS toolbar.** If a gap ever survives this, the remaining possibility is that BOTH readings are stale at load and only a later moment would catch it — a timer, or the first touch.

Measured at 390×844: with the dot alone the piece editor's header is one line; the four action states take a second, which is the trade being made deliberately. The full sentence is announced either way — it sits in an `sr-only` `role="status"` live region, because a pill is a summary and a live region has to carry the whole state.

**A popover hanging off a pill mid-row needs a CLAMP, not a choice of two corners.** Anchoring it left or right by which half of the screen the pill sits in put its left edge at −87px on a 390px screen: the 20rem panel is wider than the room either side of a pill in the middle of a row. Measure the trigger at open time and slide the panel back inside (`Math.min(Math.max(rect.left, EDGE), innerWidth - width - EDGE) - rect.left`, applied as `style.left` on the wrapper). Two more things the hover half needs: the gap above the panel is **padding on the wrapper**, never a margin on the panel, or the pointer crosses a strip over neither element and the panel drops on the way to its own buttons; and the trigger carries **no `title`**, or the browser's tooltip covers the panel repeating its sentence.

**A pill that can grow needs a radius, not `rounded-full`** — the lesson that paid for the old auto-height pill, and still binding on anything that wraps. `rounded-full` resolves to half the *grown* height and draws a fat lozenge, so a box that may wrap takes half the ONE-line height instead (`rounded-[0.95rem]` against a `1.3rem` line), or the app's own `rounded-paper`.

**A `rounded-full` container that can wrap its contents to two rows is the same shape mistake one level up**: the piece editor's six-tab bar (`p-1 rounded-full border … bg-surface`) wraps to two rows in its 22rem column, and a pill stretched over two rows of buttons reads as a mis-shapen blob, not a toolbar. The individual tab buttons stay `rounded-full`; the wrapping *container* takes the app's own soft-rectangle radius (`rounded-paper`, 14px) instead — a `rounded-full` wrapper is only correct when its content is guaranteed to stay one row.

## A stage that must fill the height: measure the box, never trust a replaced element (2026-09-06)

**Measured on Road Trip's piece editor** (maintainer ask: "the preview must take the whole page height"). Three things each silently capped the picture short of the column. (1) **A canvas never displays past its bitmap**: `max-h-full` on a 720px-tall canvas stops at 720 CSS px however tall the box is — a replaced element's intrinsic size is its ceiling. (2) **CSS `aspect-ratio` cannot transfer a max-constraint back onto a definite axis**: `h-full` + `max-w-full` + `aspect-ratio` clamps the width but leaves the height, so a width-limited frame distorts or letterboxes. (3) **A container query never matches the `@container` element itself**, only an ancestor — `@min-[860px]:grid` on the section that IS the container silently never applied, and the layout stayed stacked at every width. **How to apply**: put the queried layout on a CHILD of the container; size the picture's box in JS from the wrapper's rect (largest aspect-fit, `ResizeObserver`), so the element box stays exactly the picture (hit-testing maps through it); and let the bitmap follow the displayed size × `devicePixelRatio` between `PREVIEW_LONG_EDGE` and `MAX_PREVIEW_LONG_EDGE`, or a bigger preview is a blurry upscale. In the stacked layout the wrapper's height must come from CSS (`min(62vh, 100cqw / aspect)` — `cqw` is the editor's container) because the box inside no longer has content height of its own; and a `min-h-0` on the stage column must stay wide-only, or flex-shrink squeezes it to nothing inside the fixed-height shell when the editor is stacked at a mid width.

## A media preview never view-zooms; a day-sized zone does (2026-09-08, rev. 2026-09-10)

**Decision, and it reverses the first half of this entry.** A view zoom over a MEDIA PREVIEW is out, in both editor stages, on the maintainer's call: the pill was wanted for Road Trip's day heatmap and its stage ruler, and over a preview it fought the gestures the picture itself answers — a trackpad pinch had to be taken away from the badge stage's own framing zoom to feed it. **A preview fits the room it is given, and that is all**: the Studio centres its canvas under `max-w-full max-h-full` (a canvas is a replaced element, so that is the whole arithmetic), Road Trip measures the largest aspect-fitting box in its wrapper. Do not put a `StageZoomControl` over a picture again; looking closer at ONE picture is the lightbox's job (entry below).

**The primitive stays, for the zones it was really for**: `shared/ui/stage-zoom.ts` + `use-stage-zoom.ts` + `StageZoomControl.tsx`, consumed by the trip overview's day grid and stage ruler (`roadtrip.md`), with the pill also driving the lightbox. The scale is applied as **layout size** — a zone multiplies its own unit — never a CSS `transform`: a transformed element still measures unscaled, so the scroll box would not know it had anything to scroll. Panning is then native scrolling, and every pointer handler keeps working untouched, because the zones map pointers through `getBoundingClientRect()`.

**Range: 25% to 1600%**, a quarter of the fit up to sixteen times it, in ×1.25 steps that land exactly on 100% when they cross it. The zone's own floor is what really stops a zoom-out (the ruler will not draw a day under 6px, the grid a cell under 4).

**Gestures.** ⌘/ctrl-wheel (which is what a trackpad pinch sends) and a two-finger touch pinch are the view zoom. What a BARE wheel does is per zone (`WheelZoom`, `wheelZooms`): both trip-overview zones pass `wheel: 'any'`, because nothing else there wants the wheel — but a shift-wheel and a sideways trackpad swipe (the dominant axis decides) stay the browser's horizontal scroll, which is how a zoomed track is panned. `modifier` is what a zone with its own use for the wheel would pass, and no zone does today: that was the editor stages, and the arrangement is exactly what failed — the badge stage had to hand ⌘/ctrl over to the view zoom, so a trackpad pinch stopped framing the picture. **A pinch means the same thing as the wheel on the same surface, or one of the two is wrong.** A pinch also makes a zone stand down while it runs (`pinching`), or the first finger drags a leg across the ruler under the gesture.

**Zoom-at-the-pointer has FOUR traps, every one of them found the same way: read the fraction of the content under a fixed point, zoom, read it again (2026-09-08).** Do that before believing any of this works — three of the four shipped green.

1. **A wheel spins out several events before React renders once.** The correction is measured from the scale the box is still LAID OUT at — the first of the batch — not from the one the last event started from; keeping the later `prev` walked the day from 65% of the ruler to 24% in three notches. So `pending.prev` is only written when nothing is pending.
2. **The scroll it is measured from must be the one the box had at gesture time**, kept in `pending` beside `prev`. Reading `el.scrollLeft` back in the layout effect is right while the content GROWS and lies when it shrinks: the browser has already clamped the scroll into the smaller content, so a zoom-out that overshot the floor projected from a position never asked for and jumped to the start of the track.
3. **The multiplier is how much the content actually grew, not the ratio of the two scales** (`growthRatio`, `scrollAfterZoom`'s `grew`). A zone with a pixel floor or whole-pixel rounding is not linear in the scale — the ruler's frozen 6px track is the extreme: it did not move at all while every notch pulled the scroll by a quarter.
4. **Content that does not scale must be subtracted first**: the day grid's weekday rail keeps its width at every zoom, so `scrollAfterZoom` takes a `fixed` prefix and anchors past it, or the day slides by the rail's width times the zoom.

**And below the fit point no correction exists at all**: while the content is narrower than its box the browser pins the scroll to 0, so nothing can hold a point under the pointer. Hence `zoomFloor` + `minScaleToFill`: a zone declares its content width at a scale (`contentWidth`) and the zoom stops going out where that stops filling the box — capped at 1, so 100% and the reset button are always reachable. Where a zone still fits at 100% (a short trip's grid on a wide screen) it is CENTRED (`w-fit min-w-full flex justify-center`) so it grows about the middle rather than rightward from the left edge; the buttons anchor on the box's centre, so they hold exactly there. The regression guard is `src/shared/roadtrip/zoom-anchor.test.ts`: a simulated scroll box, clamped the way a browser clamps one, swept both directions over each zone's real width function.

**Three tolls a scroll box charges, all three invisible until something is zoomed past the viewport.** (1) **A scroll container's intrinsic width is zero** — it advertises no content width, so any ancestor sized by its content collapses. (2) **The content is a flex item and will be squeezed**: a 949px box inside a 486px viewport shrinks back to 486 unless it is `shrink-0`. (3) **`min-w-full` alone leaves the left half unreachable**: the centring wrapper's width is still 100% of the viewport, so a centred overflow spills both ways and the scroll extent only counts the right. It needs `w-fit h-fit min-w-full min-h-full` — fit-content around the picture, never smaller than the viewport, which centres while it fits and starts at the corner once it does not. All three were learned on the editor stages and paid there; they are the price of the scroll box, which is why a preview that no longer zooms is no longer one.

**A measured box must never be sized by the picture it holds (2026-09-09, rev. 2026-09-10).** Anything that reads a box and then fits a picture into it — `useStageZoom` did, `BadgeStage` still does — is measuring its own output the moment that box takes its size from its content, and every gesture or repaint feeds the next. Measured in Chromium at 390×844 when the shell still went `h-auto` on a phone: one pinch out collapsed the canvas to 1×1 and the measured viewport oscillated 201 → 4 → 1 → 201 px, while zooming in grew the stage 240 → 1920 px tall and pushed the inspector 1 700 px down the page. **The rule survives the zoom's removal**: Road Trip's piece column keeps its `w-full` (`PostEditor`) so the badge's own measured box cannot decide it, and a stage whose ancestors are NOT definite states its own height from the media's aspect and the screen (`h-[min(62vh,calc(100cqw/var(--aspect)))]`). The Studio needs neither — the compact shell keeps `h-dvh` at every width, so its stage is plain `flex-1 min-h-0`; do not re-add a height restatement there. No unit test can see any of this: check it in a browser at 390, 900 and 1280, reading the box twice a second apart, and a correct one does not move.

## A drag surface inside a scroll box claims only the axis it writes (2026-09-09)

**Rule, learned on Road Trip's stage ruler and binding anywhere a draggable thing sits in something that scrolls.** `touch-none` is the reflex for a pointer-drag surface — it is what stops the browser stealing the gesture — and it is a trap the moment that surface lives in a scroll box, because a finger has no other way in. The ruler's scrub surface covered the whole track and set `touch-none`, so a track wider than the phone by construction could not be scrolled AT ALL on touch: every swipe meant to read the rest of the trip was read as a scrub instead. The split that works, verified with `Input.dispatchTouchEvent` in headless Chromium:

- **A surface that is only pointed at claims nothing.** Picking a value is a `click` — a tap on touch, one gesture, no capture — and the browser is left free to pan. A press-and-travel gesture for choosing a value is what forces the `touch-none`, so the two decisions are the same decision.
- **A surface that is dragged claims its own axis and no more**: `touch-pan-y` on a thing dragged sideways. The browser then leaves horizontal movement to the pointer handlers while a finger travelling UP it still scrolls the page — `touch-none` had made the whole band a place the page could not be scrolled through.
- **An ORDINARY scroll box declares no `touch-action` at all** (2026-09-10). This whole rule is about a surface that WRITES a drag; a plain `overflow-x-auto` strip — Road Trip's counts on a phone — is not one, and the browser already does both axes on it. Adding `touch-pan-x` there buys no horizontal scrolling and costs the vertical swipe that scrolls the page under it. Reach for `touch-action` only when pointer handlers are taking a gesture away from the browser.
- **A flex child that scrolls also becomes crushable** (2026-09-10). An `overflow` other than `visible` sets the item's AUTOMATIC MINIMUM SIZE to zero, on the cross axis too — so the moment that counts strip could scroll sideways, the column above squeezed it from 47px to 18px with the numbers sheared off at the waist. `flex-none` (or an explicit basis) is part of making a flex item scroll, not tidiness. Same family as the `min-h-0` rules above, running the other way: there the automatic minimum stops a child shrinking; here `overflow` removes it and nothing does.
- **The scroll box gets `overscroll-x-contain`**, or a track sitting on its first day hands the next swipe to the browser, which reads it as *Back*.

**What it costs, and it is the right trade.** `useStageZoom`'s two-finger pinch and native panning are mutually exclusive: once the browser owns a gesture it cancels the pointers, so on a zone that lets it pan the pinch is best-effort and `StageZoomControl`'s +/− is the way in that always answers — which is now the case on both zones that zoom. No CI gate can see any of this — `touch-action` is a string in a class list and the failure is a gesture that does nothing — so check it with real touch events, not with a mouse.

## Looking at ONE picture is the other zoom, and it is a transform (2026-09-08, rev. 2026-09-09)

**Decision.** ONE sheet shows a media large, wherever it lives: `shared/ui/MediaLightbox.tsx` over `shared/ui/pan-zoom.ts` + `use-media-viewer.ts` — a second primitive beside the zones', deliberately not the same one, and since 2026-09-10 the ONLY way to look closer at a picture. It knows nothing of sources; a caller hands it `LightboxItem`s (title, facts, kind, `src`, a `still` for the neighbour slots, the natural size, an `unavailable` reason, and whether the URLs need the cookie) plus the footer, which is the only thing the two callers do differently: `WinnowLightbox` brings the picture across, the Library sidebar puts it to work in the tool. A pool asset reaches it through object URLs the sidebar pins for a ±2 window around the open one (`shared/lib/use-object-urls.ts`) — `createObjectURL` reads nothing but pins the handle, so a library of thousands must not hold thousands. A RAW that arrived without its JPEG twin says so instead of handing the browser bytes it cannot decode. A zooming zone scales its LAYOUT so the browser's scroll box does the panning; a viewer's deck has to follow a finger *past its own edges*, which no scroll box does. So everything here is a `transform` and every limit is arithmetic we own (`panLimit`, `clampView`, `zoomAbout`, `rubberBand`, `swipeCommit` — all DOM-free, tested in `pan-zoom.test.ts`). Range is **fit to 8×**, in ×1.5 steps: fit is the floor because a viewer showing less than the whole picture shows nothing. `StageZoomControl` drives both, over a `ZoomControls` interface `StageZoom` now extends, with a `hint` prop for the gesture the surface really offers.

**The pager floats IN the frame, not beside it (2026-09-09).** Previous/next were a column each side of the deck, so the black frame stopped two buttons and a gap short of the sheet's own padding and sat out of line with the title above and the footer below. They are now `absolute` inside the frame, wearing `StageZoomControl`'s translucent-paper pill (it reads over a white sky as well as over the frame's black) — the media box then runs edge to edge with the header and footer content, which is what the maintainer asked for, and covering a sliver of the picture is the cheaper price. **Two rules for that placement**: they are siblings of the viewport element and never children of it — that element answers every pointer event itself, in the capture phase, so a button inside it would be a drag; and a single media draws **no** pager at all, a dead control over a picture being noise the header's `1 / 1` already covers. Under 820px there is still none: the swipe is the gesture there.

**What each gesture means, and why the two surfaces disagree.** ⌘/ctrl-wheel and a two-finger pinch zoom, as everywhere. But a **bare vertical wheel zooms here too** — the stages leave it to the page, and a viewer has nothing else to do with it while a mouse has no other way to zoom. A **bare horizontal wheel** (a two-finger trackpad swipe) pages the deck, and pans instead once the picture is zoomed and has somewhere to go. A **drag pans when zoomed and swipes when not**: at the fitted size there is nothing to pan, so the same gesture is free to mean the other thing, decided once after 6px of slop. Two fingers pinch AND pan by their centre, which is why the viewport is `touch-none`.

**The exposure line is a second fact line, and both sources fill it** through one pure formatter (`shared/exif/exif-summary.ts`): body · lens · focal length · ƒ · shutter · ISO, each part absent when the picture does not say it — the line never writes `—` and never guesses. A local file is read for it when the sheet opens (`readEffectiveExif`, the head only; a CLIP is not read at all, since an MP4 has no EXIF and only what its source vouched for is worth having). An instance fills it from its own columns: Winnow's `/api/assets` returns `a.*`, so `lens` was already on the wire and only missing from `WinnowAssetRow`. The camera's name and the lens stay OUT of `exifFromRow` — they are display labels, not cue values — and are added where they are read.

**The three things that are not obvious:**

- **A wheel gesture has no end event.** A trackpad swipe is a stream of `deltaX`; the release is 140ms of quiet, and only then is `swipeCommit` asked. Without that the deck can only ever page on distance.
- **The deck pages on a timer, never on `transitionend`.** The offset is sometimes already at its target, and that fires nothing — the deck would hang mid-slide. One `DECK_SETTLE_MS` timer slides the deck a whole slot, and only THEN moves the index and drops the offset in the same commit: the neighbour is already where the middle slot is, so the swap shows no seam. A `busy` ref refuses every gesture while it is in flight.
- **Key the slots by row id, not by slot** — React then carries the neighbour's already-loaded picture into the middle and the page shows no reload. With exactly two media both neighbours ARE the same row, so the key has to fall back to the slot; with one, there is a single slot.

**Pan limits are measured off the PICTURE (`containedSize`), never off the box**, or a letterboxed panorama pans into its own bars. Winnow's row and the library's `MediaMeta` both already carry `width`/`height`, so the limits are right before a byte arrives; the media reports its own through `onMeasured` otherwise.

**Looking at a picture and putting it to work are two verbs, so they are two buttons.** A library row's 80×56 cover opens the sheet (a `⤢` on hover, nothing at rest) and its text column stays the activate target — a button inside a button is not markup, and the cover was inside one. Same rule as the Winnow tab's tiles, where a click already previewed rather than fetched.

**A clip keeps the bottom 56px to itself** — a heuristic strip for the native control bar, since the browser does not expose it — and above that a clip swipes like a picture. Everywhere, a tap still reaches the controls because nothing is `preventDefault`ed before the drag slop. **Not built, and cheap to add later**: a swipe that continues out of a zoomed picture once it hits an edge (iOS does it; it needs a per-axis "how far past the edge did this push" state machine).

**Nothing waits, and nothing is ever greyed out.** Two answers to "why does it feel stuck while it loads", neither of which blocks a hand: (1) a slot draws the cheap **still under the full rendition**, so a page never opens on black and simply sharpens — and the full one is mounted in EVERY slot, not just the middle, because rendering the neighbours is what preloads them (three requests instead of one, which is the point; a clip is the exception, only the middle slot mounts a `<video>`); (2) a 2px indeterminate bar (`--animate-deck-load`) along the top of the frame while the middle slot is not yet sharp, with `aria-busy` on the frame. And a gesture arriving DURING a page no longer waits for it: it lands that page at once (`land`) and starts from there, which was the only place the deck genuinely refused a swipe. A clip reports ready on `loadedmetadata`, never `loadeddata` — with `preload="metadata"` a browser may hold the first frame back until play, and a bar that never stops is worse than no bar.

**A media opens on the instance in its own tab** — the secondary action beside "Add to library". It points at the PROXY's own URL, because Winnow has no page per asset (checked in its repo: `/library`, `/sift`, `/search`, no `/assets/[id]`, and its viewer takes no deep link) and its `/download` route sends `Content-Disposition: attachment`, which saves a file rather than showing one.

**Testing gestures in a throttled browser will lie to you.** A background tab clamps `setTimeout` to ~1s, so synthetic `pointermove`s land a second apart and every velocity reads ~0.02 px/ms: the distance path pages, the flick path never does, and nothing is wrong. Check the event `timeStamp` deltas before believing a velocity bug.

## 2026-09-12 — A verb about a named thing rides that thing's strip

The Library's Sources cog used to be a control of its own in the panel header. In the phone sheet the header already gives its name away to the sheet, so the cog was alone on a line — a whole row of a picker spent on one glyph. **Decision**: with an instance connected the cog is the trailing cell of the source tab strip, beside the tab that already names that instance; the header button is drawn only when nothing is connected, where it is the way IN and wears the word "Sources". **How to apply**: a control that acts on something already named on screen belongs next to the name, not on a line that repeats it. ## 2026-09-12 — Standing prose folds behind `InfoDot`; a readout stays

Road Trip and the Studio explain themselves, and the explanations had piled up: the Studio bridge alone carried three paragraphs saying the same thing twice, the Stages panel five lines of what a leg is, two modals a subtitle each. **Decision**: the ⓘ moved out of `SectionLegend` into `shared/ui/InfoDot.tsx` so any surface can fold a paragraph away — a modal subtitle, a sidebar footer, a panel with no legend at all — and the prose goes behind it rather than on screen. **How to apply**, the test that sorts them: a sentence that is TRUE EVERY TIME is standing prose and folds (what a stage is, that the folder is remembered, that handles are all the Library holds). A sentence that answers about the thing in hand is a READOUT and stays inline — "this slide animates, so it goes out as a 3.5s video", "no stage covers this day", which grade a Studio export will use. Folding a readout is what makes a tool read as broken, and the anti-fabrication rule (`roadtrip.md`) is the same rule seen from the other end.

**Hiding prose below a breakpoint was rejected**: a phone is where a first-timer learns, and a sentence present at one width and absent at another is two products. The ⓘ costs 16px at every width. The exception stays what it already was — a gesture a phone does not HAVE (`max-[600px]:hidden` on the right-click hints), which is not the same thing as an explanation a phone does not deserve.

`SectionLegend`'s row is now `flex-wrap` with the note `basis-full`: an open note takes the line under the label instead of squeezing it as a second flex item.

**A gesture hint retires itself once the gesture has been used** (`shared/ui/learned-gestures.ts` + `use-learned-gesture.ts`, a namespaced `localStorage` flag per gesture, never in a document — it is a property of the hand, not of the trip). The stage ruler's four-gesture line and the trim bar's "drag the handles, or press I / O" are drawn until the surface has actually been worked, and live behind the section's ⓘ forever after. **How to apply**: call `learn()` from the handler the gesture itself lands in, never from a mount or a hover, and keep the sentence reachable in an `InfoDot` — a hint that vanishes with nowhere to read it again is a feature removed. Any use of the surface counts (on the ruler a leg opened, a day tapped, a leg dragged, a gap filled): the hint only ever claimed the surface exists.
