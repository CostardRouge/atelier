# Road Trip — telling a journey, not editing a clip

Read before touching `src/tools/roadtrip/` or `src/shared/roadtrip/`, and before adding anything about publishing cadence, day badges or trip persistence.

## What the tool is for (2026-08-23)

**Decision.** The Studio answers "make this clip look right"; Road Trip answers "which day have I never told, and what goes out next". The maintainer came back from Australia in February 2026 and is telling that trip through 2026 from ~2 500 usable photos and about three and a half months of drone footage. The scarce resource is not editing time, it is **knowing what is left**. So the tool is a planning surface over a calendar, not a second editor. **How to apply**: a feature that helps produce a piece belongs in the Studio; a feature that helps decide *which* piece belongs here.

## The sharing strategy this encodes (2026-08-23)

**Decisions taken with the maintainer, so they are not re-litigated:**

- **Publishing out of chronological order is fine, and is the plan.** Reels are pushed by interest, not by profile order, and the profile grid is no longer the front door it was. Coherence comes from the **badge being visually constant**, not from posts arriving in sequence — which is what lets Australia, Brittany and current life alternate freely in one feed.
- **Re-telling the same day with different media is not penalised.** Nothing on the platform knows a day was already shown; only a near-identical *piece* posted twice close together is a real risk. Hence: vary the angle and format between two posts of one day, and space them out. This is why the grid counts and colours by intensity rather than flipping a day to "done".
- **Cadence: three to five pieces a week**, not daily — 2 500 candidates over a year does not need daily output, and daily output is what burns the run out before the trip is finished.
- **The "one year ago today" calendar is the editorial spine**, because it supplies a ready-made schedule; the grid exists to catch the days that spine skips.

## The model: trip → days → posts (2026-08-23)

**Decision.** A trip holds STAGES (places, each with their own span) and POSTS. There is deliberately **no stored day record**: a day is a calendar date inside the trip's span, derived on read (`tripCoverage`). Storing 310 empty rows to answer "which days have nothing" would be a second source of truth for something two subtractions already know, and it would have to be migrated every time a trip's dates are edited.

**A post is keyed by the DATE it tells, never by a file name.** The maintainer re-exports through Capture One and the Studio, so names, sizes and mtimes all change under him; the day a photo was shot does not. This was his explicit instruction and it is the load-bearing choice of the whole model. A post DOES carry a media reference (`TripPost.media`, a `SavedMediaRef`), but only as a hint for re-finding the file in the library: losing it costs the picture, never the post or its place in the trip, and the missing case is stated plainly rather than treated as an error. **How to apply**: any new tracking field hangs off the day or the post's date, never off a filename.

**A multi-day post occupies every day it spans** (`postDays`, clamped to the trip). "Days 27–29" tells three days, and the grid must show three days told — the grid's question is "has this day been told", not "how many posts start here".

**Stage overlap resolves to the LAST match** (`stageAt`): stages are kept in the order the trip was lived, so on a travel day the later stage is where you ended up, which is what a badge should name.

## A trip is renamed on its heading (2026-09-07)

**Decision.** The name is edited in place on the Overview's `<h1>` (click the title, Enter or blur commits, Escape cancels, an emptied field gives the old name back — the "empty means computed" rule the text overrides follow). **Not in `TripSettingsModal`**, even though the name is as trip-wide as the words: that sheet is reached from a PIECE, so a trip with no piece yet could never be renamed from it. A rename is an ordinary `onChange` with a fresh `updatedAt`, so it debounce-saves and flushes to a remote trip like any other edit.

Nothing else moves with it: the trip's `id` is a uuid, and `tripFromRef` matches on the id fragment alone, so the slug in `#/roadtrip/<slug>-<id8>` is decoration that goes stale until the next navigation and every link ever made still resolves. Badge text derived from the trip's name (the headline fallback, the sub-year kicker) re-derives on the next paint — a rename is meant to reach the pieces.

## The dates and the route are re-edited in the sheet that asked for them (2026-09-07)

**Decision, from the maintainer** (*"i need the ability to change the trip date and initial places, maybe use the same modal to avoid recreating the same ui"*). `NewTripModal` is `TripDetailsModal`: given a `trip` it opens on that trip's span and route instead of creating one, reached by clicking the **line under the heading** on the Overview (the destination · dates line), beside the click-to-rename `<h1>`. Editing hides three things and nothing else moves: the **name** (renamed in place on that heading — two ways to rename one thing on one screen is clutter), the **Keep on** picker (moving a trip is the gallery's verb and the pill is what says where it stands) and the **seed from a timeline** row.

**The arithmetic is pure and lives in `shared/roadtrip/trip-edit.ts`**, not in the component, because a span that moves has consequences. The rules, all tested:

- The span is the ruler's own frame, so **legs follow it**: one that overhangs is trimmed, one the new span does not reach at all is dropped (`retimeStages`). **Posts are NEVER touched** — a post's date is the key of the whole model and the work is the author's; one left outside simply stops being drawn (`postDays` already clamps).
- `spanImpact` counts what would happen and the sheet **says it before saving**, in one accent line — a trip told over a year must not lose a leg silently. Nothing is said when the span only grows, which is the normal case.
- The route is `tripRouteEnds` (moved into `trip-places.ts` beside `tripRouteLabel`, which now derives from it), so editing an end **writes back into the leg that holds it**: no third copy of where the trip went. Emptying a field removes that place and the leg keeps its dates and derives its label; naming an end the trip had none of adds one; with no leg at all the pair seeds one over the whole span, exactly as `createTripDoc` does. The trap: a trip naming ONE place is its own start and end (`from.id === to.id`), so the "To" field opens EMPTY and an added end is minted with a fresh id — writing `to` onto that place would rename the start, and reusing its id would list it twice.
- `destination` — the prose subtitle composed from the two ends at creation — follows the route **only when the route actually changed**, so fixing a year keeps a line the author (or a timeline import) wrote.
- The open day is in the route, so a shrunk span moves it to the trip's new first day rather than leaving `DayPanel` on a day the calendar no longer draws.

Verified in headless Chromium: the sheet opens on the trip's own values, the impact line reads "1 leg will be trimmed to fit", saving trims the leg and renames the chip, and 0px document overflow at 390px with the sheet open.

## The whole trip card opens the trip (2026-09-07)

**Decision, from the maintainer** (*"ability to click on the trip card to open it (pointer on hover, no need to translate up)"*). `TripCard`'s body is the click target — a card showing a name, its dates and how much of it is told is the thing you point at — with `cursor-pointer` and **no `hover:-translate-y-1`**: the lift was decoration on something that was not clickable, and it is the one card in the suite that now is. The **Open / Resume / Open here button stays**: it is the keyboard and screen-reader path and the only thing that says which of the three it is. Everything else in the card keeps its own click through one guard on the card's handler — `(e.target).closest('button, select, input, label, a')` — rather than `stopPropagation` sprinkled over the export, delete and move controls; a busy card (a move or a delete in flight) does not open at all. Verified: Delete opens its confirm without opening the trip, a click on the card's body opens it.

## The trip card wears a cover, taken from the BUSIEST days (2026-09-08)

**Decision, designed with the maintainer and validated by him.** A trip shows itself with pictures — its own hook thumbnails, which every post already keeps in the `thumbs` store and which are already pruned on delete, so the cover costs no new asset and no new upload. `TripDoc.cover` (v13, portable) is a `layout` and a list of `pinned` post ids; `shared/roadtrip/trip-cover.ts` resolves it and is pure.

**The automatic tier is the trip's busiest days, not its latest pieces** — his idea, and the better one. Recency churns: opening a piece would change the cover, and a test crop would become the trip. Busiest is stable and is a real editorial signal (a day told three times is a day there was something to say about). Recency is not lost, it is **subsumed**: on a trip where every day holds one piece everything ties at one, the date tie-break takes over, and the cover is the latest days again — so nothing branches on that case. The ranking, all tested: pieces on the day ↓, published ↓, date ↓ (later wins); one piece per day (its published one, else its first with a picture), never twice from one day — a mosaic of one moment is not a trip.

**A pin is a preference, never a dependency**, which is what dissolves the whole question of what happens when a piece is deleted, a picture is re-exported or the span is re-edited. The chain — pinned → busiest days → rhythm → none — resolves on **every render**, so there is no dangling reference to repair, no migration when the dates move, and no state where the card cannot draw. It is the rule the tool already binds for badge text and a piece's grade: *empty means computed, never blank*. Rejected with reasons, so they are not re-proposed: pinning a **day** (a day is derived, so outside a shrunk span it points at nothing — the one case with no rung to fall to, and a day holding three pieces still needs the piece-level tie-break anyway), and an **own cover image** (it ages away from the trip's grade and style, and thumbnails never travel, so it would die on every import and on every other device).

**A layout is what the card asks for, not what it promises.** `mosaic` (3) falls to `cover` (1) falls to `rhythm` (0) on what actually exists — a mosaic of one *is* a cover, and no layout can fail to render. `rhythm` draws the trip's own days from `tripCoverage`, so it is right on a trip created ten seconds ago and on a freshly imported one whose pictures have not been re-baked; `none` is chosen, never fallen back to.

**The chooser is ONE panel (`CoverPanel`) with TWO homes, and never `TripSettingsModal`.** The maintainer's call: a cover is a property of the trip like its name and its dates, so it is settled in `TripDetailsModal` — the sheet that already holds them, reached from the overview's heading line — and it is *also* reachable from the gallery card (`TripCoverModal` over the same panel), because the gallery is where a cover is looked at. Neither home can be the only one: the details sheet needs the trip open, and a `none` layout leaves the card with no cover zone to click. `TripSettingsModal` stays out of it — that sheet is reached from a PIECE, so a trip with no piece could never be settled from it (the same reason the rename and the dates live on the heading). One panel, two hosts, exactly as `StylePanel` and `GradePanel` went shared on their second consumer. It loads every thumbnail of the trip, not the handful the card needs, and revokes them on unmount: seeing the pieces IS the panel. Creation does not offer it — a trip with no piece has nothing to show, and a form must not ask what cannot be answered.

**A layout that draws nothing must never be a one-way door.** Picking `none` removes the cover zone the chip lives on, and the maintainer read that as a trap. Every coverless card therefore carries the same verb in its actions row, and the details sheet holds the panel regardless. Rule for any control anchored to what it edits: check what happens when the edit removes the anchor. The **open** trip's cover is handed back to `RoadTripTool.handleChange` (`onChangeOpenTrip`) rather than written to the store — the tool's save machine holds that document while the gallery shows, and would flush over anything written behind it. A cover on a remote trip takes the same road a creation does: written **there** first, mirrored here after.

**A hook thumbnail is sized for its LARGEST consumer, not its first one (2026-09-09).** `THUMB_LONG_EDGE` was 224 because the day panel was the only reader, and it draws the picture 38×48 CSS. The gallery card's cover then wanted ~378×336 device px on its mosaic tile and ~600×336 full-bleed, so the same file was being upscaled three times over and read as soft — the maintainer spotted it. It is **640** now. What makes that free: every display size in the suite is CSS and none is derived from the picture, so raising it changes nothing but sharpness and bytes; and `canvasThumbnail` never upscales, while the badge stage paints at `PREVIEW_LONG_EDGE` (720) or more, so the size asked for is always actually reached. Rejected, with the reason, so it is not re-proposed: a **second, larger picture kept only for the cover**. Which pieces the cover draws is not knowable at bake time (the busiest days move as the trip is told, and pins change), so the big one would have to be baked for every piece anyway — two stores and more bytes, not fewer; and baking it lazily cannot work, since the canvas it comes from exists only while that piece is open in the editor. A stored thumbnail keeps its old size until its piece is next opened: it is a cache, and there is no migration for the same reason.

**A cache is only allowed to be written from the real thing.** `captureThumb` fires on any hook paint, so opening a piece before its file is in the Library used to store the black placeholder over the good thumbnail and empty the trip's card — measured, not theorised. The guard is the `missing` the editor already computes, and the distinction it draws is the whole point: a piece that names NO picture is a badge over nothing and is worth storing as it is, while a piece that names one the Library cannot resolve *right now* is drawing a placeholder and must be refused. Checked again when the debounce fires, since the picture can go between the paint that scheduled the write and the write itself. Applies to any derived artefact kept beside a document: write it from the real source or not at all — a stale cache costs a repaint, a wrong one costs the work.

**A picker has to be findable, and an empty preview has to say so (2026-09-08).** Both learned from the maintainer, who twice looked for the layout chooser and concluded it had not been built. It HAD — it was one item in the card's overflow menu, and on his trips (no piece composed yet, so no thumbnail anywhere) the Mosaic and Cover previews drew flat `paper-2` fills, so the row of four read as nothing at all. Two rules from it: the cover carries **its own verb** (a `Cover` chip on the cover zone, on card hover; the menu item stays as the keyboard and touch path) — a control only an overflow menu offers is one nobody finds; and a preview with nothing to draw draws the **slot** (bordered cell + picture glyph) and a sentence saying why it is empty, never a blank fill. It is the anti-fabrication rule's other half: a preview must show the real thing or the reason it cannot, and a silent blank reads as a broken feature exactly like an invented example does. The pin grid is hidden outright when nothing can be pinned, rather than repeating that sentence twice.

**Three things the browser caught that no CI gate could**, all now fixed and worth not repeating:

- A grid item's `min-height` is `auto`, so an `<img>` taller than its cell **refuses to shrink** and bleeds over the card's own text. It looked fine at desktop width only because the card clips. Any picture in a grid or flex cell needs `min-h-0` (`min-w-0` for the row axis).
- A `pointerdown` listener that closes a menu fires **before** the `click` on the item inside it, so every row silently did nothing but shut the menu. Close on a press only when it lands OUTSIDE the menu.
- The rhythm strip cannot sit on `paper-2`: that colour **is** the ramp's bottom rung, so a trip with nothing told drew an empty box. It goes on the card's own surface, keeping the grid's relationship (bare cells against the page behind them), and an untold day is a 12px stub rather than a 4px one — a told day RISES from a ruler that is always drawn.

**The cover is portable and the pins travel with it**, because they are post ids and posts are in the file — an imported trip lights up on the pieces it was pinned to as its thumbnails come back. The trap this re-proved: `tripDocFromFile` spreads `createTripDoc`, so a portable field forgotten *there* compiles and drops silently. TypeScript caught the other three write/read paths in `trip-file.ts`; that one it cannot. Same fault `applyProjectFile` once had on the studio's intros (`studio.md`).

## Dates are calendar days, and the arithmetic runs in UTC (2026-08-23)

**Decision.** `IsoDate` is a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` string and every subtraction in `trip-days.ts` goes through `Date.UTC` / `getUTC*`, never a local `Date`. **Why**: the same rule `telemetry/time-format.ts` follows — parsing locally lets the *reading* machine's timezone move a value, so a trip planned in France and reviewed in Australia would disagree about which day a photo belongs to. UTC also has no DST, so stepping a day is one constant; a local-`Date` implementation lands on 30 March 2025 twice or skips it (both cases are tested). The one place a LOCAL reading is correct is `todayIso()` — "today" is the date on the wall behind the person, not an instant — and it is frozen into an `IsoDate` immediately.

**`parseIsoDate` refuses calendar impossibilities** (`2025-02-30`, `2025-02-29`) rather than letting `Date.UTC` roll them over. A trip whose end date silently moved by two days is worse than one that refuses to be created.

## The grid draws holes, so empty is a real cell (2026-08-23)

**Decision.** Every day of the trip is drawn whether or not anything came out of it, and the first and last weeks keep their real weekday shape (leading/trailing `null` padding in `heatmapWeeks`). **Why**: a trip starting on a Thursday must start three cells down, or the whole grid reads as the wrong weekday and the "I never post on Sundays" pattern the grid exists to reveal would be a lie.

**Five rungs, and they are the maintainer's question in order**: nothing here · drafted but never sent · sent once · twice · more. A drafted day must be visibly **not** an empty one (there is work sitting there) and just as visibly not a published one. Measured in the browser: the first pass used `#f6e6df` (the accent-wash token) for the drafted rung and it was indistinguishable from bare paper at 14 px — the ramp is now `#efe9dd · #f4cdbd · #eb9878 · #e26a45 · #d9442a`. Do not flatten it back toward the wash token for palette tidiness; the rung has to survive at cell size.

**The grid scrolls inside its own container.** 310 days is 45 columns, wider than most screens; the page itself must never scroll sideways (verified 0 px document overflow at 390 px and 1440 px).

## Storage: the studio's hand-rolled store, not Dexie, not SQLite (2026-08-23)

**Decision.** `trip-store.ts` mirrors `projects/project-store.ts` — hand-rolled IndexedDB, one database, one object store, migrate on read, degrade to no-op on failure. **Rejected in conversation, with reasons, so they are not re-proposed:** *SQLite* (wasm/OPFS) would embed a relational engine for a model that is one document per trip with no joins and no queries; *Dexie* was floated and the maintainer leaned toward it, but at implementation time the existing store turned out to be 100 lines and directly reusable, so a dependency would have bought ergonomics for schema work that does not exist here. If the model ever grows genuine cross-trip queries, revisit Dexie — not before.

**Its own database (`atelier-roadtrip`), not a second store beside the studio's**: the two documents have separate versions and separate migrations, and a schema bump on one must not force an upgrade transaction on the other.

`requestPersistentStorage()` stays exported from `project-store.ts` and is called by both tools — the grant is **origin-wide**, so whichever tool mounts first serves both.

## The badge is overlay ELEMENTS, not a second renderer (2026-08-23)

**Decision.** `badge-layout.ts` turns the badge's text into ordinary `text` `OverlayElement`s and hands them to `drawOverlays`. **Why**: `shared/overlay/` already places, styles, themes, glows and burns in text, in the preview and both export paths — so the badge inherits the four title-style presets for free and the engine never learns that trips exist. Same lesson as the studio's intro (`studio.md`): extend the element model, never add a parallel class of thing. `shared/roadtrip/` importing `shared/overlay/` is shared→shared and fine; the tool imports both.

**The content has FIVE pieces and the numeral is alone in one of them** (`day-badge.ts`): kicker · label · headline · counter · caption. The word ("Jour") is its own piece precisely so it can be set at a fifth of the numeral's size — folded into the headline it would be drawn at the same size and the badge would read as a phrase, which is the opposite of the maintainer's "one dominant number". `RATIOS` in `badge-layout.ts` keeps every other piece under a third of the headline, and a test asserts it.

**"Il y a 1 an" is a KICKER OVERRIDE, not a counter mode.** It answers "why is this going out now", a different question from "where in the trip is this"; as a mode it would have cost the day number its place as the headline. The two compose. It falls back to the trip's name under a year rather than announcing "0 ans" — `yearsBetween` counts on the calendar, so a 29 February anniversary lands on 1 March in common years (dividing by 365.25 lands either side by turns).

**A stage counter over a date outside every stage falls back to the day of the trip**, never invents a place. Same anti-fabrication line as the overlay palette and the battery gauge.

**The layout needs the frame's ASPECT**, because element `y` is a fraction of the height while `sizeFrac` is a fraction of the *shorter side*: on 9:16 a line of 0.17 covers 0.17 × 9/16 of the height. Stacking in raw `sizeFrac` spaces correctly on a square and pulls apart on a portrait frame — `heightFractionOf` is the one conversion, tested both ways.

## The badge's words are DATA, and English is only their default (2026-08-23)

**Decision.** The maintainer's instruction: "english by default, no need for french actually, give option to override text". So the fr/en enum is gone. The trip carries a `BadgeWords` record — six fields, English out of the box — and any single piece can additionally be replaced with free text per post (`PostBadge.textOverrides`). **Why this scope split**: the vocabulary is trip-wide (typing "Jour" 250 times is not an option), while "THE RED CENTRE" instead of the trip's name is a property of one post. A one-click Français button fills the six fields; it is a convenience, not a second built-in language.

**An empty override means "computed", never "blank".** Clearing the field has to give the derived value back, or an override would be a one-way door with no way to see the real number again. Tested.

**The v2 → v3 migration translates the retired enum into the vocabulary it stood for** rather than dropping it: a trip that was set to French keeps saying exactly what it said. A migration must never silently re-language published copy.

## A piece may depart from the trip's style, and departure is two moves (2026-08-23)

**Decision.** `BadgePieceStyle` (casing, ink, panel fill/radius/outline, animation) is applied in `badge-layout.ts` by writing the element's own value **and pinning exactly that key in `styleOverrides`**. Anything left unset stays fully themed, which is what keeps one preset change restyling the whole deck — the cascade in `title-styles.ts` already does the work, nothing new was invented.

**Casing is applied to the STRING, then `uppercase` is pinned off.** Setting the element's `uppercase` flag would let a theme that uppercases undo a deliberate lowercase; transforming the text and pinning the key is the only way round that survives a theme change. Tested both ways.

**An outline with no fill is a real look** — a hairline frame around the trip's name — so `borderColor` alone builds a `box` with a transparent fill rather than requiring a background first.

**Animation is the engine's own model, with no translation layer** (`shared/overlay/animation.ts`): the same fade / slide / scale / typewriter / wipe the studio's intro titles use, so a look authored on a badge means the same thing there. An animated piece is given `window = {start: 0, end: null}` — an animation needs a life to play inside, and a badge lives for the whole shot. `badgeSettleSeconds` is what a still defaults to, so a PNG is never caught mid-slide; it ignores exits deliberately (a still wants the badge settled, not gone).

## The piece editor is four tabs, ONE deck band and a trip sheet (2026-09-06, rev. 2026-09-14)

**Rev. 2026-09-13 — every tab speaks the inspector grammar** (`frontend.md`, «Every side panel speaks ONE grammar»). Content: Slide (text or caption, goes out as, the clip's range and speed as rows — the speed writes the same field as the transport — on screen), Day, Counter, Time. Look: Opener, Title style (Trip), This piece (reset in its header), Placement (anchor, numeral, duration), Shades. Picture: Picture, This day on the instance (a CONTROLLED section, so the instance is asked only while it is open), Framing (reset in its header), Develop, Format, Grade. Export: What goes out (the plan, a switch for everything-as-images, the primary), One format at a time, Studio. The piece picker is a row above the tabs. `ModeChoice` is gone: a mode is a select whose options carry the real line, the chosen one repeated as the hint. Section folds are remembered per id (`atelier.inspector.piece.*`).

**2026-09-14 — the deck and its transport are ONE band under the picture, «Aiguille»** (`DeckStrip` + `use-deck-transport.ts` + `shared/roadtrip/deck-strip.ts`), the maintainer's pick from a three-direction design pass (Piste: bars as a timeline · Loupe: the open slide expanded over its source · Aiguille: a fixed needle with the piece sliding under it), and it REPLACED three things — `ClipTransport` under the stage, `DeckTimeline` under it on a wide screen, `SlideRail` beside it on a phone — about 175px of height for a 32px control row and a 56px band, the same at every width. **What it is**: every slide end to end on the piece's clock, a clip as wide as its cut and a still (photo, closing card) as wide as the seconds its inspector gives it (`screenLength`; the maintainer's answer: "une slide image dure la durée renseignée dans son inspecteur"), at a fixed px/s (42 wide, 30 compact) with a 26px floor — so the mapping is piecewise and time ↔ x only ever goes through the cells. The slide under the needle IS the open slide: dragging scrubs and selects in one gesture, a flick glides and snaps to a slide edge within 10px, a tap opens the cell under it, ←/→ step slides, Shift ±0.5s, a horizontal wheel scrubs. The row carries ▶ (plays the WHOLE piece — his answer), the timecode, the open slide's name and cut, the clip's speed pill, **Cut** (✂: the Studio's `TrimBar` replaces the band in place and ▶ loops the stretch; `I`/`O` cut at the playhead anywhere on a clip slide), the ticks toggle, `⋯` (move earlier/later, remove, closing card on/off/edit) and `+`. **How the whole piece plays on a stage that decodes one slide**: `useDeckTransport` opens each slide in turn with two clocks, never both — on a LOADED clip the `<video>` is the clock (`onTime`, and `onEnded` advances), on anything else a rAF loop counts the slide's seconds; the piece's time is derived (open slide's start + local), a still's local is stored WITH its slide key so a slide opened from elsewhere starts at 0 with no reset effect, and a scrub into the middle of a clip hands its local to the editor's playhead reset through `pendingLocal(key)`. **Traps, both real**: (1) the stage's `srcInfo` must carry the FILE it describes — while the piece plays from one clip into the next the old duration cut the new clip with a stretch it does not have; `duration` is 0 until `srcInfo.file === slideFile`, and a still's clock waits (≤2s grace) on a picture still decoding so a clip is not skipped before its first frame. (2) Space on a focused `role="slider"` is owned by the slider in `transport-keys.ts`, so the band handles Space itself. Decoded clip durations are remembered by file name so a stored 5s over a 3s clip reads 3 on the band after that slide was opened once. **Verified in the desktop Browser pane** (clip hook + two photos + a clip): ▶ from 0 ran Hook 0→5, Picture 2 5→8, Picture 3 8→11, held ~0.5s on 11 while the second clip decoded, played it to 14 and stopped; a drag, a tap (→ 8.0 on Picture 3), End then ← (→ 11.0), Cut + `I` at 1.0s (range 1→3, piece 14→13s), the cut looping, `⋯` Move earlier (selection and local followed), and at 375px one band under the stage, no rail, no overflow.

**Rev. 2026-09-13 — the inspector rides the foundations.** The tab strip is a `Segmented` (fill), the badge piece is ONE select in the suite's dress (`PiecePicker`; six chips read as a filter that changed the whole panel — a click on the stage stays the better way), "Goes out as" is a labelled row with a `Segmented` (Auto · Image · Video, the real cost of each in its title), the grade scope too. `panels/ui.ts` is now a thin wrapper over `shared/ui`: `smallButton` IS `buttonClass('default','sm')`, `chipClass` wears `Segmented`'s tile, inputs and notes take the control radius, `rowLabel` is the label column of a field row. `ModeChoice` keeps its cards — they carry the REAL line each mode would draw, which a segment cannot. **Rev. 2026-09-14**: the docked inspector wears the Studio's rounded frame (tab strip + piece row + sections in one box); the PageBar and the name stay above it, outside — `frontend.md`, «A section folds from its WHOLE header band».

## The closing card is the LAST thing the rail offers, and it is opt-in (2026-09-09)

**From the maintainer**, who found a carousel arriving as hook · closing card · `+`: the way to add a picture sat *after* the card, while adding one inserts it *before* the card — so the rail contradicted the deck it draws. **Decisions, and they apply to every kind alike** (his instruction: take the reel's behaviour everywhere): the rail is pictures → `+ Add` → the closing card, which is either the card itself or the offer of one and is always last; and `createTripPost` gives **no** piece a closing card, carousel included. A slide nobody composed does not belong in a deck by default, and it is one click to add, on the rail, where it is seen — the same rule the v5 migration already followed ("nothing existing gains a call to action").

**A card that can be turned on has to be turnable off**: the open card carries the same `×` a picture does. That is the one-way-door rule the cover panel had to be taught (see above), met a second time — check what happens when the control that adds a thing is the only control there is.

**The index a cell reports is its place in the DECK, not in the rail** (`slides.indexOf(s)`): the two now differ by the card the rail draws last, and a cell reporting its rail position would open a different slide.

**The offer leads somewhere when it cannot be taken.** With `includeCta` on but the trip's card emptied, `deckSlides` yields no last slide, so the rail would show a `+` whose click repeats a click already made; it opens the trip sheet at the closing card instead. `DEFAULT_CTA` is filled, so this is only reachable by clearing all three fields.

## A rail cell is the SLIDE, not the file behind it (2026-09-09)

**The maintainer's report**: a zoom or a crop on a picture, and a caption added to a slide, never reached the rail's thumbnails — so the strip showed a picture the piece does not deliver. It was literally true: a cell drew the raw `File` through an `<img>` with `object-cover`, which is a different picture from the composed slide the moment anything is framed, captioned, shaded or graded. **Decision**: a cell goes through `renderBadge`, the one renderer the stage and the PNG deck already share, at cell size (`use-rail-thumbs.ts`). A preview that is a cheap approximation of the deliverable is the fault this tool keeps re-learning — the same rule as `BadgeStage`'s own comment, applied one level down.

**What each slide is made of now lives in ONE function**, `shared/roadtrip/slide-render.ts`: the badge over the hook, a caption over a content picture, the trip's flat card at the end, each with its own framing. It was derived separately by the stage and by `deck-export.ts`, and a third copy for the rail is exactly how a thumbnail starts disagreeing with the file that goes out. Merging them fixed a real divergence in passing: the PNG export handed `block: null`, so a shade set to *follow the hook* fell back to its plain reach and landed somewhere other than in the preview. The badge's CLOCK is deliberately NOT in that function — it moves 60×/s while the transport plays, and folding it in would rebuild every element per frame.

**What makes a whole deck affordable to draw**, and the constraints any future consumer of it must keep: each cell carries a SIGNATURE of everything drawn into it (the render options, the framing, the file's identity, the grade, the aspect), so a pass redraws only what moved and switching slides costs nothing; pictures are decoded through `loadBadgeSource`'s new `maxWidth` (512 px) rather than at their own density, since a carousel of 48-megapixel stills would otherwise put 194 MB up **per cell** — the trap `media-pipeline.md` names for the stage, one level down; one decoded source is kept between passes so a run of keystrokes decodes once; cells are drawn one at a time and a pass is abandoned when its inputs change; and the badge is drawn **settled**, never at the transport's time, or a thumbnail changes while you watch it. `maxWidth` bounds the WIDTH alone because the natural size is unknown before the decode, and a browser that ignores `resizeWidth` simply decodes at full size.

**This reverses the "a clip costs a decode per cell" rule** that kept clips showing a film glyph. It was right when the alternative was decoding per paint; with a signature cache a clip is seeked once per CHANGE, which is what the deck export already pays. What survives of it: never decode a clip on a path that runs per render.

## Click selects a piece, drag moves the BLOCK (2026-09-06)

**Decision, with the maintainer.** `BadgeStage` hit-tests the canvas with the engine's own `measureBadge` → `hitTest` (the boxes of the last paint, measured with the very options it used) and a drag adds the pointer's travel to `post.badge.layout.x/y` (`moveBlock`, pure: clamp 0..1, soft-snap to 0/0.5/1 via the engine's `snap`, Alt to bypass). The whole stack moves, never one piece — per-piece free placement was offered and declined, so `RATIOS` and the block model stand. The 3×3 anchor grid stays the coarse tool. Drag is hook-only: a caption and the closing card have nothing to write back to, so there a click selects, focuses the field and the cursor says `pointer`, not `grab`.

A selection is cleared on slide change — the ids belong to one slide.

(2) `e.preventDefault()` on the canvas's `pointerdown` — cancelling it cancels the mousedown behind it, whose default action moved focus to the body the instant the field had taken it; without it every stage click "focused nothing".

## The grade is the trip's, a piece may depart, and the engine is the Studio's (2026-09-06)

**Decision, with the maintainer** (*"roadtrip must become a facade of the studio… underneath we use the studio rendering engine"*). `TripDoc.grade` (v10) holds `{ layers: SavedLutLayer[]; output }` — the Studio's own stored shape, so a custom `.cube` rides as text and the trip file carries it; `TripPost.grade` is `null` to FOLLOW the trip (the "empty means computed" rule) or a grade of its own, seeded from the trip's when the author departs. One grade per post for every picture of the deck; a per-slide grade is said in the panel as not offered, never hidden. `interpolation` is deliberately not stored — a render preference of the machine.

**Rendering is ONE insertion point**: `renderBadge` grades the source at its own density, then cover-crops (the `photo-frame.ts` rule — grading the crop would differ at every output size); shades, QR and the badge stay after. Preview and PNG deck share it. **The trap is the grader's lifetime**: `makeFrameGrader` is a WebGL2 context, only reclaimed on GC, and the stage repaints per transport frame — so `RenderBadgeOptions.grader` is caller-owned: `BadgeStage` keeps one keyed on (LUT, source size), `badgeToPng` makes and disposes one per slide. Rev. 2026-09-14: the STAGE decodes its still within the stage pixel budget (`boundSource`) and holds its grades (`holdGrades`, invalidated on `frameSeq`) — grading per repaint made every drag crawl (`media-pipeline.md`, «A preview grades once per picture»); the PNG deck and the exports still grade at source density.

## A picture's DEVELOP is per slide, and the sheet's draft rides the stack (2026-09-13)

**Decision (P2 of `docs/photo-develop.md`).** `PostBadge.develop` and `PostSlide.develop` (**v17** — v15 on its branch, renumbered on the merge because main took v15 for the opener and v16 for the speed the same night, the third time the rule under «A clip slide stores…» applied; `DevelopSettings | null`, null = as shot) sit beside `framing` and follow its rule: about ONE photograph, never inherited (`hookDefaultsFrom` leaves it out; `createPostSlide` starts null). `TripDoc.developPresets` (portable, `trip-file.ts`'s four places) is where time is saved — presets are APPLIED, never followed (see «One light on many pictures» below). The migration block is at the END of `migrateTripDoc`, the v12 trap; it reads through `developOrNull`, so junk lands clamped or as nothing.

**One cube per slide, from one stack.** `LutStack.composeWith(develop)` bakes the shared stack with a slide's own develop, memoised on the develop's value and reset when the stack changes; `PostEditor` hands it to the stage (`lutFor(slide.develop)`), the rail (`use-rail-thumbs` signs each cell with its own cube), the PNG deck (`renderDeck.lutFor(slide)`) and both hook videos. Never one cube for the deck: a corrected hook would otherwise grade the carousel's other pictures too.

**The sheet (`shared/develop/DevelopSheet.tsx`, one component for every host) keeps its DRAFT in `stack.develop`** so each slider step takes the deferred bake the strength slider already has, and paints from `stack.composed`; the host's own renderers keep reading the STORED value through `composeWith` until Done writes it, so the stage behind the sheet does not chase the sliders. Cancel puts the stack back (`setDevelop(null)` on unmount). It opens from the Picture tab's settled row (`describeDevelop`, `Develop…`, `↺`) between Framing and Format, never inside the hook-only branch — the medium/duration rule.

**Two traps, both measured in the probe.** (1) `loadBadgeSource`'s `maxWidth` ENLARGES a smaller picture to the cap (right for a rail cell, wrong for a preview: a 1600 px probe came back at 3840); the sheet decodes as-is and scales a big bitmap DOWN from the decoded one (`boundSource` in `badge-render.ts`, shared with the stage since 2026-09-14 and bounding by AREA, not the old 3840 px width), a resample and not a second decode. (2) On a phone the picture's height is a share of `--app-h`, never `vh`, and the column scrolls under it with Done pinned; the sheet is a `fixed` overlay rendered beside `TripSettingsModal`, inside the editor's `@container` section, exactly as that modal is.

**Verified in headless Chromium** (dev server, a dropped JPEG, a Single-photo piece): the sheet opens with 0 px overflow at 1280 and 390, +0.7 EV lifts the sheet's pixel from 163 to 203, Done writes `+0.7 EV` to the row and re-grades the stage and the rail cell, Escape closes it. The wipe and the hold chip were not driven by the probe.

## One light on many pictures: clipboard, presets, apply-to (2026-09-13)

**Decision (P4 of `docs/photo-develop.md` §8).** Every batch is a COPY into each target's own `develop` field, never a reference — the never-inherit rule kept under a time-saver. Three mechanisms, pure and tested in `shared/roadtrip/develop-apply.ts` and `shared/develop/develop-clipboard.ts`: (1) a session CLIPBOARD, module state shaped for `useSyncExternalStore`, never persisted (a clipboard outliving the tab would paste yesterday's light without anyone copying it) — it is what crosses Trips ↔ Studio with zero storage, since both hosts render the one `DevelopSheet`; (2) the trip's PRESETS, `savePreset` replacing a name already taken in place (saving "Desert noon" twice is one preset with the newer numbers), refusing a blank name or an as-shot develop (a preset of zeros is a button that does nothing), `removePreset` touching no piece; no factory set — a fabricated "Punchy" is an invented example; (3) two APPLY-TO verbs the host builds and the sheet draws with their counts: *the other slides of this piece* (`applyDevelopToPost`, skipping the open slide by id or `'hook'` and any slide without a picture) and *the other pictures of this day* (`applyDevelopToDay`, the other posts with the same `date`, never the post itself). A whole-trip batch is deliberately not offered.

**A verb writes NOW, Done writes the open picture alone.** The sheet's Done goes through `onChangePost` and a day batch through `onChangeTrip`; `RoadTripTool.updatePost` merges into the trip it holds in its closure, so two writes in ONE tick would clobber each other. Running a verb on its click (a separate tick from Done) is what keeps them apart — do not move the batch onto Done. The day batch stamps `updatedAt` in `PostEditor`, not in the pure helper. `newId` is exported from `trip-types.ts` for the preset id.

**The name field owns Enter.** `useDialogKeys` binds Enter on `window` and a text input does not own it (`dialog-keys.ts`), so with the preset field open the sheet passes `onConfirm: null` and the field's own `<form>` submits; Escape in the field stops propagation and closes the field, not the sheet.

**Verified in headless Chromium** (a carousel of a hook + two slides of one picture, a second piece of the same day): "Apply to 2 other slides" wrote `+0.7 EV` onto both slides, Enter in the name field saved the chip without closing the sheet, Paste was enabled in the second piece and restored `+0.7 EV`, the chip applied it, "Apply to 3 pictures of this day" wrote `+0.7 EV · contrast +10` onto the first piece's hook and slides while the open piece stayed as shot, and × removed the chip with every row unchanged.

## A derived element's id is a function of what it stands for (2026-09-06)

**Decision.** Road Trip's `OverlayElement[]` are rebuilt on every render and never stored, so `createTextElement`'s `uid()` gave each line a NEW id per repaint — useless to a stage that hit-tests the canvas and hands back an id. Every derived layout now mints a deterministic id and exports its inverse: `piece:<key>` (`pieceElementId` / `pieceFromElementId`, `badge-layout.ts`), `caption:<line>` (`deck.ts`), `cta:<role>:<line>` (`cta-slide.ts`). Safe because no document ever sees these ids; the Studio bridge prefixes them `roadtrip:` on the way over, so a resend still replaces. **How to apply**: any new derived element gets an id its module can parse back, and a test that two calls agree and the ids stay unique when a piece is absent.

## The engine's legibility box gained a radius and an outline (2026-08-23)

**Decision.** `LegibilityStyle` grew optional `radiusFrac`, `borderColor` and `borderWidthFrac`, and all four draw sites now go through one `paintLegibilityBox` helper. Before this each site hard-coded `pad * 0.5` and none could stroke. `radiusFrac` is a fraction of the PADDING and defaults to 0.5 precisely so every stored document keeps the exact shape it had. **How to apply**: the memory rule about `measureOverlays` still holds — the stroke sits on the box's own path, so half of it lies outside, and the grab box was widened by that half.

## The picture follows the Library selection (2026-08-23)

**Decision.** The badge composes over whatever is active in the shared Library sidebar (`useActiveAsset`), and the two stay in step both ways: opening a post activates its stored picture, and picking another asset in the sidebar re-points the post. **Why**: the maintainer asked for it by name to cut friction — a dropdown duplicating the sidebar is a second list to keep in sync by hand. **The trap to respect**: the restore must run only once and only after the library has loaded, and the record-back effect must be gated on it having run — otherwise the first render writes whatever happened to be active over the post's own choice.

**And when the library has nothing** (2026-09-06): a slide whose ref names a connected Winnow has its picture **fetched back** instead of being reported missing — a reload empties the pool, and re-finding the day on the calendar was the whole friction. It lives in `use-slide-library.ts`, which now returns a `SlideRecovery` the Picture tab draws (fetching, or the reason with a sign-in link). The mechanics that make it safe are in `architecture.md`, «A remote ref is re-FETCHED, never cached»; the one specific to this hook is that the restore gate is **claimed before the await and released when the files land**, so nothing downloads twice and the arriving picture still gets activated. A ref with no `assetId`, or one naming an instance this browser has not been given, keeps the old sentence — the honest one.

## The temporal line replaced the anniversary boolean (2026-08-24)

**The complaint that caused it**: "one year ago today" fired on any date a year or more after the shot, so it announced an anniversary on days that were not one. That is the one thing this tool must not do. **Decision**: the boolean became a MODE (`time-ago.ts`) — off · auto · anniversary · years+months · months · weeks · days · since — and `anniversary` now fires only when the month AND day match. Every other mode says something true on any day, and `auto` picks the truest striking line for the gap on the day it is read. When a mode has nothing true to say it returns null and the trip's name comes back; the panel shows the ACTUAL line rather than describing it, so a mode that is silent is visible as silent.

**The reference day is an input, not `Date.now()`** (`PostBadge.referenceDate`, null = the real today). A post is composed before it goes out, and the line has to read correctly on the day it is published. This is also what makes the whole module testable without freezing a clock.

**Units are counted on the calendar, never by division** (`monthsBetween` beside `yearsBetween`): a month is not 30.44 days, and "1 month ago" turning over on the 30th of a 31-day month reads as wrong to the person who took the photograph. `quantity()` picks the noun by the number from a table, so a language whose plural is the same word ("mois") is a data problem, not a rule.

## The hook has a duration, and that is what makes exits work (2026-08-24)

**The bug**: out animations did nothing. Cause — `badge-layout` gave every animated piece `window = {start: 0, end: null}`, and the engine lays an OUT step against the window's END, so a null end left it nowhere to play. **Decision**: `PostBadge.durationSeconds` (default 4) is the hook's own life; a piece that EXITS gets `{0, duration}`, a piece that only enters keeps `{0, null}` so it does not vanish for no reason, and an unanimated piece gets no window at all. The transport loops over the duration when anything exits — you have to watch it leave.

## The hook's picture is reframed, and the crop is a value (2026-09-07, rev. 2026-09-13)

**Decision, with the maintainer** (*"we need the ability to drag, crop, resize, rotate the media or picture use for the hook"*), reversing the note in `coverRect` that said an author reframes by choosing a different picture. That held while a piece went out as shot; it stops holding the moment the frame is 9:16 and the subject is not in the middle of a 3:2 photograph. `PostBadge.framing` (the hook) and `PostSlide.framing` (a carousel picture) carry `{ scale, x, y, rotation }` — v12 — the same split `videoTimeSeconds` already makes, because both are about ONE photograph rather than about the piece. A crop is **never inherited**: `hookDefaultsFrom` leaves it out, or one picture's subject decides the next one's crop.

**Two invariants make it safe to store** (`shared/media/framing.ts`, pure and tested): scale is clamped at 1 — the scale that exactly covers, whatever the rotation — and the pan is clamped to the slack that leaves, so no stored value under `fit: 'cover'` can produce a gap; and the pan is a fraction of the output's LONG EDGE, so the 720px preview and the 2160px export frame identically. Under cover the pan runs along the PICTURE's axes, not the frame's: once a horizon is straightened, "move it left" means along that horizon, and the clamp is a rectangle instead of a rotated diamond (`panBy` converts a pointer delta). The cover condition with rotation is exact, not conservative: an axis-aligned rectangle contains a rotated one exactly when it contains its bounding box.

**2026-09-13 — the maintainer reversed "never a gap": a picture can be shown WHOLE, with black bars, and mirrored** (*"option to flip media on x and/or y … possibility to have black bars, so we can see the whole media"*). `Framing` gained `flipX`, `flipY` and `fit: 'cover' | 'contain'` (**v18**, the block at the END of `migrateTripDoc`, every stored framing landing unmirrored on cover). The gap is now only ever ASKED for: under `contain` scale 1 is the scale that shows the whole rotated picture, the pan runs along the FRAME's axes and clamps the rotated picture's bounding box inside the frame (exact at any angle — the picture-axis clamp let a turned picture's corners slide out, caught by a test), and `drawFramed` paints the frame `#000` first, because the video pipeline reuses one canvas and an unpainted bar shows the previous frame. The mirror is stored in the picture's axes (applied innermost, before the rotation), and the UI never toggles it raw: `flipFraming` mirrors what the FRAME shows by also negating the rotation and the pan along that axis (M·R(θ) = R(−θ)·M), so "Horizontal" on a quarter-turned picture still flips left ↔ right on screen. **How to apply**: switching fit re-centres at scale 1 (a crop's zoom means nothing once bars are allowed); Reset keeps the chosen fit; the fit is per PICTURE like the rest of the framing, never a trip or piece default. Verified with a real H.264 hook clip in the Browser pane: bars black, mirror burned in.

**One transform, three renderers.** `drawFramed` is called by `renderBadge` (preview and PNG deck) and by `exportVariantVideo` (the burned-in hook clip), which gained an optional `framing` defaulting to the centred cover — so the Studio's own exports are untouched. `coverRect` was deleted: it had no caller left, and a second copy of this maths is exactly how a preview and an export start disagreeing. The framing does NOT cross the Studio bridge — a reel from a linked project is framed there, over that project's footage — and the Picture tab says so when a project is linked.

**Gesture.** Drag the picture where no element sits (the badge keeps first claim on a press — a hook is composed far more often than it is reframed), wheel or trackpad-pinch to zoom, a Fill/Whole switch, sliders, ⟲/⟳ 90° and the two flips in the Picture tab, "Reset" back to centred inside the chosen fit. The wheel listener is attached NATIVELY and non-passively: React's own wheel handler is passive, so `preventDefault` there is ignored and the page scrolls away under the picture.

**Trap, measured.** `migrateTripDoc`'s blocks run in SOURCE order, not version order — the badge itself is only built by the v2 block and slides arrive with v5. A v12 block placed at the top wrote `{ framing }` over a badge that did not exist yet and left every other field undefined, on a v1 document. Anything filling a field goes at the END of that function, and there is a regression test for it.

## The Itinerary opener: the author's own map, and the media along it (2026-09-14)

**Decision, from the maintainer** (*"a map hook, where i can pick different place on the map, and display them on a map path … options to present the media along the map"*). The engine's third variant, `map` — **Itinerary** on screen, `map` everywhere it is written down, the Trips-vs-`roadtrip` rule. It is deliberately the inverse of the Route: the Route READS the legs and therefore refuses to pin a point (a place has no dates), while here every stop is the AUTHOR's assertion — picked, ordered and given its picture by hand — so marking a point and captioning a photograph with a place name claims nothing the document cannot support. That one sentence is what justifies everything the Route would not do — and it is what still separates the Itinerary from **Virée**, the suite's other map opener: Virée's stops come from the DOCUMENT (the legs, or a picture's own GPS) and it drives a car over them; the Itinerary's come from the author, hold one picture each and are drawn still. Two map openers are not a duplication while they answer different questions; the Route, which answered the same one as the Itinerary with less, is the one that went.

**A stop is a place and, optionally, ONE picture** (`MapStop {id, name, lat, lon, picture?}`), stored in the variant's own options, so it travels in `.roadtrip.json` through `portablePost`'s deep clone with no migration and no fifth site (the `PostBadge.hook` field is v15). `mapOptions` reads a stored list the way `routeOptions` reads a colour: a stop with no finite coordinates is DROPPED (a place typed by hand is a complete place, just not a point), a picture whose ref cannot name a file is dropped, and the list is capped at `MAP_MAX_STOPS` (24) — past that the dots merge and every picture is another decode.

**The clock is PHASES, not one curve.** Hold, then per hop: travel, then dwell. Travel time is shared out by planar hop LENGTH (an even split makes a long hop look teleported) and the easing shapes EACH hop, so «Even» + no dwell is a constant-speed pen and «Settle» + a dwell is a car stopping at each town. One curve over the whole line cannot express a wait, and the wait is what makes a picture at a stop legible rather than a flicker. With the drawing off, `mapTiming` returns every arrival at zero rather than an empty list — the whole path drawn, every pin up, the last stop showing — so no reader downstream branches on "is this one moving".

**The picking map is the export's own projection, run backwards.** `fitProjection` gained an `unproject`, so a click on the field is a coordinate pair and a stop can be dropped where there is nothing to click on; what you point at is what goes out, at the same scale and with the same bow. **No tiles and no basemap, deliberately**: the suite has two network exceptions (the Flight Map's opt-in OSM layer, the opt-in place lookup) and a third hidden inside an options panel is how a local-first promise stops being checkable. The backdrop is a graticule, the trip's own located places are the landmarks (a click adopts one), and a place you cannot see is found by NAME through the same `PlaceSearchField` the legs use. Every gesture has its keyboard twin in the panel beside it: chips for the trip's places, the search field, two number fields for the selected stop's position, buttons for the order.

**Four ways to present the media, and what each is for**: **pin** (a tile beside the stop's own dot, several on screen at once — the mode that makes it an itinerary rather than a slideshow), **card** (one tile under the map, cross-fading, captioned with the stop's NAME — which is why a paper mount is the default: the mount is where a name can go), **backdrop** (the picture fills the frame, dimmed so the line survives; the only mode that replaces the piece's own picture), **strip** (every stop's picture in a contact row, the ones ahead held back behind a veil — the only mode that shows what is still to come). A stop with no picture, or one that could not be decoded, draws **nothing**: no stand-in, no previous picture carried forward — a card captioned "Exmouth" showing Coral Bay is the exact fabrication this tool refuses. Because that means an opener can END on an empty frame, the panel says so when the last stop has no picture; it never quietly fills it.

**The line ahead is the path's BED, not what is left over (2026-09-15).** The maintainer's report: *"when the current trace path is being drawn it removes the faded line underneath (dashed, faint…)"*. The paint drew each hop once, up to the pen — so the moment the pen entered a hop, that hop's dashed remainder vanished and the shape of the journey stopped being readable exactly where the eye was following it. It is now TWO layers over every hop: the undrawn part in the ahead style (`quadTail`, the other half of the de Casteljau split `quadSplit` already gave), then the travelled part solid on top. The rule generalises: **a progress drawing shows the whole path and fills it in; it never grows into nothing.** `hidden` still means hidden — it is the one style that draws no bed.

**Names can ACCUMULATE behind the pen (2026-09-15).** `labels` gained `passed` — every stop the pen has reached keeps its name — beside `current`, which names only where the pen is. Both readings are wanted (a list being written vs. one name following the pen), which is why this is a list of modes and not a switch; `all` was the only way to see more than one name, and it says every name from the first frame, which is a different picture again.

**The opener is CONTENT on the stage: click it, drag it (2026-09-15).** The maintainer: *"we should be able to drag the opener anywhere, like any other content: we click on it and it opens its inspector and we can drag it too"*. `HookVariant` gained two optional, **editor-only** members — `frameBox(options, ctx, frame)` (where the drawing sits, asked for at the stage's own pixel size, so a rect measured against another size cannot hit-test a place the drawing is not) and `moveBy(options, dx, dy)` (pure, incremental, fractions of the frame; options in, options out, so the stage writes them back through the one path the panel uses). Deliberately NOT on `HookRender`: `prepare()`'s closure is what every renderer and both exports share, and none of them has business knowing where a pointer is. A variant that offers neither is simply not grabbable, which is right for the badge and for Défilé's full-frame tape. On the stage the **badge keeps first claim** — an opener is placed once, a badge is composed constantly — so the opener is tested only where no element sits, and the picture only where neither does. Selecting it switches the inspector to Look (its panel IS what was selected; there is no one field to focus) and it wears the same dashed outline an element does. The Itinerary stores the drag as `offsetX/offsetY` beside the coarse anchor, clamped to ±45 % so a map can never be dragged out of the frame, and the panel grows a **"Put it back"** row once there is something to undo — the one-way-door rule the cover panel had to be taught.

**A pinned picture's STEM is drawn under the dot (2026-09-15).** The maintainer, looking at a real piece: *"the picture stem should be displayed un[der] the place dot"*. It was painted with its tile, after the dots, so the hairline crossed the numbered disc and read as a line THROUGH the place rather than as a picture belonging to it. The pins are now LAID OUT before the stops are drawn (`layoutPins`) and painted in two halves on either side of them — stem under, tile over. The rule for anything that points at a marker: the marker is the last thing drawn where they meet.

**Traps, all three measured in the browser:**

- **A pin covers what is under it.** Two stops a degree apart put the second's dot (and the hop reaching it) under the first's photograph — Exmouth vanished from the first render. A pin spot is now rejected if it covers ANOTHER stop's dot, on top of the frame and other-pin tests; the placement order is above, right, left, below, then dropped. The names use the same rule and take the placed pins as `reserved` boxes, so a name never lands on a photograph. That placer moved to `label-place.ts` (route-plan re-exports it) — the `easing.ts` / `tick-kits.ts` pattern, on its second consumer.
- **An inline `<svg>` with no height attribute defaults to `height: 100%`**, which in an auto-height column collapses it and leaves `aspect-ratio` nothing to govern: the picking map drew as a 25px band. `height: auto` is load-bearing on it.
- **A numbered dot has to be big enough to read a numeral in**: at the un-numbered dot's radius × 1.55 the glyph came out at 12px on a 1080-wide frame.

**The chooser takes files from the computer too (2026-09-15, from the maintainer: *"in Pictures for the sweep modal: lets be able to also pick local images as well"*).** It offered what was SHOT in the span — the Library's photographs, dated from their EXIF, and the instance's — so a picture it could not date into the span meant closing the sheet, expanding the Library, dropping the file and reopening. "Add from this computer…" opens the ordinary picker, puts the photographs in the Library (the one place every surface resolves a picture from) and remembers them for the life of the sheet. **The rule that makes it work: a file the author picked by hand is CHOSEN, not found, so the span does not filter it** — it stays in the grid whatever day it was shot on, ticked on arrival even when a held list does not name it, and grouped under its own date (outside the trip it simply has no "Day N"). Clips and RAWs are refused rather than added invisibly: this grid is photographs only. Défilé still leaves a picture dated outside the trip off the tape and says so — that is its own reading of a day, not the chooser's.

**The chooser's dates reach the WHOLE trip, and a picture the opener will not use is marked, not hidden (2026-09-15, reverses "nothing shot after the piece is ever offered").** The maintainer: *"we are reducing in term of media choice which feels legit but there are case where I want to fetch beyond the leg date range"*. So a **Whole trip** pill leads the quick spans (widest first) and both date fields are bounded by `tripSpan`, not `reachSpan`; `reachSpan` now only decides what the chooser OPENS on. Défilé and Virée still drop a picture shot after the piece's day (`partitionPicked` — a sweep or a drive cannot tell a day not yet lived); rather than refuse to show it, the chooser labels its day and its tile "left off" and counts the ticked ones, unless the variant says `HookPictureChoice.keepsLater` (the Itinerary, whose stops are the author's). **Trap it closed**: `defaultSpan` bounds a HELD list by the trip too — bounded by reach, a held picture from past the piece reopened outside the span and was silently dropped on confirm, because confirm keeps only what the grid shows. The two date fields are the shared `DateRangeField`: the hand-rolled `1fr auto 1fr` grid left each inline field on the left of its column and the arrow floating in the gap.

**The chooser had to learn one thing** (`HookPanelHost.choosePictures` gained an optional `HookPictureChoice`, and `defaultSpan` an `includeThisDay`): a sweep is a RUN-UP to the piece, so it opens on the days BEFORE it; an itinerary's stops are as often the day being told, and a chooser that hides today's photographs reads as "there are none". A pick of several is generous rather than clever: the first lands on the stop it was asked from, the rest fill the stops AFTER it that have none — never one that already holds a picture, never one before it — and what had nowhere to go is COUNTED in the panel.

**The PNG deck and the rail's thumbnails had to be taught the opener's pictures.** `slide-render.ts` passed none, on the reasoning that a still is drawn SETTLED, past the sweep, where the scrub shows the piece's own picture. That holds for Défilé and fails for this one: an itinerary still shows its stops' photographs at rest, so both surfaces were drawing a map the stage did not show. `slideRender` now takes an optional decoded set, `renderDeck` and `useRailThumbs` pass it, and the rail's signature carries a WeakMap token of the picture MAP (a `ResolvedHook` is closures, and `JSON.stringify` of a decoded bitmap is nothing — the cell would keep the empty map it was first drawn with). Only the hook's cell is signed with it, or one picture landing would redraw a whole carousel. The video paths already worked: they take the `ResolvedHook` the stage prepared.

## The picture gets treated, not the typography (2026-08-24)

**Decision.** `BadgeBackdrop` (vignette + scrim) is painted in `badge-render.ts` between the picture and the badge, never as overlay elements: it is full-frame, the engine has no such concept, and a scrim is a property of THIS picture rather than of the composition. The scrim runs either across the whole frame from the badge's own edge or confined to the hook zone (`gradientBand`, pure and tested), and the confined band is derived from `badgeBlockExtent` — the same numbers the layout uses — because a gradient that does not line up with the text it exists to lift is worse than none. **Why darken the picture rather than panel every line**: a bright sky exactly where the hook sits is the normal case, and doing it optically keeps the typography clean.

## A stage is a LEG, and its places are an ordered list (2026-09-03)

**Decision, taken with the maintainer.** A stage carries `places: TripPlace[]` — a name, a region, optional `{lat, lon}` — **in the order they were lived**. He asked for "un lieu de début et un lieu de fin" and answered his own question in the asking: *"toujours une collection, mais on prendra le début et la fin en fonction du besoin"*. So `stageStart`/`stageEnd` are `places[0]` and the last (`trip-places.ts`), never two stored fields. **Why**: the same rule that keeps a trip's 310 days derived from two dates — a second copy of a fact is a second thing to migrate and a second thing to get wrong. With one place, start and end are the same place, which is the truth: you did not go anywhere.

**A place carries NO dates.** The stage is the dated unit; a place is a point inside it. "Uluru on the 12th" inside a nine-day leg means splitting the leg. A dated place would be a stage under another name, and `stageAt` would then have two things to listen to.

**`stage.name` empty = computed, never blank** — the `textOverrides` rule again. Empty derives "Perth → Cairns" from the two ends; a typed name always wins; clearing it gives the derived label back. `stageRegionLabel` derives only the region the places **agree** on: Perth and Cairns have no common region, and printing either would be a quiet lie about the other. The whole derivation is `trip-places.ts`, pure and tested; `day-badge.ts` reaches it in two lines and still refuses to invent a place when a stage names none.

**The separator is `→` (U+2192), not an emoji** — the same measured rule as the `◆` marker below: this string is drawn on a canvas through a font stack we do not control, and `📍` drew *nothing at all* where no colour-emoji font existed. A test pins the glyph.

**The New trip modal's "Destination" became "From" / "To"**, one row, so it did not grow — the maintainer said explicitly *"je ne veux pas alourdir la modale de départ"*, and the old free text was already trying to be a route ("Australia — Perth to Cairns") that nothing could read. Filled, they seed **one stage covering the whole trip**, unnamed so its label derives. **Know the side effect**: that stage makes `stageAt` answer for every day, so the default badge's caption goes from empty to "Perth → Cairns" and the stage counters stop falling back. Nothing is false — it is the trip's route — but it only happens to a trip whose author filled the fields, and empty fields seed **nothing**, keeping the old behaviour exactly. `trip.destination` survives as the prose subtitle, composed from the two ends at creation.

## The legs are a RULER under the grid, one open at a time (2026-09-07)

**Decision, with the maintainer, from a three-direction design pass** (a timeline ruler · coloured bands over the grid plus a card rail · a split list-and-inspector; he chose the ruler). The stages accordion — every leg with every field, the widest thing on the page — is gone. `StageRuler` lays the legs along one horizontal track scaled to the trip's days, DaVinci-style; `StagesPanel` opens ONE leg beneath it; the geometry is pure (`shared/roadtrip/stage-ruler.ts`: bars in day offsets, greedy lanes for overlaps, the uncovered gaps, month rules) and the edits are pure (`stage-edit.ts`: resize an edge, slide a leg, start one on a day, cover a gap, the day's menu), so the component only turns pixels into whole days and hands over. **What a drag writes is the stage's two dates** — the same fields the date inputs edit — so the two can never disagree, and a leg cannot be dragged outside the trip because the trip IS the track.

**The grid and the ruler are one calendar seen twice, and the seam is four things.** Each day's cell wears its leg's tint as an inset stripe along its foot (`boxShadow: inset 0 -3px`, so the rung that says what was told is untouched); the open day is a playhead on the ruler; a click on a day a leg covers opens that leg, while a click on an uncovered day leaves the editor as it was rather than closing it; and a click on a LEG opens it and goes to the day it began. Selection lives in `TripOverview`, not the panel, because the calendar is the other way in. **Opening a leg says WHICH leg** (`onOpenStage(stage)`, never "select the day then derive"): on a travel day two legs overlap and `stageAt` answers with the later one, so deriving would open a different leg from the one clicked.

**A drag moves a leg and nothing else; a day is picked with a CLICK (2026-09-09, replacing the scrub).** The playhead used to be *scrubbed*: press the 16px head strip or the empty track, travel, and commit on release (a live `navigate` would have buried the day you came from under thirty history entries). The maintainer rejected the gesture on both counts, and the second is the load-bearing one — to receive those drags the scrub surface had to cover the whole track and set `touch-none`, so **a phone could not scroll a track that is wider than the screen by construction**, and every swipe meant to read the rest of the trip moved the day he was working on instead. Now: the surface is a plain `onClick` (`pickDay`) claiming no `touch-action`, so one finger scrolls the track exactly as it scrolls the calendar above; the playhead is a marker with arrow keys (a week with Shift) and no pointer drag; the release-deferred commit went with the gesture that needed it, since one click is one `navigate`. The general rule this fixed, and the `touch-pan-y` a leg's bar and edges now carry so a vertical swipe still scrolls the page, are in `frontend.md`, «A drag surface inside a scroll box claims only the axis it writes» — read it before adding any gesture here.

**A drag says the date it would land on** (`DatePin`): a chip following the pointer with the edge's new date and what the leg would then be ("7 Sep 2025 · 15 days"), both dates for a slide. It is `position: fixed` and clamped to the viewport for `DayCard`'s reason — the track scrolls sideways inside its own box, so a pin positioned inside it would be clipped by that box on the very drag that reaches its edge.

**"Start a stage here" inside a leg is a CUT**, the way a cut on a video timeline ends one clip where the next begins: the covering leg ends the day before and the new one takes its remaining days (`startStageAt`, tested). A leg that itself begins on that very day is left alone and the new one is a single day beside it. The menu (`dayStageActions`) names the real leg it would touch — "End “Perth → Kalbarri” here", or "End stage 2 here" for an unlabelled one, the same number the editor prints — never "this stage", which could be any of them; it does not offer to end a leg on the day it already ends. It is a right-click only: touch gets the ruler's `+` and the header's, and the hint under the ramp hides below 600px because it names a gesture a phone does not have.

**Four muted tints, never the accent** (`STAGE_TINTS`, oklch, same lightness and chroma, hue only): the accent means *selected* everywhere in the suite, and a leg must not look selected for being third. This is the one place the "one accent" identity admits a categorical palette, and it stays at 22% mixes and 3px stripes.

**Drag is back for places, and it is not drag-only.** `PlacesEditor` is a row of chips joined by the badge's `→`, reordered by HTML5 drag — the slide rail's own gesture — and, because drag is unreachable by keyboard, a focused chip moves with the arrow keys; so do a focused bar (slides) and edge (a day, a week with Shift). The earlier "buttons, not drag" call for places stands corrected by the maintainer's explicit ask for drag-and-drop; what survives of it is the rule that a drag surface always has a keyboard twin. A chip is dragged by its body; the ruler's bars swallow the click that ends a drag (`swallowClick`) so sliding a leg does not also toggle it. The ruler measures its own width with a `ResizeObserver` and lets a day shrink to 6px before the track scrolls inside its box — verified: 0px document overflow at 390px.

## The day's pictures come from the instance, keyed by the piece's own day (2026-09-06)

**Decision, from the maintainer's own report of the friction**: *"i have to pick the day that matches the current roadtrip element data manually"*, picked media *"pollutes the list"* once the piece moves on, and *"picked media from winnow are not persisted so every time i rerun the web app i have to reimport everything"*. Three complaints, and only the third was answered by the re-fetch (see «The picture follows the Library selection»). The other two are answered by `DayFromWinnow` in the Picture tab: **a post is keyed by the day it tells, so the day IS the query** — the strip asks the connected instance for `date_from = date_to = post.date` and needs no date picked by hand, and it re-points itself when another piece is opened.

**What it deliberately is not**: a second pool, a bulk import, or a second browser. One picture crosses per click, at proxy fidelity; it lands in the ordinary Library through `addFiles` and is then made the active asset, so the existing Library ↔ slide machinery records it onto the slide and no second path to a piece's picture exists. `WinnowBrowser` stays the way to bring a whole day in; this is the way to dress ONE piece, and that is what stops the pool filling with a day the author has moved past. **How to apply**: the strip opens by itself only when the slide has no picture — the case the friction is about — and its tiles wait while `SlideRecovery` is fetching, or the two races for which picture the slide ends up with.

**Its tiles shipped with `aspect-square` on a `1fr` auto-fill track (2026-09-07 fix)** — the exact trap `frontend.md` already names for the calendar cells in `WinnowBrowser`, struck a second time because the lesson lived only in the file that had already learned it. A tile's `<img>` now takes a fixed `h-[74px]` instead, matching the browser's own `Thumb`. See `frontend.md`, «A `1fr` grid track cannot be trusted to carry `aspect-ratio`».

**Since the Library's Winnow tab (2026-09-07) the strip is the SECOND consumer of the same two hooks** — `winnow/use-connection.ts` (which instance) and `winnow/use-scope-rows.ts` (its rows for a span, forgotten before re-asked) — so the piece editor and the sidebar cannot drift on which instance they ask or how. Road Trip is what makes the sidebar's tab follow a day at all: `PostEditor` publishes the piece's `date`→`endDate ?? date` and `TripOverview` the selected day through `usePublishMediaScope` (`shared/sources/media-scope.tsx`), taken back on unmount. **How to apply**: a Road Trip screen that shows a day or a span publishes it; a stage/leg would publish `stage.startDate`→`stage.endDate` the same way — the sidebar needs no new code for that, only a publisher. Whether the strip stays now that the tab shows the same day is the maintainer's call ("si elle est réparée elle pourrait finalement servir"); it was kept.

## The timeline is ASLEEP, and Atelier does not lean on it (2026-09-07)

**Decision, by the maintainer, and it settles a confusion worth naming.** Winnow's timeline is a young feature of another project; Atelier is mature. A mature tool must not depend on an immature one, so **`TIMELINE_SYNC_ENABLED` in `shared/sources/winnow/features.ts` is `false`** and every entry point disappears: the leg tab in the media browser, the "seed from" row in the New-trip modal, the "↓ From <host>" button over the stages, and the two link routes, which are consumed and land on the ordinary screen. `hasTimeline` reads the switch first, so one line closes them all and no immature route is ever called. The work is not deleted — `timeline-import.ts` and its 42 specs, `TimelineImportPanel` and the wire reading stay tested and dormant, and flipping the switch restores the previous behaviour exactly, capability degradation included.

**The confusion the switch ends.** Four recent features were being read as one because a spec note mixed them: the timeline sync (this one, asleep), the Road Trip editor (done), **the Library sidebar synchronised with an instance** (the maintainer's actual request, and the least advanced), and the trip's persistence on an instance. The instruction that produced all this asked for **media of the day being edited**, never for chapters — and a day, or a stage, is a DATE RANGE, which Winnow has filtered on since long before its timeline. **How to apply**: anything about reaching a Winnow's media goes through `date_from` / `date_to` (`WinnowBrowser`'s day view, `DayFromWinnow`), never through a chapter; a Road Trip stage carries its own two dates and needs nothing remote to say what it spans. Never put a media-reaching feature behind this switch.

## A Winnow chapter and a stage are the same object — seed, never sync (2026-09-03)

**Anticipated, nothing built.** Winnow is growing a **timeline**: media grouped into chapters by place and by date, with exports taken on a segment. That is `TripStage` reached from the media side — a span plus its places in lived order — so the trip model needs no invention. The full brief, with the field-by-field mapping, the API Atelier needs the spec to answer, the traps and the phases, is **`docs/winnow-timeline.md`**; it is a companion to `docs/winnow-bridge.md` and every claim about Winnow's side in it is marked as an assumption.

**The four rules it fixes, so they are not re-litigated when the spec lands**: a timeline is a FACT about media and a trip is a DECISION about telling it, so **an import creates stages and never posts** — a pre-filled calendar destroys the holes the grid exists to show; re-running an import is a **diff the author accepts leg by leg**, never a sync (two moving documents with one shape is the sync engine `winnow-bridge.md` ruled out twice); Atelier writes back in **Winnow's nouns** (asset, chapter, place), never its own, so Winnow learns a chapter has been told by *receiving the finals*, not by being told about a trip; and a chapter id is a hint for the next reconcile, never a pointer the trip dereferences — a seeded trip must open with the instance switched off.

**Phase T0 is built (2026-09-06)**: `TripDoc.sourceId` (**v11** — v10 went to `grade` the same day, and two branches each claiming v10 was the merge conflict that taught this: bump the version last, against `main`; the migration files every older trip under `local`, `createTripDoc` takes it as a trailing argument) and `TripStage.origin` (`StageOrigin`: the instance's host as `sourceIdFor()` mints it, the chapter's own id, an optional `revision`, `importedAt`). **The split in `.roadtrip.json` is deliberate and asymmetric**: `sourceId` is stripped both ways (an imported trip belongs to the source that imports it — `tripDocFromFile` takes the receiving source), while `origin` TRAVELS — the one pointer outside the browser the file keeps, because a host + that host's own chapter id means the same thing on another machine connected to the same Winnow, and dangles harmlessly where it is not. Never reuse a chapter id as `TripStage.id`, and never let anything but the reconcile diff read `origin`: a seeded trip must open identically with the instance gone.

**Phase T1 is built (2026-09-06)**: `shared/roadtrip/timeline-import.ts` — `importTimeline` (chapters → stages, span, uncovered runs, destination, warnings), `tripFromTimeline`, `diffTimeline`, `applyTimelineDiff`; pure, 42 specs. The choices in it, so the panels of T3 do not re-derive them: **`TimelineChapter` is Atelier's own shape, not the wire's** — the client normalises whatever the spec sends into it at the boundary, exactly as it does for the calendar's bounds, and this module never fetches. **A date that is not a plain `YYYY-MM-DD` is refused with a warning, never sliced** (the §8.1 trap made executable); a reversed span is swapped and said; a chapter with no dates or a duplicate id is left out and said — no chapter disappears in silence. **A title that is only the route yields an EMPTY name** (compared through `routeKey`, which reads `→`, `-`, `->`, `–`, `—` and "to" alike), so the label keeps deriving; the stage's own `region` stays empty and the place carries its own. Stages are sorted by span (the only lived order the dates give) and consecutive overlaps are warned with the leg a badge would name. **The diff matches id → same span → same first place with overlapping spans**, in that order, so it stays right when ids are regenerated nightly and a hand-drawn leg meets the chapter Winnow computed instead of doubling up; `changes` compares LABELS (`stageLabel`), so a title that still names the leg after its places changed is not reported as a rename. **A hand-made stage is never `dropped`**; only one whose `origin` names this source can be. **Apply touches only accepted keys**: a change lands on the SAME stage (id and the typed region survive), an accepted `unchanged` only stamps `origin` onto a stage met without one, the span is widened to hold an accepted leg and never shrunk, posts/words/theme/cta/hookDefaults are never read. `tripFromTimeline` makes the document `local` — where it LIVES — while every stage's `origin` names the instance.

**The wire the timeline is read from is Winnow's real one (2026-09-06, revised the day it was written)**. The first version was built against a guessed shape and was wrong in every key, so no chapter survived the boundary and the leg tab was empty against a real instance while every test passed against the guess. What the instance actually serves, and the two mappings that are judgement calls rather than transcription: Winnow names a derived chapter after its dominant place, so **only an authored chapter (`override_id`) yields a title** — importing a computed name pins as a decision what the place already says; and Winnow orders a chapter's places **by how much media each holds, not by lived order**, while a stage's first and last places are its start and end, so **one place crosses, never the list** — a route assembled from a dominance ranking can come out reversed. The full mapping table is the status block of `docs/winnow-timeline.md`. **How to apply**: when a brief's wire shape is a guess, the tests written beside it prove only the guess; go and read the other side's code before calling the feature built.

**The trap to carry into any date mapping**: take Winnow's `capture_date` as the day, **never recompute it from `captured_at`** in the browser. A clip shot at 07:00 in Perth is the 12th on the wall behind the photographer and the 11th in UTC, and `trip-days.ts` subtracts in UTC by design — recomputing walks a third of a trip back a day. **The timeline is the one route that offers no date** — a chapter carries instants and a `tz_offset_hours` to read them by — so the conversion happens once, at the boundary, in `localDayOf` (`client.ts`), which is Winnow's own `localDay` reproduced. Converting with the instance's offset honours the rule; converting in a panel, or with the browser's zone, is the trap wearing a different hat. With no offset (nothing in the leg is geotagged) the days are read at UTC and every surface says so.

## Seeding and completing a trip from a timeline: two screens, one arithmetic (2026-09-06)

**Phase T3 is built on Atelier's side** (`tools/roadtrip/TimelineImportPanel.tsx`), against the same assumed wire as T2. Both screens are the T1 module rendered: the SEED lists the chapters as ticks (a link's `chapters=` preselects, else all), shows the real stages / span / destination / uncovered runs / every chapter it could not use, and creates nothing until "Create trip"; the COMPLETION lists `diffTimeline`'s entries, one tick each. **Default ticks: add ✓, changed ✓, an unlinked match ✓ (it only stamps `origin`), dropped ✗** — the timeline never removes a leg on its own, and an `unchanged` leg that already carries an origin is shown inert, never hidden, so the author sees the whole comparison. Apply goes through `applyTimelineDiff`; when the span had to grow the shell says so in a dismissible line, because a trip whose dates moved silently would surprise every badge.

**Entry points are three and they all lead to the same panel**: a "or seed it from <host>" pill row in `NewTripModal` (rendered only when a Winnow is connected — the maintainer does not want the modal heavier; an instance without a timeline is offered greyed with the reason), a "↓ From <host>" button in the stages panel header, and the two link routes `#/roadtrip/new?source=<host>[&chapters=a,b]` and `#/roadtrip/<ref>/import?source=<host>` (`trip-route.ts`, `TimelineLink`). **A link names a HOST, never a URL** — `HOST_RE` refuses a scheme or a path, and the shell resolves the host against `getWinnowConnection`; unknown → `#/connect?instance=https://<host>&return=<link>`, and `ConnectScreen` now honours a same-app `return` path, so allowing the instance lands back on the proposal. The link is consumed once per path (a ref) and rewrites itself (`/roadtrip/home` or the trip) so a reload does not re-propose; the home redirect is guarded by `!route.link` or it fires first.

**Verified in headless Chromium against a stubbed Winnow** (`playwright-core` in the scratchpad, `page.route` on `https://winnow.example/**` with CORS headers, the connection seeded in `localStorage`): the whole flow at 1280 px and the seed sheet at 390 px with zero horizontal overflow. **Not verified**: a real instance, which does not serve a timeline yet. Winnow's own "Make a Road Trip from this leg" verb is the other repository's work and still open.

## Place search is online, opt-in, and never as-you-type (2026-09-03)

**A product decision the maintainer took deliberately**, against a codebase whose `TripStage.region` comment read *"never geocoded — nothing leaves the machine"* and whose README promised "one network exception". He was told that and chose the search anyway; the callout is now two exceptions and `local-first.md` carries the full terms. **Do not re-litigate it, and do not quietly widen it either.**

The shape it was given, which any future network feature should copy: the client lives in `shared/map/geocode.ts` beside the tile URL (one folder to audit); the consent flag lives in `localStorage`, **never on `TripDoc`** — an exported `.roadtrip.json` must not carry someone else's consent to another machine; URL-building and response-parsing are pure and tested (a test asserts the *entire* URL, so what would leave the machine is visible in the diff); and the search fires on **Enter or the button only**. That last is not an ergonomic choice: Nominatim caps callers at one request a second and a browser cannot send an identifying `User-Agent`, so one deliberate gesture per request is the only polite reading — and it keeps what leaves to what the author chose to send.

**The feature must stay a convenience.** Every place is typable by hand, `coords` is nullable everywhere, and no panel is disabled when the pref is off. If a future change makes the search load-bearing, the local-first claim quietly becomes false.

**Unverified against the live service, deliberately recorded.** This container's network policy cannot reach `nominatim.openstreetmap.org` (`curl` returns HTTP 000), so the round trip was never run for real. What *was* checked in the browser: the exact URL sent, that nothing leaves while the pref is off, that typing never fires a request even once it is on, and — through a stubbed route carrying a real `jsonv2` body — that the parsing, the result list and picking a candidate all work. The four failure paths (unreachable, 503, 429, empty) each report a sentence naming what happened and that typing by hand still works; a bare "Failed to fetch" reached the UI on the first cut and was fixed. **What a future agent should do on a machine with the network**: run one real search and confirm the shape still matches, since the fixture is built from the documented API rather than from a captured response.

## A post is a DECK, and a reel is a deck of one (2026-08-24)

**Decision.** `TripPost.slides` holds the pictures after the hook; `deckSlides()` assembles hook → content → call to action. Nothing branches on `post.kind`: a single photo and a reel are the same model with one slide, which is what lets a piece be re-cut into a carousel without being rebuilt. The CTA is **not stored on the post** — it is appended at render time from `TripDoc.cta`, which is the whole point of editing it once.

**A CTA that says nothing is not appended**, even when the post asks for it: a blank last slide is worse than none.

**File names are `NN-hook / NN / NN-cta`, zero-padded to at least two digits** so a file listing is already in swipe order — tested, because `10` sorting before `2` would silently reorder a carousel on upload.

**Content slides carry no badge.** The counter has done its work on slide one; repeating it would stop the hook being a hook. A caption keeps the trip's font but pins `glow` and `legibility` off — the glow is the badge's signature.

## A slide says what it IS, and the export only delivers it (2026-09-09)

**Decision, the maintainer's** (*"si j'ai l'ambition de gérer des types vidéo dans les slides, c'est un choix qu'on doit plutôt faire en amont, à la création du contenu du post… plutôt que de l'imposer à la dernière étape d'export qui va faire un choix à notre place"*). Every slide carries a **medium** (`auto | image | video`) and a **screen time** — `PostSlide.medium` / `.seconds`, `PostBadge.medium` / `.hookSeconds`, **v14** — and `deckSlides()` is the one place `auto` is resolved, handing back `medium`, `chosen`, `reason` and `seconds` on each `DeckSlide`. `auto` is video when something on the slide animates or its media is a clip (`classifyPart`, so the deck and the Library cannot disagree about what a clip is), image otherwise.

**It replaces an export-mode picker agreed hours earlier the same day** (Images · Video · Mixed at the door). The reason it lost: video content slides and animated captions are both coming, and a format decided at the door would have had to learn about each of them, while a format that belongs to the slide costs nothing later — the model is the test. What survives at export time is delivery only: combine into one file or not, plus an images override for a browser that cannot encode.

**A forced choice is obeyed and its cost is stated, never refused**: `image` over an animated hook is how a piece gets its grid picture (`reason: 'settled'`), `image` over a clip is its chosen frame (`'frozen'`), `video` over a photograph is a held card (`'forced-video'`). Each of the three chips shows what it would really do for THIS slide — and a chip whose hint would repeat its own label shows nothing instead, since "Image · image" is a control saying nothing.

**This reverses «The hook has a duration»'s session-state call.** The hook's length was deliberately not stored, on the reasoning that a length is an export choice. That stopped being true when every slide gained a screen time: it is `badge.hookSeconds` now, edited on the Content tab beside the medium, and `hookSecondsWithin` still clamps it on read so a stored 8s over a 3s clip never claims a file it cannot write. `badge.durationSeconds` is untouched and is a DIFFERENT number — the badge's own hold, what an exit animation lands on.

**The band is where the shape of the deck is read**: a cell that leaves as video wears its length. A carousel mixing a video hook and three stills says so at a glance, which is the whole point of deciding this upstream. Verified in the browser: a `.MP4` content slide resolves to video with no clicking, forcing the hook to an image drops its badge from the cell, and the document overflows nothing at 390px.

**Where the controls go, and the rule behind it**: the medium and the duration are about the SLIDE, so they sit on the Content tab and show for whichever slide is open — never inside the hook-only branch (the «A panel that belongs to the PIECE» rule, read the other way). The closing card is left out entirely: its medium is structural.

## A clip slide has an in point, a screen time and a SPEED — the source stretch is derived (2026-09-13)

**Decision** (the maintainer's ask: play the clips in the slides, trim them and choose their speed, "as the Studio does"). `PostBadge.videoSpeed` / `PostSlide.videoSpeed` (**v16**, default 1, the Studio's own steps `CLIP_SPEEDS` 0.25…4) join `videoTimeSeconds` (the in point) and `hookSeconds`/`seconds` (the SCREEN time). **No out point is stored**: the stretch of the source a slide delivers is `clipSlice(in, screen, speed, duration)` = `[in, in + screen × speed]`, derived in `hook-video.ts` and nowhere else, so the stage's trim bar, the rail's length and the encoder's cut cannot disagree about which frames go out. **Why the screen time and not the source length is what is stored**: the deck is composed in screen time (a rail cell says "3s", a reel is the sum of its slides), and it was already the stored number — a second copy (an out point) would be the "second thing to migrate" every memory entry on this model warns about. **How to apply**: a speed change keeps the FOOTAGE and moves the screen time (`retimedScreenSeconds`); an in handle moved on the trim bar keeps the out point (NLE semantics, so `seconds` changes); the filmstrip in the Picture tab moves the in point and keeps the length (slide the window). Every clamp goes through `screenSecondsCeiling(in, speed, duration)` — `hookSecondsWithin` takes the in point and the speed now, and a stored 9s at 2× over a 10s clip whose in point is 4s honestly reads 3s. A speed is never inherited by `hookDefaults` (it is about the clip in hand, like its frame), and the v16 block sits LAST in `migrateTripDoc` for the source-order trap. **It was v15 on its branch and the hook engine's branch claimed v15 the same night** — the second time two parallel branches took one version number (v10 was the first): on the merge the speed migration was renumbered v16 and placed after the opener's, since main's number wins. The rule stands and needs following, not restating: bump the version LAST, against `origin/main`, and expect a renumber on merge whenever another Road Trip branch is open. The plan (`export-plan.ts`) carries `speed` and `silent`: a re-timed clip ships without sound because audio is copied, never re-encoded (`media-pipeline.md`), and that is said in the plan, not discovered in the file. The speed is deliberately NOT in the hook's file name — it is composition, not a delivery departure the way the Studio's per-variant re-time is.

## A clip slide PLAYS on the stage, and the clip is the badge's clock (2026-09-13)

**Decision.** `BadgeStage` gained a `playback` prop and the open slide's clip plays on the stage — hook or content: ▶ / Space, the Studio's own `TrimBar` over the SOURCE duration, a speed pill (`CLIP_SPEEDS`), `I`/`O` at the playhead, the cut and its screen time read on the row. Since 2026-09-14 all of it lives in the deck band (above, «Aiguille»): the trim bar opens in the band with **Cut** and loops there, and ▶ otherwise plays the whole piece — the per-clip ↻ and the separate photo-hook transport are gone, one Space binding drives whichever applies.

**Why the stage drives the `<video>` and the editor owns the clock**: the decoded source IS a `<video>` element the stage holds (`loadBadgeSource`), so it is the only place `play()` can be called; but the badge's time is the editor's business. So the stage runs a rAF loop that reports `currentTime` (`onTime`) and bumps `frameSeq` per frame (a content slide's badge time never moves, so the caller's `timeSeconds` alone would repaint nothing), and the editor hands back `timeSeconds = (playhead − in) / speed` — the time into the DELIVERED stretch, exactly what `overlayClock: 'delivered'` gives the export (`media-pipeline.md`). Two traps, both measured: the seek effect must stand down while playing (`videoTimeSeconds` is then the playhead the loop itself reported a frame ago, and seeking to it drags the element back a few ms every frame), and the out point is watched on the rAF loop, never `timeupdate` (~4×/s, a quarter-second overrun).

**Paused ON the in point the badge draws settled** (and on a photo hook, paused on the slide's first moment) — the composition view the band, the PNG and every other surface show, and the one a piece opens on. Anywhere else (playing, or scrubbed away) the badge draws the real instant: that is what lets the author check an exit against the shot. Same rule for the hook THUMBNAIL: `captureThumb` refuses while the clip plays or sits away from its in point — the cache is the composition, never the frame the transport stopped on.

**Where the three numbers are written**: a drag on the bar writes the in point AND the screen time (`screenSecondsOf(range, speed)`); a speed change reads the footage from the CLAMPED range the stage shows, not the stored screen time — a hook still carrying the 5s default over a 3s clip must land on 1.5s at 2×, not 2.5 (caught in the browser: the rail said 2.5s while the readout said 1.5). `hookSeconds` is still clamped on read (`hookSecondsWithin`) and the deck's `seconds` is the stored value, so the plan can say "5s" for a 3s clip until the author touches the bar — known, and cheaper than writing the document on open; the band caps it once the stage has decoded that clip.

**Verified in headless Chromium** (scratchpad harness, VP9 clip built in-page with `VideoEncoder` + mp4-muxer, dropped on the Library zone — the INNERMOST matching div, an event on an ancestor never bubbles down): the transport appears, the stage changes while playing and stops on the out point, 2× halves the screen time and marked the cell `1.5s·2×`, keyboard cuts move both handles, the Content tab and the plan agree ("1.0s · 2× · silent"), and — with the encoders swapped to VP9 — **the piece export writes a 1.0s 360×640 MP4 whose first and last frames carry the source's colours at 0.49s and 2.5s**, i.e. the cut and the re-time reach the file. A second run took a MIXED deck through the one button: a photo hook forced to Video came out a 5s 800×1000 painted clip with the badge on it, a clip slide at 2× a 1.5s 360×450 clip, a photo slide a 1536×1920 PNG. **Driven again with real H.264 on 2026-09-13** (desktop app Browser pane, Chrome 152, `showDirectoryPicker` replaced by an in-page directory handle that keeps the written blobs): a carousel of a 4K60 clip hook, a B-framed clip at 2× and a photo forced to Video wrote three MP4s in 4 s — 5.000 s 1080×1350 with the source's AAC and the badge burned in, 1.500 s 360×450 silent (the source was 640×360; a variant never upscales), 3.000 s 1080×1350 painted. The one defect found was the pipeline's B-frame trim, fixed in `webcodecs-export.ts` (`media-pipeline.md`, «Exporting a trimmed range», point 4). **Trap for the next harness**: the Library RE-POINTS THE OPEN SLIDE when its active asset changes (by design), so a deck is built by adding a slide first and activating its picture after — activating first re-points the slide you were on.

## The export's failure modes are a person's sentences, and a codec it cannot decode is OFFERED its transcode (2026-09-13)

**From the maintainer's "j'ai l'impression que c'est cassé"**, with no repro: the pipeline was driven end to end in the container (above) and found working, so what was left was every way an export could fail on HIS machine without saying anything useful. Three fixed in `use-post-exports.ts`: (1) the hook's clip size came from the stage that showed it — a piece opened on another slide had never shown the hook, so `exportHookClip` said "still loading" forever and the piece export sized the encoder from 0×0; the size is now read from the file (`loadClipMeta`) whenever the stage has not measured it. (2) A `DecodeUnsupportedError` (DJI HEVC on a browser without it) used to be a sentence saying "transcode it first" with no transcode on the screen — a dead end; `PostExports.undecodable` now names the clip and the Export tab renders the Studio's own `TranscodeControl` for it through `useTranscode`, and every export reads `transcodeStore.get(file).file ?? file` (`deliverable`), so a clip transcoded here is what goes out from then on — the Studio's own preference, through the same file-keyed store. (3) A stale folder handle (`NotReadableError`) gets the Composer's "re-add it to the Library" sentence instead of the platform's. **How to apply**: any new export path in Road Trip routes its catch through `explainFailure` — one place for the sentences, and the `undecodable` flag is what makes the transcode appear.

## The hook leaves as a video whatever its picture (2026-09-09)

**The report that caused it**: an animated hook could only be exported as slides, so the animation never left the editor. The cause was not a lost button — `5d11245` removed a duplicate of the PNG deck's — but a path that had never existed: `exportProcessedVideo` needs decoded samples, and the Studio's photo branch delivers a JPEG, so a badge animated over a PHOTOGRAPH had nowhere to go in the whole suite.

**`exportHookStillVideo` (`hook-video-export.ts`) is that path**, and it is the same composition as everything else: `encodeFrames` (`shared/media/render-video.ts`) with **`renderBadge` as the painter**, at a clock instead of settled. The clip path is untouched. Two rules make it cheap and correct: the picture is decoded ONCE and **graded once into a bitmap** — grading per frame would be a WebGL2 render per frame for a result that cannot change, and a grader per repaint is never reclaimed — and the grade is applied at the source's own density BEFORE the crop, the same order `renderBadge` uses, so the video and the PNG are the same picture. A painted clip is silent and takes a delivered cadence, both said in the panel.

**The Export tab follows the SLIDE's medium, never the file type**: `slides[0].medium === 'video'`. A photograph with an animated badge offers the export; a clip the author set to Image does not, and says where to change it.

**`isEncodeSupported()` is not enough of a guard** — it answers "does `VideoEncoder` exist", not "can it encode H.264", and the difference is measured. The rule and both remedies are in `media-pipeline.md`, «Having a VideoEncoder is not being able to encode».

**Verified in headless Chromium** on a real trip and a real photograph: the badge draws over the picture, the stage canvas CHANGES between two clocks (the invariant the exporter rests on — a painter that does not move exports a still), the button appears with the photograph's own sentence, and pressing it reports the honest encoder refusal. **The encode itself is still unverified anywhere**: no runner here has H.264.

## The export READS the deck and delivers it, and the header carries it (2026-09-09)

**Decision.** `shared/roadtrip/export-plan.ts` turns a post into the list of files it would write — one item per slide, its medium, its seconds, its name and what would stop it — and the Export tab draws that list before anything runs. It decides nothing: every medium comes from `deckSlides`, so the panel is a reading of the composition, not a second opinion about it. `slideFileName` takes the extension, so a mixed deck writes `01-hook.mp4` beside `02.png` under one numbering — the swipe order is what makes a mixed carousel uploadable.

**The general button is back, and it is the piece's ONE primary action** — which amends rather than breaks the rule `5d11245` recorded. The amendment: *the header carries the piece's primary action; a tab's buttons are the escapes from it.* So the header's ↓ Export writes the whole deck in the formats the slides say they are, and the Export tab keeps "all slides as PNGs" and "the hook as a video" as named escapes. Pressing the header still switches to that tab, where the report is written.

**One override survives, and it is not a mode**: *Everything as images*. It is what a browser with no encoder can still do and what a contact sheet of a reel is. It changes the MEDIUM of every item and never the `reason` — the deck's own answer does not change because of how it is being written today.

**A blocked item stays visible, struck through, with its sentence.** Hiding it would answer "why is my hook not in the folder" with silence. And a clip that fails mid-run costs only itself: each is caught, the stills already rendered are still delivered, and the failures are named alongside them. **The check that a container can be demuxed applies only to a CLIP** — running `hookSourceProblem` over a photograph blocks every animated hook that sits on a still, which is the whole feature (caught by a test, not by reading).

## The QR is generated here, and it is verified by decoding (2026-08-24)

**Decision.** `shared/lib/qr.ts` is a hand-rolled encoder (byte mode, EC level M, versions 1–10). **Why not a library**: a card that fetched its own QR from a service would be the single place this suite phoned home, and the local-first line is the product. It is ~250 lines against a spec that has not moved since 2000 — unlike Dexie, it earns the code it costs.

**How it was made trustworthy, and how to keep it so**: the matrices are dumped from a throwaway vitest spec and decoded by `jsqr` installed in the SCRATCHPAD, never in the repo. 15 payloads across versions 1–10, UTF-8 accents and the 213-byte ceiling all round-trip, and the rendered CTA canvas itself is scanned back. Re-run that loop after any change to the encoder — unit tests alone cannot tell you a QR scans.

**The structural tests caught what decoding could not.** Reserving the format strip wrote over `(6,8)` and `(8,6)`, punching two holes in the timing lines a decoder uses to find the module grid; error correction hid it and jsqr still read the code. Index 6 is skipped on purpose now. **The lesson**: for a format with redundancy, a passing round trip is not proof of correctness.

**Refusing is part of the contract**: a link past 213 bytes returns null with a reason rather than a code that scans to half a URL. **Drawing**: modules are snapped to whole pixels and a four-module quiet zone is painted, because a QR on half pixels antialiases grey and one without a quiet zone is unreadable against a dark photograph.

## The overlay engine never wraps, so a sentence must be wrapped for it (2026-08-24)

**Measured**: the CTA's body ran off both edges of the frame. `drawOverlays` draws a text element as ONE line by design — right for a readout, wrong for a sentence. `shared/lib/wrap-text.ts` wraps onto a character BUDGET rather than a measured width, so it stays pure and testable; `charBudget` deliberately assumes 0.55 em per character against a real average nearer 0.5, because wrapping a word early is invisible and wrapping one late loses the words. Explicit newlines are kept, an over-long single word is left whole. **How to apply**: any new roadtrip text that is a sentence rather than a value goes through it, and the caption block grows UPWARD from its foot so two lines do not fall off the bottom.

## The default badge look is `neutral`, and that was measured (2026-08-23)

**Decision.** A new trip adopts the `neutral` preset (white, drop shadow). The first cut adopted `plein-cadre` on the reasoning that a badge is a signature — **wrong in the browser**: flat vermilion over warm footage all but vanishes, and warm footage is most of a desert road trip. A badge lands on a photograph nobody has vetted, unlike a studio overlay whose author is watching the clip; the default has to survive that.

**The signature comes from the theme being per TRIP, not from which preset it is.** Pick Or ciné or Pixel CRT once and every badge of the trip wears it — that is what makes a post recognisable in a feed out of order, which is the whole strategy. Per-post styling was rejected for exactly that reason.

**Preview and export are the same code at two sizes** (`badge-render.ts`): everything the render needs is a fraction of the frame, so only the canvas differs. Do not let them diverge — a badge that looks right at 480 px and lands differently at 1920 px is worse than no preview.

## The trip's name is never displaced, and the WHEN line is its own piece (2026-08-24)

The kicker is the trip's name, full stop. Adding the piece needed no document version — `pieceStyles` and `textOverrides` are `Partial` records.

## Never offer a fabricated example — show the real line or the reason (2026-08-24)

**Measured complaint**: three of the four counter modes "didn't work". They worked; they fell back to the day of the trip **in silence**, so clicking one changed nothing and said nothing — and the hints beside them ("Kalbarri · 2 · of 3") were invented values for a trip with no such stage. `post.endDate` had no editor at all, which is why "Range of days" could never differ from "Day of trip". Before adding an option that reads a field, check something can WRITE it (the same lesson the stages editor taught).

## The day a piece tells is measured from the picture (rev. 2026-09-07)

Every number the badge draws is a subtraction from `post.date`, so a picture filed under the wrong day reads confidently wrong — a Brittany 2026 photo dropped into an Australia 2025 trip renders "day 261 of 310" and "9 months ago", both correct arithmetic about a day the picture has nothing to do with. `media-date.ts` reads EXIF `DateTimeOriginal`, **then the capture time the source vouched for** (`MediaOrigin.exif`, 2026-09-07), and only then falls back to `File.lastModified` — each **labelled**, because a copy or an export rewrites a file date and an instance that found no EXIF stores the mtime in the same column. The editor shows the answer, offers to file the piece under it and calls out a date outside the trip's span. **It never rewrites a post on its own** — measuring is the tool's job, filing is the author's.

**Why the middle rung exists**: a Winnow photo proxy is a WebP re-encode with no EXIF at all, so every fetched picture used to fall through to its `lastModified` and be called a weak guess — while the camera's own answer sat one field away, and while `isoFromTimestamp` reads that instant in the READER's timezone, which can name the wrong calendar day outright for anyone not near UTC. Nothing else in the app filters on a modification date: the Library's Winnow tab, `DayFromWinnow` and the browser all send `date_from`/`date_to`, which Winnow answers on `capture_date` — `(captured_at AT TIME ZONE 'UTC')::date`, and `captured_at` is exiftool's `DateTimeOriginal` with mtime as ITS own last resort (`lib/extract.ts`, `lib/indexer.ts`). Checked against Winnow's source 2026-09-07; do not "fix" the span queries.

The EXIF date is read as written, never converted: EXIF has no timezone and the day a photo belongs to is the day it was where it was taken. That is the one deliberate exception to the trip's UTC rule.

**`exif-parser.ts` moved to `shared/exif/`** when Road Trip became its second consumer — a tool never reaches into another tool (the same move `StylePanel` made).

## Scrubbing seeks the open clip; it never re-decodes it (2026-08-24)

**The trap, measured**: `BadgeStage`'s decode effect listed `videoTimeSeconds`, so every nudge of the frame picker tore the `<video>` down (`removeAttribute('src')`, `load()`, `revokeObjectURL`) and built a new one. Choosing a hook frame stuttered and flashed "decoding…" the whole way across. `BadgeSource` now carries an optional `seek(seconds)`; the decode effect depends on the FILE alone and a second effect seeks the element that is already open.

**The paint has to wait for the frame.** Repainting on `videoTimeSeconds` would draw the OLD frame, since the seek has not landed. A `frameSeq` counter is bumped when the seek resolves and the paint depends on that. **Always clamp a seek short of the end**: past the last frame `seeked` never fires, and the preview simply stops updating.

## The hook's frame is chosen on a filmstrip, not a number (2026-08-24)

A slider makes you scrub blind — nudge, look up, nudge again. `FrameStrip` draws the clip as a strip of stills and you point at the moment (`filmstrip.ts` is the pure half: how many cells, which moments, where a time sits).

**A cell is sampled at the MIDDLE of its slice**, never its left edge: the edge puts cell one on frame zero, which on a drone clip is the props spinning up, and leaves the last slice unrepresented. **Frames arrive through `onFrame` one at a time** so the strip fills in instead of staying blank until the last decode lands, and **one video element is seeked from moment to moment** — one element per thumbnail is how a dozen frames become a second of stutter. **The drag is throttled to one change per animation frame**, the rate the preview can repaint; per pointer event queues seeks the element never catches up with. Every thumbnail blob URL is revoked on unmount.

## Where you are lives in the ROUTE (2026-08-24)

`#/roadtrip/<trip>/<day>/<piece>`, any tail of which may be absent (`trip-route.ts`, pure and tested). It was component state, and coming back from a piece dropped you on the trip's FIRST day — a real loss on a 300-day trip, every time. The route also survives a reload and makes a day linkable.

The trip's part is `<slug>-<first 8 of its id>` (`australia-d1060760`): the slug is there to be read and is ignored on resolve, so renaming a trip cannot break a link and two trips called "Australia" stay distinguishable. A malformed day drops itself AND everything after it — half a route (a piece under a nonexistent day) is worse than none.

## A panel that belongs to the PIECE must not be hidden behind a slide (2026-08-24)

The Studio bridge and the per-kind defaults were rendered inside the hook-only branch, so opening a carousel's second slide made them vanish — and the maintainer reported both features as missing. **How to apply**: before putting a control inside `isHook`, ask whether it is about the SLIDE (its picture, its caption, the badge's own styling) or about the PIECE (its name, its link, the trip's defaults). Only the first belongs there.

## The whole row opens the piece (2026-08-24, rev. 2026-08-25)

A list you sweep through should not make you aim at a 38px thumbnail or a small button. The row is a `div` with `role="button"` (a real `<button>` cannot contain the buttons the row already holds), and **every control inside it stops propagation and prevents default** — without that, marking a piece published also opened the editor and the action was lost behind the screen change. Duplicating carries the look and the slides but never the publication, the Studio link, or any id (piece, slide or shade): two documents sharing an id is how a list starts editing the wrong row.

**The secondary actions are ICON buttons, opening is a filled pill** (maintainer ask, 2026-08-25): ⧉ duplicate · ✓ publish-toggle (accent-washed while published) · × delete, each a small round glyph button carrying its full name in `title`/`aria-label`, plus an ink "Open" pill — three words of button chrome per row drowned the titles the list exists to scan. Delete keeps its inline two-step confirm; only the trigger shrank. The pill does not replace the whole-row gesture, it makes it visible — and it stops propagation like everything else, or `onOpen` fires twice through the bubble. Glyphs are DOM text (full font-fallback chain), so the canvas glyph rule above does not bind here.

**Trap**: creating a project with a media ref but no directory handle greets a brand-new project with "1 media file not in this folder" — `reconcileMedia` runs whenever `media.files` is non-empty and finds nothing. ## One stack of SHADES, not a vignette control and a scrim control (2026-08-24)

**Decision, with the maintainer.** The two were the same thing seen twice — a gradient of some colour, anchored somewhere, reaching some distance — and keeping them apart cost every combination that actually comes up (a wash from the left AND a vignette; a band clear at the top edge and dark at mid-frame) and left the vignette locked to black. `shades.ts` replaces both: up to four layers, each a DIRECTION (four edges, four corners, two middle bands, radial), a reach, a strength, a colour, an `invert` flag, and `followHook`.

**`invert` is not "pick the opposite edge".** `top` reaching 0.5 inverted is clear at the top and dark at mid-frame; no un-inverted shade draws that. It is the case the maintainer asked for by name — a portrait frame whose text sits in the middle.

**`followHook` keeps the old "under the hook" behaviour**: a linear shade lands on the badge block's own edge (with a margin of the block's height, so the fade clears the first line), a radial centres on the block. A scrim that moves with the text it protects beats one placed by eye, which is why it survived the merge.

**Trap, measured in the browser**: a middle band drawn as centre→edge blacks out the whole far half — a canvas gradient holds its end colour past its endpoints. A band must run EDGE TO EDGE with the peak at 0.5 (`stopsFor(..., mirrored)`). The unit tests assert the stop shape, and the browser check samples five points of the real canvas per direction; the pure geometry alone would not have caught it.

**The direction is a 3×3 GRID, not a list, and it grew corners (2026-09-14).** The maintainer found the eleven-sentence select had to be read every time and asked for the anchor grid's shape. Each cell is a CSS-gradient sketch of what it draws (`GLYPHS` in `ShadesPanel`); the centre holds THREE shapes (radial + the two bands, which cross the frame and fit no cell), offered in a column beside the grid only while the centre is the cell. **Multi-select was weighed and declined**: most of the 512 combinations mean nothing, and every one that does is already a band, an inverted band or two shades. A corner is a RADIAL centred on the corner (the maintainer's pick over a diagonal linear, which draws a straight edge across the frame), radius `reach × 1.2` of the shorter side. The four ids were added to `ShadeDirection`, so no stored document changed. `SHADE_GRID` is the one table of cell ↔ shapes.

**Follow badge is a three-way choice, No · Edge · Anchor** — the maintainer could not choose between "always follow" and "only when asked", so the author picks. Stored as the old `followHook` plus an optional `followAnchor` (absent = off, the `enabled` rule above); `shadeFollow`/`followFlags` are the only readers/writers. *Anchor* resolves the direction from `HookBlock.anchor` (which `badgeBlockExtent` now carries) through `directionInCell`, keeping `shade.direction` UNDERNEATH so switching off returns the hand-picked cell, and a centre shape survives a centred badge. It follows the ANCHOR, not the dragged x/y: a drag keeps the anchor. Picking a cell by hand while anchored drops to *Edge*. The reach slider is dead only where the block really sets it (`reachFollowsBadge`: top/bottom) — the block is measured vertically only, so sides and corners keep their slider.

`shadeGradient` returns the gradient in FRACTIONS of the frame (radii against the SHORTER side, so a radial stays a circle on 9:16) and `paintShades` is a dumb translation into pixels — the same split that keeps the burn-in and the PNG identical.

**A shade has an `enabled` bypass, same as a LUT layer (2026-09-14).** The maintainer wanted to A/B a shade against the preview without deleting and re-adding it — the round trip `GradePanel` already gives a look. `Shade.enabled` is OPTIONAL and absent means ON — read it `enabled !== false`, never `!enabled`: the first version made it required and tested `!shade.enabled`, which silently blanked every shade stored before the switch existed (rev. 2026-09-14). Any field added to a stored `Shade` follows the same rule. `shadeGradient` returns null when off, so the one call site (`paintShades`) needs no change and preview/export stay identical. `ShadesPanel` mirrors `GradePanel`'s row exactly: a `ToggleField` first in the header, the row's left border and opacity drop when off, the strength/reach sliders (not the other fields — `GradePanel` doesn't grey those either) get `disabled`.

## Never read a canvas's size before an await and draw after it (2026-08-24)

**Measured**: a miniature badge stayed burnt into the corner of the stage. `renderBadge` read `canvas.width/height`, awaited the fonts, and drew — while a newer render had resized the canvas in between. The stale call painted at the OLD scale over the new frame. The trigger was `BadgeStage`'s decode effect painting an empty frame into a canvas it never sized (300×150, the element default), but any caller could have caused it.

**How to apply**: the font wait is now the FIRST thing `renderBadge` does; everything after it is synchronous, so two overlapping renders each draw a complete, self-consistent frame and the last one simply wins. The general rule: in an async paint, read the surface's dimensions AFTER the last await, never before.

## The grid's hover card, the panel's own scroll, the day's thumbnails (2026-08-24)

Three asks from the same session, one theme — the tool has to be readable at a glance.

**The hover card is ours, not `title`.** The native tooltip waits about a second on the first cell, which is useless on a grid meant to be *swept*, and it cannot show the kinds. `DayCard` is `position: fixed` with coordinates from the cell's `getBoundingClientRect()`, clamped to the viewport and `pointer-events: none`. Fixed rather than absolute on purpose: the grid scrolls sideways inside its own `overflow-x-auto` box, so an absolutely-positioned card would be clipped by it or drift with its scroll. `aria-label` still carries the same sentence — the card is decoration, the label is the accessible name. **It names the LEG the day belongs to** (2026-09-08, `DayStage` → `stageOf`, one callback for the tint and the name rather than two over the same lookup): `"Kalbarri · day 2/3"` with the stage's tint as a dot, so the card and the cell's foot-stripe read as the same leg. A stage that names nothing shows the rank alone — never an invented place, the `stageLabel` rule — and a day no leg covers shows no line at all.

**The stage takes the height it is given** (`flex-1 min-h-0` down the column, `max-h-full` on the canvas — both max constraints apply, and a replaced element honours them proportionally, so it fills the box without distorting). The 62vh cap is kept only for the STACKED layout, where the page scrolls and an unbounded picture would push the transport off a phone. **The editor's panel scrolls by itself above 860px** (`overflow-y-auto` + `min-h-0` on the column, `overflow-hidden` on the section) so the badge stays in view while the folds are worked through — the studio's layout, same reason. **Below 860px the page scrolls as one**: a panel with its own scrollbar inside a scrolling page is a trap on a phone. Both breakpoints are CONTAINER queries (`@min-[860px]`), never viewport ones — the Library sidebar eats 288px the viewport cannot see.

**Each post keeps a thumbnail of its hook**, in a second object store (`thumbs`) of the roadtrip database, keyed by post id — apart from the documents because they are the only heavy values and a trip doc is read on every gallery render. It is a picture of the BADGE, not of the raw media: what you need to recognise a day you last touched in March is what you already made of it. **It is drawn at the piece's own orientation** — fixed height, natural width, no letterbox and no crop — because the shape of a piece is half of what identifies it in the list; a fixed box cropping 16:9 and 9:16 to the same rectangle hid the one thing the picture was for. The stage takes it (`BadgeStage.onRendered` → `canvasThumbnail`), debounced 700ms — the stage repaints on every animation frame of the badge's transport, and writing each one would be an IndexedDB write per frame. It is a cache: losing it costs a row its picture, never the post. **Prune on delete** (`deleteThumbs`), for a post and for a whole trip; nothing else ever will.

## The hook burns in through the Studio's pipeline, not a second exporter (2026-08-24)

**Decision.** "Export hook video" hands the badge's own `OverlayElement[]` to `exportVariantVideo` — the Studio's WebCodecs export — with no LUT and no cues. Nothing about decoding, cover-cropping, cadence or muxing is re-implemented; `shared/roadtrip/hook-video.ts` only decides WHICH slice goes out and under what name, and `hook-video-export.ts` makes the call (same pure/DOM split as `deck.ts` / `deck-export.ts`).

**The clip starts on the frame the author scrubbed to**, as a trim in point. That is not a convenience: `DrawOptions.originSeconds` is the trim's start, so the badge's windows count from the first EXPORTED frame and the entrance lands on frame one. Export the whole rush instead and the entrance has already happened somewhere in the middle. `hookRange` returns null when the whole clip goes out, which the pipeline reads as "no trim" and origin 0 — correct, not a fallback.

**The scrim and vignette needed a hook in the shared pipeline.** They are painted between the picture and the overlays, and `exportVariantVideo` had nowhere to do that, so `VariantRenderOptions` gained `paintUnderOverlays?(ctx, w, h)` (unset by the Studio) and `paintBackdrop` was exported from `badge-render.ts` with a context type widened to include `OffscreenCanvasRenderingContext2D`. A gradient that showed in the PNG and vanished in the reel would be a different picture.

**The hook's LENGTH is session state, not part of the document.** It is derived from the badge's own hold (`defaultHookSeconds` = duration + a beat) and clamped to the clip in hand by `hookSecondsWithin`, so the read-out never claims 5s over a 3s clip. A length is an export choice; storing it would have cost a document version for nothing.

**MP4 and MOV only, said in words.** The pipeline demuxes MP4 boxes; a WebM produced mp4box's own "Invalid data found while parsing box of type…", accurate and unreadable. `hookSourceProblem` refuses first with a sentence naming the file and reminding that the PNG export still works. **Unverified end to end**: this container's Chromium reports `avc1.*` unsupported by `VideoEncoder` (vp8/vp9 only), so no H.264 encode can be driven here — the UI, the guard and the arithmetic were checked in the browser, the encode was not.

## The trip file is a BACKUP, not a template (2026-08-31)

**Decision.** `shared/roadtrip/trip-file.ts` writes a whole trip to `.roadtrip.json`, and it deliberately does **not** copy the studio's split. A project file carries a *portable half* — a template you can mail. A trip has no such half: its stages, the days it has told, the words its badges say and the look they wear ARE the trip. So the file carries everything except what is meaningless in another browser: the trip `id` (a fresh one is minted, so importing the same file twice gives two trips instead of silently overwriting one), the timestamps, and each post's `projectId`, which addresses a Studio project in *this* store and would otherwise dangle as an "Open in Studio" that opens nothing. Post thumbnails live in their own IndexedDB store and are re-baked from the preview, so they never travel.

**Media refs DO travel**, and that is the point: since they carry a content hash (`studio.md`, «Media identity»), a trip opened elsewhere finds its pictures again in a folder whose files have been renamed or re-graded. This is how a trip crosses from one source to another (`docs/winnow-bridge.md`, invariant 2).

**How to apply.**
- Everything else follows `projects/project-file.ts` exactly: a `kind` marker so a stray `.json` is rejected, `{ ok: false, error }` parsing that never throws and always says something actionable, and a file from a **newer** version refused rather than half-read.
- Reading replays `migrateTripDoc` over a document built from `createTripDoc`, so an older file lands on the current shape exactly as an older stored trip does, and anything a past version never wrote gets the same default a new trip gets. Add a field to `TripDoc` → it is carried and defaulted by that path, with nothing to edit here.
- `projectId` is stripped **twice** — writing and reading — because a hand-edited file could smuggle one in.
- Import always creates a NEW trip. Merging two trips is not offered, on purpose.
- Export sits on the gallery card, import in the gallery header — the same places the studio puts them.

## The mirror's bookkeeping is a reducer beside the document, never on it (2026-09-06)

**Decision.** A remote trip's distance from the authority lives in a `SyncRecord` (`sources/doc-sync.ts` — moved out of Road Trip when the Studio became its second consumer — IndexedDB store `sync`, DB v3) — etag, `dirtyAt`, status, the server's copy behind a conflict — and every transition is `reduceSync(record, event)`, pure and tested state by state. **Why beside**: on `TripDoc` it would leak into `.roadtrip.json` and onto the wire; a sibling row keyed by the trip id costs nothing. **Why durable**: `dirtyAt` surviving a closed tab is the entire crash story — the next open on that device pushes the edit; there is no `keepalive` fetch on unload (64 KB body cap, a trip can exceed it).

**Rules the reducer encodes, so a driver never re-decides them**: the idle clock counts from the LAST edit; an edit during an in-flight push leaves the record dirty after `pushOk` (`pushStartedAt` is what tells); `offline` and `unauthenticated` retry on every trigger (only trying can tell the network or the session is back); `forbidden`, `conflict` and `gone` HOLD until a person acts and an edit does not move them; a `protocol` failure keeps the edit dirty with the message. `pillText` is the honesty rule again — every status yields the sentence the pill prints, with the conflict's time in the reader's own clock. The client side is `WinnowClient.listDocs/getDoc/putDoc/deleteDoc` (`architecture.md`, «Remote sources»): 304 is an answer, 404 is `notfound` (never forbidden — a foreign row's existence is not revealed), 412 is `conflict` carrying the server's etag, and the body cap is checked BEFORE a PUT so an oversize trip is refused with a sentence rather than a 413.

## Local now, remote on idle — and every state is a sentence (2026-09-06)

**Decision (D1–D4 of `docs/roadtrip-persistence.md`, taken by the maintainer, not to be re-opened).** The 800 ms IndexedDB write is untouched; a remote trip additionally pushes its mirror after 5 s of quiet, on `visibilitychange: hidden`, in the unmount flush, and on "Save now" (`RoadTripTool.tsx`, `remoteFlush`). The remote copy is the authority and the mirror is its cache; opening a remote trip opens the mirror at once and `GET`s with `If-None-Match` — 304 nothing, 200 + clean mirror → replaced silently, 200 + dirty mirror → `conflict` and the pill offers **Keep mine** (re-PUT over the server's etag) / **Take theirs** (two-step, drops local edits); unreachable → the mirror opens and the pill says offline. That IS the resume workflow. A 404 on push is `gone` with **Keep here as local** / **Delete here**. The bucket is generic (`kind: trip | project`), trips first; thumbnails never cross (re-baked from the preview); writes are own-docs-any-role on Winnow, foreign rows 404.

**Rules learnt wiring it**: the tool reduces `pushStarted` / the outcome around `pushOnce` ITSELF and reduces the LIVE record after the request (an edit can land while the PUT is out, and only the live record's `dirtyAt` knows — `pushTrip` is for callers with no live state: create, import, move); the record is written by one function (`setRecord`: ref + state + store) so timers, unmount and the pill never disagree; a remote creation or import pushes FIRST and keeps nothing here if the instance refused (one gesture, one request, the result said); a remote delete carries `If-Match` and is refused while unreachable — there are no tombstones; a move writes the target and waits for the acknowledgement before deleting at the origin, keeps the id so `tripRef` links survive, and goes through the portable file so `projectId`s are nulled (projects do not cross). What leaves is the document minus `sourceId`; what comes back is stamped with the id and source of the REQUEST and migrated like a stored trip. The pill is one component (`SyncPill`) rendered by the shell into a `headerExtra` slot of both the overview and the piece editor, so the two screens read the same record.

**SHIPPED and confirmed working by the maintainer on the deployed pair (2026-09-07).** Before that, verified end to end against a REAL Winnow, its migration 0041 applied by the stack's own runner and its routes answering: create-on-remote (preflight 204 → PUT 201, the row in Postgres with `sourceId` absent from the stored document, as the portable split requires), re-open (GET `If-None-Match` → 304), an edit made elsewhere (push → 412, pill «refused: changed on another device at HH:MM»), **Keep mine** (re-PUT over the server's etag, checked in the table), the row deleted elsewhere (push → 404, pill «deleted on <host>»), and **Keep here as local** (the trip falls back to `local` losing nothing). Ownership holds: another account's row answers **404** on GET and on PUT, never 412, and the list shows only your own. The refusals answer 415 / 400 / 413 / 412 as designed. **The deployed pair was never needed** — two localhosts are same-site, so the cookie travels; the recipe is in `testing.md`. **Still unverified**: offline and 401 mid-edit, and «Take theirs».

**The trap that hides a shipped capability: the capabilities sheet is a SNAPSHOT taken at connect time and nothing refreshes it** (`client.capabilities()` has exactly one caller, `ConnectScreen`). A browser that connected before the instance grew the document bucket goes on believing it has none, so the «Keep on» picker, «Move to» and the remote list all stay hidden and Atelier tries nothing — a shipped feature that looks unshipped, on every device that connected early. The cure is the `reconnect` link in the media browser's header, which re-runs `#/connect` and replaces the stored sheet. **How to apply**: any feature gated on a capability needs that sentence in its release note, and a capability that turns on later is a reason to re-read the sheet, not to assume the stored one is current.

## The overview's counts are ONE ROW on a phone (2026-09-10)

**A figure block laid out to breathe does not shrink on a phone, it WRAPS** — and wrapping is what costs the fold. The five counts sat in a `gap-x-10` flex row whose labels are whole phrases ("days on the road", "days told · N left"); at 390px that is three rows plus `p-5`, and the calendar the screen exists for started below it. Shortening each label to one word and forcing a `grid-cols-3` still left two rows and 102px, and the maintainer's answer to that was "still too big — you can even make it scrollable horizontally".

**So: one row, `overflow-x-auto`, one `flex-none` figure per count — 47px, measured.** It costs one line whatever a trip's numbers turn out to be, where a grid's height is decided by how many figures there happen to be. All six fit at 360px and it scrolls at 320; when it does, the figure cut off at the right edge is what says there are more, which no wrapped grid can say. Read along a row, "days told · N left" is two figures pretending to be one, so the compact list is a DIFFERENT list and not the same one abbreviated: days · told · left · pieces · published · silence. Nothing changes above 820px, where the phrases are the better label and there is room for them.

**Two traps, both worth the measurement.** A flex child whose `overflow` is other than `visible` has an automatic minimum size of **zero**, so the moment this row could scroll sideways the column above crushed it to 18px with the numbers sheared off at the waist — it needs `flex-none`. And it must declare **no `touch-action` at all**: the rule elsewhere (`frontend.md`) is about a surface that WRITES a drag, while this is an ordinary scroll box where the browser already does both axes, and `pan-x` would not add horizontal scrolling — it would remove the vertical swipe that scrolls the page under a 47px strip. The end padding of a flex scroll container IS honoured (13px of paper after the last figure, scrolled fully right), so no trailing spacer is needed.

## The overview's two zones zoom — and they are the ONLY ones (2026-09-08, rev. 2026-09-10)

**Superseded 2026-09-13**: both pills are gone — the heatmap fits its box and the loupe windows the ruler (the L1 entry above). What survives of this entry is the rule that a media PREVIEW never carries a view zoom, and the traps `useStageZoom` still carries for the lightbox.

**Decision.** The day grid and the stage ruler each carry a `StageZoomControl` (`shared/ui/`, see `frontend.md` for the primitive and its traps), because both are day-sized zones that are either too dense to aim at or too wide to see. **They are what the pill was ever for**: the same control briefly sat over the piece editor's badge stage and the Studio's canvas, and was taken back out on 2026-09-10 — over a media preview it fought the picture's own framing gestures. Never add one to a preview. What the scale multiplies is the zone's own unit, not a transform: the heatmap rounds `CELL`/`GAP` to whole pixels at every scale so the cells stay on one lattice, and the ruler multiplies the fitted `width / total` day width while keeping the 6px `MIN_DAY` floor — a bar thinner than that cannot be dragged. Both already scrolled sideways in their own box, so nothing else changed; drags keep working because the ruler measures the pointer through the track's rect and the day width it just drew with.

**Each zone declares its geometry as a pure function of the scale** — `rulerDayWidth`/`rulerTrackWidth` in `stage-ruler.ts`, `heatmapColumn`/`heatmapWidth` in the new `day-grid.ts` — because the zoom needs to ask "how wide would you be at this scale?" without a layout, twice: to know how far out it may go, and how much the content really grew. The ruler reads its box from `zoom.viewport` rather than measuring it a second time; two measurements of one box is how the drawn day width and the zoom's floor start disagreeing.

**`MIN_DAY` is the ruler's BASE, not a clamp after the zoom** (2026-09-08). Clamping after froze the whole track: a 616-day trip in a 460px box drew the same 3696px picture from 25% to 800%, so zooming in did nothing but yank the scroll. The day width is now `max(6px, box/total) × scale`, strictly proportional, and the panel floors the zoom at **100%** — a day is therefore never drawn under 6px, a leg can always be grabbed by an edge, and on a trip too long for the box 100% simply scrolls. `−` greys at 100% on the ruler, which is the honest face of it.

**The ruler's scale steps with the day width it actually draws** (2026-09-12). Month labels alone never said how wide a day IS, so the track carries day strokes under its axis rule: every day while a day is at least `MIN_TICK_GAP` (9px) wide, real Mondays alone below that, nothing at all if even a week would crowd — `rulerTicks(trip, dayWidth)`, pure and tested. Mondays and firsts of the month are the taller stroke, so a dense comb still has a rhythm to read against. The step is chosen from the DRAWN day width, never from the zoom percentage: at 100% a long trip sits at the 6px floor and gets weeks, while a short trip fits and gets days at the same percentage. Weeks are real Mondays rather than every seventh day of the trip — a rhythm anchored on the trip's arbitrary first day means nothing.

**A bare wheel zooms them** (`wheel: 'any'`): the maintainer asked for it directly, and neither zone has another use for the wheel. Horizontal panning is what survives — shift-wheel and a sideways swipe still scroll the track.

**Where the control sits (rev. 2026-09-08).** Neither zone pays a row for it: the heatmap's rides the legend row it already had (`ml-auto`), and the ruler's sits in `StagesPanel`'s header beside "+ Stage" — which is why the zoom is created by the PANEL and handed to `StageRuler` as a prop. A row of its own under the track was the first shape and the maintainer rejected it. `StageRuler` uses the handed-down `zoom.viewportRef` AS its scroller ref rather than keeping a second one: one element, measured for the fitted day width and scrolled by the zoom. That header row's controls all stand `h-[2.125rem]` (34px, the zoom pill's own height), and "+ Stage" is the gallery's ink-filled primary at header size rather than another bordered pill — adding a leg is the panel's one creative act and must not read as a status chip.

## The hook is a VARIANT now, and the contract is three rules (2026-09-13)

**Decision, with the maintainer.** A piece's opener is no longer only the six-piece badge: `shared/roadtrip/hooks/` holds a registry of hook variants, `PostBadge.hook` (v15) names which one draws, and `resolveHook` folds them into one reader the stage, the PNG deck and (later) the clip export all paint. The exemplar that drove it is the **scrub** — the trip's measuring tape sweeping to the day being told, flashing that day's pictures, ticking — chosen by the maintainer out of four explored directions. The whole design, the sound path and the seven phases are `docs/hook-engine.md`; what follows is only what the code cannot say.

**`prepare()` returns a CLOSURE, never a plan object.** The stop list, the projection, the timings are computed once and captured; `content`, `paint` and `score` read them by construction. Handing a plan around would let one of the three recompute it and drift — which a sound bed cannot survive — and it also removes every generic from the registry. **How to apply**: a variant that needs a value in two of the three computes it in `prepare` and closes over it, never in the callbacks.

**Three decisions taken before the first line, each cheap now and expensive later.** (1) A variant rewrites the TEXT of named pieces (`content(t): Partial<Record<BadgePiece, string|null>>`, merged over what `badgeContent` computed) and never builds `OverlayElement[]` — so `badgeElements` stays the single builder and the theme, ratios, deterministic ids and hit-testing keep working whatever draws; the headline may be rewritten but never hidden. (2) `owns: 'frame' | 'layer'` is declared from the start even with one layer: a scrub IS the picture while it sweeps, a route trace draws over it, and that is the only conflict a stack can ever produce. (3) The field is stored as a **list** with one entry, so the stack arrives as UI with no second migration on documents remote trips already carry. An unknown id is **skipped** and the badge stands in — a trip written by a newer build opens here and loses its opener, never fails to open.

**A field on `PostBadge` needs no fifth site**: `portablePost` deep-clones the whole post, so it travels in and out of `.roadtrip.json` by itself. The four-site rule (`ProjectPortable` and friends, `studio.md`) bites fields on the DOCUMENT, not on a post.

**What phase 1 deliberately did NOT wire: `content()`.** Both call sites (`slide-render.ts`, `PostEditor.tsx`) `useMemo` the elements on a stable `content`, and making that depend on the transport's clock would rebuild every element sixty times a second for a variant that rewrites nothing. The answer when the scrub needs it is to move the element build INTO the render — `renderBadge` taking `content` + `layout` rather than finished `elements` — not to add a time dependency to the React memo. The paint seam IS wired (`RenderBadgeOptions.hook`, painted between the picture and the shades), because it was already per-frame.

**The picker (2026-09-13) — two departures from the brief's first draft, both deliberate.** (1) A card's box holds the variant's `Sketch`, a drawing of what the variant DOES, and **not a render of this piece**: the stage beside the picker already shows the real thing, and a live render per card costs a decode and a WebGL context each to repeat it worse. (2) **No second picker for the trip-wide default**: ⚙ Trip → New pieces already saves the whole look through `hookDefaultsFrom`, which carries `hook` — two places to choose the same thing is the fault this tool keeps removing. The picker sits at the top of the Look tab, OUTSIDE the hook branch's other controls, because the opener decides what the hook is and the title style below it belongs to every slide. Its two writers replace the first layer only, so a stored stack survives a card click.

**Défilé (2026-09-13) — the traps a moving opener exposed.** A hook can now move WITHOUT an animated badge piece, and three places assumed the opposite; each showed up as a visible bug on the first render, and each is now the rule. (1) `useBadgeClock` takes the opener's seconds: without them the transport never started and the stage sat forever on the sweep's first, dark frame. (2) The hook thumbnail is never captured while the transport plays — a scrub caught mid-sweep stores ANOTHER day's picture as this piece's hook, and that thumbnail is what the gallery cover draws. (3) `deckSlides` resolves `auto` through `hookMoves`, measured by preparing the hook: a scrub on day 1 plays nothing and stays an image. **How to apply**: anything that asks "does this hook move / when is it at rest?" reads `ResolvedHook.seconds` beside `badgeSettleSeconds`, never the piece styles alone.

**An untold day goes dark during the sweep.** Not a stand-in and not the hero shown early: an empty day looks empty. The piece being composed never counts as telling its own day. The numeral steps only under the `day` counter; under any other it would be a fabricated reading.

**Défilé flashes SOURCE pictures, and the author can pick them (2026-09-14, reverses "the flashes are the thumbs store").** The maintainer's report on the first build: selection rules the maintainer could not read (two "Day 1 / Day 21" selects, a Sampled/Chosen switch whose tiles toggled only in one mode) and, worse, flashes that were the thumbs store's 640px hook JPEGs with OTHER pieces' badges burned in — *"ce n'est pas du tout ce que j'avais en tête"*. **Rejected, so it is not re-proposed**: any derived picture (the thumbs store, a composed hook) as a flash source — "already graded, one read" was the whole argument and it produced the wrong picture. **Now**: `stopsOn: 'pieces' | 'picked'`; the pieces' days flash the ref each standing piece is composed over; picked pictures are refs + capture day stored in the options, chosen in `HookPicturesModal` the way the maintainer described it — a span, everything shot in it (Library + instance) ticked, untick the rest. Loader rules (`use-hook-pictures.ts`): Library by name/hash, else the instance's proxy fetched WITHOUT entering the pool, cropped to the frame at decode, one 32 MP budget shared by the set, graded with `composeWith(null)` so the flashes match the piece. Design and the rest of the rules: `docs/hook-engine.md` §9. **How to apply**: a variant that wants other pictures asks by REF through `wantsPictures`, never by day or post; a panel that needs a shell service gets it through `HookPanelHost`, never by importing from `tools/`. Verified in the Browser pane with ten generated Library photos (chooser at 1400 and 390 wide, untick/reopen/look, eight distinct pictures read back off the stage canvas in shot order); **not** exercised: the Winnow path, a piece's clip frame, an export carrying picked pictures, memory on a phone.

**`content()` reached the renderer as an optional `elementsAt(t)`**, not by moving the element build into `renderBadge` (the brief's first idea): `renderBadge`, `measureBadge`, `exportVariantVideo` and the still painter each fall back to `elements`, and callers build the function only when `ResolvedHook.rewrites`. The shared pipeline's `paintUnderOverlays` gained the clip-relative time as a fourth argument — additive, the Studio passes nothing.

**A `<style>` inside a React component leaks into the button's `textContent`**: the picker card's sketch put its keyframes inline and the card's text read "@keyframes scrub-sketch…". Keyframes belong in `src/index.css`, beside the others.

**Défilé ticks (2026-09-13).** The scrub's score is a detent per landing, a `leg` voice on a day a leg starts (the only sound carrying meaning, so the only different one) and a `seat` on today — read off the same stops as the head, so the ticks cannot leave their frames. The `sound` option defaults ON because the variant is chosen deliberately, and it reaches every video the hook makes: a still's and a clip recorded without sound take the ticks as their track, while a clip WITH sound keeps it bit-for-bit unless the author ticks "Mix them into a clip's own sound" (`mixWithClip`, off by default, hidden while the ticks are off) — `media-pipeline.md` for the plan. In the editor the ticks play through a live `AudioContext` behind a speaker toggle in the transport, OFF on every visit — a panel that ticks while its sliders are dragged is unusable. The toggle only shows when the opener scores anything.

**The route trace (2026-09-13) is RETIRED (2026-09-15), and what it proved is why the Itinerary may pin a point.** The Itinerary replaces it — the maintainer's call — so it is gone from the registry and from the tree; a stored `route` layer is CONVERTED rather than dropped back to the plain badge (`mapFromRoute` + the v20 migration: the trip's located places become the stops, every option that means the same thing comes across, the pictures start off and the line straight, which is what the Route drew). Keep these three, which outlived it: (1) it marked the LEG a day belongs to and **never a point** — a place has no dates and the badge's caption names the leg, so a "you are here" pin would be a claim the document cannot back; that refusal is exactly what makes the Itinerary's pin legitimate, because there the stop is the author's own assertion. (2) It needed no line changed in `HookVariant`, the resolver, the picker, the renderer or either export — only one optional `HookContext.stages` that `needs.places` had announced, which is the engine's contract test passing. (3) Its projection, its label placer and its distance format live in `geo.ts` — lifted out the day the DRIVE reached for them, not the Itinerary — and `currentLegIndex` moved to `hook-calendar.ts`, which is where Virée reads it. Retiring a variant costs nothing when its shared parts were lifted out the day a sibling wanted them; what would have cost is deleting the file Virée still imported.

**What the route's options taught (2026-09-14), which outlived the variant.** Three things a future agent should know that the code does not say: (1) what two variants both want lives beside the contract, not in either — `hooks/easing.ts`, `tick-kits.ts`, `panel-ui.tsx` and `colour.ts` were lifted out of Défilé the day the route reached for them, and Défilé re-exports the old names so its stored options and tests never moved; a variant importing its sibling's plan would be the "tool reaches into another tool" fault at a smaller scale. (2) The ticks are timed on lengths measured in the projection's OWN units (`planarLengths`), not on pixels — the projection is one uniform scale, so the ratios are frame-free and the score written at export lands where the preview's dots appear. (3) Every text the route draws is the place's own name, a distance the pen has actually covered, or the letter N — nothing derived, nothing guessed; the distance is great-circle and says "so far" in the panel because a road distance is a claim the document cannot back. All three carried straight into the Itinerary, which is what made it a file plus a registry line.

**Mobile border fix on a long trip (2026-09-14).** Reported: on a phone, a long trip's heatmap + loupe read as too-round cells, a too-thick black loupe frame, and the frame's border looking cut where it crosses a cell. Cause: the cell's `rounded-[3px]` was fixed regardless of `cellPx` — at the 6px floor a 300+ day trip hits on a phone, 3px reads as a near-circle, not a square (fixed: `cellRadius = clamp(1, floor(cellPx/4), 3)` in `DayHeatmap.tsx`, applied via inline `borderRadius` since a class can't take a computed value); the frame kept a flat `border-2 rounded-[7px]` meant for a roomier grid, disproportionate at that same floor (thinned to `border` + `rounded-[5px]` in `LoupeBrush.tsx`). The frame also had no explicit `z-index` — a cell's `hover:scale-125` (`transform`) promotes it into the browser's "positioned descendants" paint step even though it stays `position: static`, which can put it ahead of an absolutely-positioned sibling with `z-index: auto` in that step's DOM-order comparison; `LoupeBrush`'s root now carries `z-10` so the frame's border wins regardless. **How to apply**: any overlay drawn absolutely over a grid of hoverable/transformed cells needs its own explicit `z-index`, not just `position: absolute` — `z-index: auto` is not safe near a transform.

**Défilé's sweep is authored (2026-09-13): easing, a hold, and the days by name.** Rules that came out of building it: (1) **every easing offered must have a closed-form inverse** — stops are placed on the inverse and the head glides on the curve, so a numeric inverse would land a stop NEAR its time and the numeral, the flash and the tick would each read a different frame; `EASINGS` in `scrub-plan.ts` is the whole table and a round-trip test guards it (it caught a wrong `ease-in-out` inverse first time). (3) The author's picture choice is DATA the variant hands the shell (`wantsPictures`, refs under keys since 2026-09-14 — see «Défilé flashes SOURCE pictures»), never a fetch. **Trap, measured**: a tile that switches its wrapper element (`<span>` reporting vs `<button>` toggling) REMOUNTS its canvas, and a paint effect keyed on the unchanged picture never repaints the new node — every tile came back transparent on a pixel readback. A canvas painted from React uses a **callback ref**, which runs on every attach.

**Défilé's ticks are tuned in the score (2026-09-13): kit, pitch, drift.** Same rule as the volume — every tuning is an event property (`voice`, `rate`) written by `scrubScore`, never a node downstream, so live playback, a still's bed, a silent clip's track and a mix follow one set of numbers. `SCRUB_KITS` keeps two invariants across every kit: a leg's landing is the DIFFERENT one (a distinct voice on the ratchet, the same voice lower and a little louder elsewhere), and the seat is the seat everywhere — it ends the phrase rather than being a step in it, which is also why a drift moves the landings and not the seat. Pitch is read out in semitones (`12·log2`), the unit an ear thinks in. **How to measure a pitch change without ears**: zero-crossing rate over a tick's first 30ms; coarse on band-passed noise (an octave reads as ≈×1.76), but direction and order are unambiguous.

**The paint order sorts FACES, never parts (2026-09-16).** The maintainer reported pieces of the car going missing as he turned it. The cause was `renderOrder` ordering PARTS by the depth of `part.centre`: comparing two convex solids by one scalar is only sound when neither one's span along the view contains the other's, and this model breaks that everywhere — a decal lies ON the face carrying it, the cabin sits ON a body whose top face is one polygon spanning the whole car. Measured over a sweep, the old rule buried the solar panel and its frame to **0%** of themselves, the cabin to 2% and the flares to 5%; the wrap-around corner lights vanished for a ~20° arc of headings at **every** elevation, the cabin for up to 95° of them below 46°. What the code does not say: (1) **The key is the face's FARTHEST vertex, and the reasons are specific.** A face that spans the car then sorts by the end genuinely behind everything, so it is laid down first and cannot cover what stands in front of it; and it is exact for a decal, because depth is linear over a plane, so the maximum over a polygon contained in another cannot exceed the container's — which is why a decal must stay INSIDE the face it is pushed off. The average key and the nearest-vertex key were both measured and are both worse (nearest catastrophically); a `0.85·max + 0.15·mean` blend trades a better whole-car average for a worse floor on the flares, so it was declined — the floor is what the complaint was about. Do not "simplify" any of this back. (2) **Convexity is load-bearing**, not a style note: it is what lets one global sort be right, because after culling no two visible faces of a convex part overlap. (3) **What is left is the model's, not the order's.** ~6.6% of a view still shows a surface that is not the nearest, almost all of it the wheels sunk into a body with no arches cut in it, plus that same full-length top face landing before everything. Splitting the body into real panels is the fix and the maintainer declined it (renderer only, 2026-09-16) — so if the wheels ever bother him, that is the next step, not a smarter sort. (4) **A painter's algorithm cannot order interpenetrating solids at all**, only least-badly; never widen the test's epsilon to hide one, separate the surfaces in the model instead.
