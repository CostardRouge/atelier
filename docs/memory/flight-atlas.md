# Flight Atlas (experimental)

Read before touching `src/tools/atlas/` — the instrument that draws every selected drone flight on one map and animates them together.

## Why it exists and what it is not (2026-10-12)

**Decision.** The maintainer asked for a NEW, EXPERIMENTAL usage («peut-être pas quelque chose qu'on va garder»): load all his drone trajectories — telemetry only, never the videos — and draw them on a map as elegantly as the Perth bus animation he showed (dark ground, violet network of every route, cyan comets with white heads, a big clock and «413 buses running» in a corner, the activity curve under it), with many original sliders for colours, places, map visibility, speed, per-flight colours, pulses, and STAGGERING (all at once, one by one, slightly offset). He left it to the agent overnight («carte libre, sois autonome»). **How to apply**: it is an `instrument` in the registry (`/atlas`); since 2026-10-17 it keeps ATLASES (below), and what leaves is an MP4/PNG. Keep it deletable: nothing outside `src/tools/atlas/` depends on it, it touches `shared/` only by importing.

## The shape (2026-10-12)

- **Pure modules, tested**: `atlas-flight.ts` (cues → typed-array track in Web Mercator WORLD units, ≥ 0.25 s between kept fixes, a jump > 120 m/s dropped, TIME from the cue's wall-clock stamp so a conformed clip runs at its real pace, `startWall` a UTC calendar READ AS LOCAL time — read with UTC accessors everywhere), `atlas-schedule.ts` (the choreography), `atlas-view.ts` (camera), `atlas-places.ts` (single-linkage places at 4 km + Douglas–Peucker), `atlas-settings.ts` (dials, presets, sanitising), `atlas-colour.ts`, `atlas-hud.ts`.
- **One painter** (`atlas-paint.ts`) for the preview and the export, sizes given for a 1080 short side × `unit`. Points are projected BY HAND through one affine (never a canvas transform: line widths stay pixels, doubles never pass a float32 matrix), into reused buffers; lines are thinned per zoom level by `LodCache` (a third of a pixel). Measured: a swarm of 37 flights at dpr 2 holds 60 fps headless.
- **The view is normalised by the frame's short side**, so the same view is the same picture in a 700 px preview and a 4K export. Trap met on the first run: `fitView` floored the room at «1 pixel», which in short-side units is the whole frame — every fit was 2.4× too tight. Any floor in that module must be relative to the short side.
- **Choreography in FLIGHT seconds, then scaled to the asked length**, so a dial keeps its meaning at any length. Five modes: together, cascade (spacing = stagger × mean slot), relay (overlap), clock (seconds of day, a flight slowed `dayStretch`× against the clock, night croppable), calendar (idle between flights capped, the clock RACES through the cut via piecewise-linear `ClockMap` anchors). `equalize` pulls each slot towards the mean; jitter and the shuffle are SEEDED — preview = export.
- **The follow camera is a function of time**: a track simulated once at 1/30 s (exponential ease in centre and log-scale, out faster than in, the zoom held back when the next box is more than 0.6 frames away so a jump between places reads as travel) and interpolated. Never an accumulator in the rAF loop — an export at another fps must draw the same frames.
- **The stage** paints from a `PlayClock` store outside React (the transport subscribes; the 200-control inspector never re-renders while playing), letterboxed to the export's aspect (WYSIWYG), wheel/pinch/drag through `useZoomGestures`, any of which turns the camera to `free`.
- **Tiles** (`atlas-tiles.ts`): the canvas IS Web Mercator, so a raw tile is drawn with one affine `drawImage`, TONED ONCE on landing on its own opaque 256 px canvas (blend modes on a frame with holes would tone the bare ground too), dimmed by one full-frame fill of the ground colour (a no-op where there is no tile), an ancestor standing in while loading; the export awaits `ready()` per frame. The coarse 1:50m coastline is NOT drawn over tiles (it is a wrong second coast at drone scale).

## Round two: overlay, framing, mount (2026-10-12)

His second ask, the same night: statistics in the overlay, title/subtitle «style look like in studio and trip», Format and Export tabs (resolution, ratio, borders), a way to CROP the atlas (whole world, the concerned zone, a chosen zone), a camera that dives on a drone from time to time (random or picked, optional), the drone's brand and model, a date range. **Decisions**:

- **The overlay is drawn by the shared text engine** (`drawOverlays` + `StylePanel`, `atlas-overlay.ts`), never by hand: each piece becomes ordinary `text` elements under `hud.theme`, a label pinning `uppercase`/`letterSpacing`/`weight`, an accent pinning `color`. The activity curve is the one piece the engine has no element for; it is drawn beside them. Pieces live in six SPOTS and stack in one fixed order; a statistic's numeral is the engine's ODOMETER, but only when LIVE — a total held still would freeze mid-roll — and its text is CUT, never rounded (`atlas-stats.ts`), or 41.7 rounded to «42» would roll past itself.
- **Statistics are measured** from running totals kept per fix (`cumKm`, `cumAltS` — the time integral of height, so the average height is time-weighted —, `cumMaxAlt`, `cumMaxSpeed`); nothing estimated.
- **The aircraft comes from the clip's MP4 metadata, never the log** (a DJI `.srt` names none): `read-aircraft.ts` walks box HEADERS (16-byte reads) to `moov`, reads only its `udta`/`meta`; `atlas-aircraft.ts` parses QuickTime `©mdl`/`©mak`/`©enc`/`©too`, an `ilst`, Apple `keys`+`ilst`, then a camera code or a `DJI…` word. A short code table (FC8482 Mini 4 Pro — his body —, FC3582, FC7303, FC3411, FC3170, FC220, FC330); an unknown code stays the code. Untested on a real DJI MP4: which tag his drone writes is unknown.
- **The mount**: `mapRect(format, W, H)` is the map's rectangle; every camera fits to `mapUnitFrame` (the MAP's shape, not the frame's), the painter offsets its affine by the rect's corner and clips to it — no second canvas. Gestures and the export's tile wait measure inside the mount too; forgetting that offset is the bug to expect.
- **Framing** adds `world` (`WORLD_BOX`, Mercator's useful band) and `zone` (`camera.zone`, a world box drawn on the stage — the four screen corners unprojected — or the view's `visibleBox`).
- **Spotlight** (`atlas-spotlight.ts`): episodes laid out once (every N s, the next flight in the air with ≥ 60 % of min(hold, its slot) left — the first cut required a whole slot and found nothing in a tight cascade), seeded; the camera track now aims at VIEWS (`buildCameraTrack(targetAt: t → view)`), the spotlight's view centred on the head at `closeness` km. **Trap**: an exponential approach on the centre throttled the zoom (held back until the aim is near) to ~1.4 levels a second — a 12-level dive took 8 s; while travelling the centre now moves ~3× faster. On a place or zone frame only the flights it shows are followed (random picks otherwise jumped continents). Short slots (a dense relay) give short dives — inherent, said in the hint.

## A frame's cost is the LAND, kept as a raster under a still camera (2026-10-14)

His «fluidité des réglages». Measured headless on a production build (300 synthetic flights, SwiftShader): the main thread sits IDLE through a slider drag — React, the schedule and even the follow camera's track cost a few percent — and the frame rate is the canvas's own raster. Turning effects off one by one (glow, network, afterglow, additive, halo, vignette) changed nothing; the shipped COAST did: thousands of vertices filled through a transform every frame, half a frame's cost (8 → 17 frames in 2 s without it). **Decision**: `paintLand` keeps the land as a raster of the map's rectangle per TARGET canvas (a `WeakMap` on `ctx.canvas`, so the stage and an export never trade), keyed on the affine, the size, the land's mode, colour and data — and only once the same key is asked twice running, so a moving camera (follow, drift, dive) draws directly and pays no copy. Measured: playing 6–7 → 18 frames in 2 s; a Width drag's bar moves 3 → 10 playing, 5 → 19 paused; the follow camera unchanged; the stage's pixels match the old build to a mean of 0.009 of a code (max 2). **How to apply**: before optimising the painter, toggle features and COUNT frames — the obvious suspects (additive strokes, gradients) were not it. What is left after the land: the overlay's text (its shadow legibility) is the next piece, ~25 % of a frame.

## Named presets: how it draws, never what it draws (2026-10-14)

His pick from the open list («Préréglages nommés»). **Decision**: *Presets* (at the inspector's foot since 2026-10-15; `AtlasPresetsSection.tsx` over `atlas-presets.ts`, pure, and `use-atlas-presets.ts` — one book in the atlas store's `kv` since 2026-10-17, moved once from `localStorage['atelier.atlas.presets']` —, read back through `sanitizeSettings` like the dials): chips that apply and stay LIT while the dials match (`isCurrent`), one field that saves — or *Replace*s a preset of the same name, compared without case —, × to remove. A preset leaves out the CONTENT of one set of flights — `hud.title`/`subtitle`, `camera.place`/`zone`/`picked`, `aircraft` — and applying one keeps the screen's (the Trips rule for openers' `contentKeys`, `roadtrip.md`). **Why**: a preset made over Perth must dress Guadeloupe's flights without Perth's words or box. **How to apply**: a new dial that names one dataset joins `withContentOf`, or a preset will carry it from one set to another. A preset is the device's, one book for every atlas, never synced and never on an atlas; the book of Develop presets (`develop-roll.md`) is a different thing.

## The drone is a GEAR STICKER, and a maker's logo is the person's own (2026-10-15)

His ask: the drone's official DJI look, «une sorte de logo outline», shown in a second lab (https://claude.ai/artifact/Mbfsump5ZDmvKpBNNiXbRW) after his screenshots of DJI's own «Shot on» cards. He read two styles in them and **picked**: Montserrat, *Bold + light* by default, no backing; the recent models' camera codes «on s'en fout» — the person names what is not detected. **Decision**: the overlay's Drone piece IS the sticker (`atlas-sticker.ts`, pure: `stickerParts` splits a display name into maker + words, `autoLightFrom` sets the trailing `QUALIFIERS` light, a name opening on none of `KNOWN_MAKERS` gets `hud.sticker.maker` in front; `stickerCase` keeps «iPhone» and «α7C»). No «Shot on» (the atlas shows no video). Ink `auto` = white or near-black by WCAG contrast against what is under the text (`stickerInk` in `atlas-paint.ts`: the mount when the text is across the frame, else the ground, the tiles dimmed by it). Per-name weights live in `aircraft.weights` (content, so presets leave them). The sticker is drawn by hand in `atlas-overlay.ts` like the curve — the text engine has no element for two weights side by side behind an image — and its measure is wrapped in `save/restore`, because `letterSpacing` left on the context spaced every text the engine drew after it.

**The logo is never shipped** (a trademark, in a public repo served by Pages): `atlas-logos.ts` keeps one per maker in IndexedDB `atelier-atlas-logos`, imported from the person's files, never in the settings, a preset or the repo; without one the logo style writes the maker heavy. A canvas draws an SVG through its intrinsic size, so `drawableSvg` writes width/height from a viewBox-only file (Chrome otherwise letterboxes it into 300 × 150); tinting is `source-in` on a cached per-size canvas; a file with no transparency is said to tint as a block. **2026-10-16, his report** («pas gérées en transparence … gérer les SVG»): a raster logo saved on a plain ground — or on the grey CHECKERBOARD image search sites bake into a «transparent» PNG — is KEYED at decode (`atlas-logo-key.ts`, pure: the ground read off the picture's border, one colour or two neutral ones holding 92 % of it, every near pixel cleared across the picture with a ramp, a file that already has alpha left alone), the author's «keep the background» stored on the record (`keepGround`); an SVG gets the `xmlns` an `<img>` demands (one copied out of a page has none) and, with no size and no viewBox, a viewBox measured by `getBBox` in a hidden holder. **How to apply**: never add a maker's artwork to `public/` — offer the import; a picture the person brings is made drawable here, never refused for a fixable reason.

## The inspector is LAYERS, picked on the picture (2026-10-15)

His pick C of the inspector lab (https://claude.ai/artifact/3PnBCnbrr3jdkju6Hmu3Q3 — today measured at 123 rows in 23 sections over 8 tabs, Overlay alone 2.2 screens; A five tabs, B numbered chapters, C click what you see). **Decision**: no tabs. ## The film: an intro, the flights, an outro (2026-10-15)

His picks from the lab (https://claude.ai/artifact/YbmRQdhJH3mufSmfc3kF1t): a title card by default (map reveal and world dive beside it), a summary to close (pull back, fade), both in presets without their words. **Decision**: `atlas-story.ts` (pure) puts three spans on ONE clock — the flights' own length untouched, the intro added before and the outro after (`storyOf`) — and every time the stage, the export and `scene.viewAt` are handed is STORY time; `paintAtlas` converts it once (`flightTime`) and everything about the flights runs on their own. How visible each piece is at an instant is a pure function (`introLook`, `outroLook`, ported from the lab's player, its fixed beats scaled down for an intro under 4 s / an outro under 4.5 s); `atlas-story-paint.ts` paints from those numbers only. The camera of a dive and a pull-back lives in `viewAt`, never in the painter. **Trap met**: a dive that moves the centre linearly in the world (or weighted by the linear scale) leaves the place far off screen halfway down, where the frame is already small — `diveView` shrinks the place's SCREEN offset in step with the log zoom instead, so it stays in frame and lands in the middle. **How to apply**: anything new that keeps time reads story time and converts with `flightTime`; a whole-overlay fade goes through `DrawOptions.alpha` (the engine assigns each element's alpha itself, so a wrapping `globalAlpha` is stomped) and a per-block fade through `Block.alpha`; a block fainter than 0.3 is not clickable; a held total never carries an odometer (it would freeze mid-roll — «0 min» for 599.99 s). The title falls back from the field to the overlay's title to the biggest place's name; the second line to «N flights · dates». **2026-10-16, his report**: a wheel during a world dive zoomed for a frame and sprang back — the hand wrote a free view that `viewAt` ignores while the story moves the camera. The stage now asks `cameraHeldBy` (a dive's intro, a pull-back's outro) and REFUSES the gesture, saying why in a chip; a new story camera must be added there or the bounce returns.

## Pieces are DRAGGED on the stage, the six spots kept (2026-10-15)

His ask after the layers: drag the overlay's pieces like in Develop / the Studio / Trips, keeping the default placements. **Decision** (`atlas-place.ts`, pure): six PIECES (title, dates, stats, clock stack, spotlight card, sticker), each in its spot by default; a press on one picks it up (its layer opens), the piece follows the hand and goes back to a spot when its anchor comes within 6 % of the short side of that spot's (the left/middle/right edge, the top or the bottom it pins), else it is held where it is let go as `hud.free[piece]` — the edge it grows from (chosen by the third of the area it is in, so a longer title never runs off the near edge) and its top, as fractions of the overlay's area, kept inside. Picking a spot in its *Where* row forgets the hand placement. The drag paints a preview through `withPlacement` (the same write the drop commits) and writes ONCE on release; the map pans when the press is not on a piece. **How to apply**: a new overlay piece is `put(piece, …)` in `overlayLayout` with an entry in `PIECE_SPOT` / `PIECE_LAYER`, or it cannot be dragged; the painter lays spots and hand placements as one list of stacks; a hand placement is layout, so presets keep it. The intro's and outro's words are centred and not draggable.

## The story's words are kept inside the frame (2026-10-16)

His report: a long intro title ran off both edges. **Decision**: a `TextLine` may carry `fit: { maxW, mode }` and `paintOverlay` EXPANDS it into one block per line before laying the stack (`breakLines` / `fitSize`, pure, `atlas-text.ts`): `wrap` breaks between words at 84 % of the area's width (`STORY_TEXT_WIDTH`), the author's own `\n` kept, a single word wider than that shrunk rather than cut; `shrink` puts it on one line and lowers its size until it fits. Any text holding a `\n` is split even without a fit, since `drawOverlays` draws one line per element. The intro's `titleSize` also sizes the outro's title (the title is one thing). **How to apply**: a new long text in the overlay gets a `fit` rather than its own wrapping; a size setting multiplies the block's `size`, never the theme's scale.

## The outro's totals are chosen, sized and laid out (2026-10-16)

His ask: pick which totals the summary counts, their size, and a grid rather than one row. **Decision**: `OutroSettings.stats` (the eight `STAT_KEYS`, its own choice apart from the Statistics layer's — `AtlasStatChips` draws both), `statsSize`, `statsLayout` `row | grid` and `columns` 2–4. A `StatRow` with a `fit` is expanded by `paintOverlay` like a text: a grid gives every cell the widest one's width and keeps a short last row on the columns above (`cell`, `slots`); a row breaks where the next item would pass 84 %; both shrink when one line still would. The old `totals: false` reads as none ticked. **How to apply**: a statistics block that must stay in the frame gets a `fit`, never its own layout code.

## A world dive starts on a world that COVERS the frame (2026-10-16)

His report: black bands while the dive zoomed. The start was `fitView(WORLD_BOX)` — the world CONTAINED, narrower than a 16:9 frame and far shorter than a 9:16 one. **Decision**: `diveStart` (pure, `atlas-story.ts`) starts on the scale that covers the frame with the world (sides cut in a tall frame), centred on the target and clamped inside the world's edges, or — `intro.diveFrom` — on a continent / country / region (the short side spanning 5000 / 1500 / 400 km above the target), never closer than the target and never under the cover. **How to apply**: a camera that shows the whole world as a backdrop covers the frame; contain only where the edges are meant to be seen.

## The social apps' zones can be kept clear (2026-10-16)

His ask: keep the words out of where Reels / TikTok / Shorts draw their interface, Ignore or Apply. **Decision**: `format.safe` `ignore | clear` (default ignore: nothing moves); `atlas-safe.ts` holds the zones per ratio as fractions of the FRAME (9:16 the three apps put together and rounded from their guides, NOT measured on a phone; the others a feed's crop or a player's bar). The painter hands `safe` (frame px) to every layout; `spotLines` is the one answer to where a spot pins (margin, then the zones) for the painter AND the drop's snap; `placeBounds` stops a hand placement at the zones' edge; a centred stack centres between the lines and a `fit` width never exceeds them. The stage hatches the zones (an SVG over the canvas) only while the Export layer is open — never painted, so never in a file. **How to apply**: a new overlay position reads `spotLines`, never `SPOT_MARGIN` by itself.

## Drone names are edited where they show (2026-10-16)

## The credits are a piece of their own (2026-10-16)

His ask: place the mandatory credits (data line, © OpenStreetMap) apart from the clock stack. **Decision**: a seventh piece, `credits`, owned by the Clock layer; `hud.creditsAt` is `'clock'` (the default, where every saved atlas keeps them: they ride the clock's stack — its spot or its hand placement) or a spot; dragged, they are held as `hud.free.credits`. ## Every overlay piece has its own size (2026-10-16)

His audit ask («liste de tous ces éléments où il manquerait un paramètre size»), then «toute la liste, un réglage par élément». **Decision**: `hud.sizes` (dates, stats, clock, counter, curve, card, credits — 0.4–3, default 1) multiplies each block's base size in `overlayLayout`, on top of `hud.scale` (the overall Text size) — the title keeps `titleSize`, the sticker its px size; a curve's `scale` widens it and its end labels too. ## Atlases: a gallery, everything on the device (2026-10-17)

His ask («une page authentique de galerie comme Studio, Trips et Develop … renommer, cloner, supprimer, dupliquer»), approved on the lab https://claude.ai/artifact/WLcXUFFwTnbDKdetjux9JF, then amended: «les utilisateurs du Flight Atlas sont plutôt sur du local … ceux qui sont dépourvus d'une instance Winnow … doivent pouvoir tout avoir et récupérer à partir de la galerie»; Winnow is comfort and its limits are accepted. **Decision**: an ATLAS is a document (`atlas-doc.ts`: name, source, EVERY dial content included, flights BY REFERENCE, hidden ids) in its own IndexedDB database `atelier-atlas` (`atlas-store.ts`: `atlases`, `logs`, `thumbs`, `sync`, `kv`). A flight is the SHA-256 of its log's TEXT; the text is kept ONCE in `logs` and shared, pruned with the last atlas drawing it (spared for 10 min after it was taken, the open atlas's debounce). So an atlas reopens with no Library and no instance; the Library's selection only FEEDS an atlas now (a button, and *New atlas* with the ticked ones). The aircraft a clip names is read at ADD time and kept on the flight. The poster is the card's cover, painted at 4:3 by the export's painter after 2.5 s still (`use-atlas-cover.ts`), never in a file. Presets moved to `kv`; logos keep their own database. Routes `/atlas/home` · `/atlas/<ref>` (`atlas-route.ts`); shell `AtlasTool.tsx` (save debounce, `useDocumentLock` kind `atlas`, `useDocumentSync`), editor `AtlasEditor.tsx`, gallery `AtlasGallery.tsx` over `useDocumentGallery`.
